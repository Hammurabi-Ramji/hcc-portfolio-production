### 1.0 Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the process for the comprehensive reporting and management of adverse events associated with animal pharmaceuticals. The purpose of this SOP is to ensure compliance with all applicable regulatory requirements, including those set forth by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), and current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP). This document provides detailed instructions for the identification, collection, assessment, reporting, and follow-up of adverse events to ensure the safety and efficacy of animal pharmaceutical products and to protect animal and public health.

This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the manufacturing, testing, distribution, and post-market surveillance of animal pharmaceutical products within [Company Name]. This includes, but is not limited to, manufacturing personnel, quality control, quality assurance, regulatory affairs, research and development, and sales and marketing teams. The scope encompasses all adverse events, product defects, and unexpected findings related to the use of animal pharmaceuticals, from initial observation through to regulatory submission and final resolution. This SOP is specifically tailored for large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations, addressing the unique considerations and complexities associated with such scale and scope.

Adherence to this SOP is critical for maintaining regulatory compliance, ensuring product quality, and safeguarding the well-being of animals and consumers. It serves as a foundational document for training and continuous improvement in adverse event reporting processes.




### 2.0 Responsibilities

**2.1 All Personnel:**

*   All employees are responsible for promptly reporting any observed or suspected adverse events, product quality defects, or other product-related problems involving animal pharmaceuticals to their immediate supervisor or the designated Adverse Event Coordinator.
*   Familiarize themselves with the content of this SOP and adhere to its procedures.

**2.2 Adverse Event Coordinator (AEC) / Pharmacovigilance Department:**

*   Serves as the primary point of contact for all adverse event reports.
*   Ensures timely and accurate collection of all relevant information pertaining to adverse events.
*   Conducts initial assessment and triage of adverse event reports.
*   Maintains a comprehensive database of all reported adverse events.
*   Prepares and submits adverse event reports to relevant regulatory authorities (e.g., FDA, USDA) within specified timelines.
*   Coordinates with other departments (e.g., Quality Assurance, Manufacturing, Research & Development) during investigations.
*   Ensures compliance with all regulatory reporting requirements.
*   Provides training and guidance to personnel on adverse event reporting procedures.
*   Manages follow-up communications with reporters and regulatory agencies.

**2.3 Quality Assurance (QA) Department:**

*   Oversees the overall quality management system, including adverse event reporting processes.
*   Reviews and approves all adverse event reports prior to submission to regulatory authorities.
*   Ensures that all investigations are thorough, well-documented, and compliant with cGMP and other relevant regulations.
*   Participates in root cause analysis and corrective and preventive action (CAPA) initiatives related to adverse events.
*   Maintains records of all adverse event reports and associated documentation.

**2.4 Manufacturing Department:**

*   Provides information related to manufacturing processes, batch records, and product specifications as required for adverse event investigations.
*   Implements corrective actions identified during investigations to prevent recurrence of product-related issues.

**2.5 Research & Development (R&D) Department:**

*   Provides technical expertise and product knowledge to assist in the assessment of adverse events.
*   Participates in investigations, particularly for events related to product formulation or intended use.

**2.6 Regulatory Affairs Department:**

*   Ensures that all adverse event reporting activities comply with current national and international regulations.
*   Liaises with regulatory authorities regarding adverse event submissions and inquiries.
*   Stays updated on changes in adverse event reporting regulations and communicates them to relevant departments.

**2.7 Management:**

*   Provides necessary resources and support for the effective implementation and maintenance of the adverse event reporting system.
*   Reviews and approves this SOP and any subsequent revisions.
*   Ensures that personnel are adequately trained and competent in their responsibilities related to adverse event reporting.




### 3.0 Procedures

This section details the step-by-step procedures for handling adverse events related to animal pharmaceuticals, from initial receipt of information to final closure and reporting.

#### 3.1 Initial Receipt of Adverse Event Information

**3.1.1 Identification of an Adverse Event:**

Any individual, whether an employee, veterinarian, animal owner, or other stakeholder, who observes or suspects an adverse event related to an animal pharmaceutical product manufactured by [Company Name] is responsible for reporting it. An adverse event is defined as any untoward medical occurrence in an animal administered a pharmaceutical product, whether or not considered to be product-related. This includes, but is not limited to, adverse drug reactions, product quality defects, lack of expected efficacy, and environmental contamination.

**3.1.2 Channels for Reporting:**

Adverse events can be reported through various channels, including:

*   **Internal Reporting:** Employees should report to their immediate supervisor or directly to the Adverse Event Coordinator (AEC) / Pharmacovigilance Department via designated internal reporting forms or systems.
*   **External Reporting:** Veterinarians, animal owners, or other external parties can report adverse events via the company's dedicated pharmacovigilance hotline, email address, website portal, or through direct contact with sales representatives. All external reports must be immediately forwarded to the AEC.

**3.1.3 Initial Information Collection:**

Upon receipt of an adverse event report, the AEC or designated personnel must gather as much initial information as possible. This includes, but is not limited to:

*   **Reporter Information:** Name, contact details, and relationship to the animal/product.
*   **Animal Information:** Species, breed, age, sex, weight, and any relevant medical history or concurrent conditions.
*   **Product Information:** Full product name, lot number, expiration date, dosage, route of administration, and indication for use. If possible, obtain the remaining product or packaging for investigation.
*   **Adverse Event Description:** Detailed description of the signs and symptoms observed, onset date and time, duration, severity, outcome (e.g., recovery, death, euthanasia), and any treatment administered.
*   **Concomitant Medications/Treatments:** Any other drugs or treatments the animal was receiving at the time of the adverse event.
*   **Relevant History:** Any other relevant information that may contribute to understanding the event.

**3.1.4 Documentation of Initial Report:**

All initial adverse event reports, regardless of the reporting channel, must be immediately documented on the official Adverse Event Report Form (AERF) or within the electronic pharmacovigilance system. The date and time of receipt must be recorded. Incomplete reports should still be documented, and efforts made to obtain missing information.

#### 3.2 Investigation and Data Collection

**3.2.1 Triage and Prioritization:**

Upon receipt and initial documentation, the AEC will triage the adverse event report to determine its seriousness and potential impact. Serious adverse events (e.g., those resulting in death, life-threatening situations, permanent disability, or requiring hospitalization) must be prioritized for immediate investigation and reporting.

**3.2.2 Detailed Data Collection and Follow-up:**

The AEC is responsible for conducting a thorough investigation to collect all necessary data. This may involve:

*   **Contacting the Reporter:** Follow-up communication with the reporter to clarify details, obtain additional information, or request medical records.
*   **Veterinary Records Review:** Obtaining and reviewing veterinary medical records related to the animal and the adverse event.
*   **Product Sample Analysis:** If a product quality defect is suspected, coordinating with the Quality Control (QC) department to retrieve and analyze retained product samples from the affected lot.
*   **Batch Record Review:** Reviewing manufacturing batch records to identify any deviations or anomalies during production that could be linked to the adverse event.
*   **Literature Review:** Conducting a review of scientific literature and product labeling to assess known adverse reactions or product characteristics.
*   **Expert Consultation:** Consulting with internal or external subject matter experts (e.g., veterinarians, toxicologists, pharmacologists) as needed.

**3.2.3 Causality Assessment:**

The AEC, in consultation with veterinary professionals and other relevant experts, will perform a causality assessment to determine the likelihood of a causal relationship between the administration of the animal pharmaceutical product and the observed adverse event. This assessment will consider factors such as the temporal relationship, biological plausibility, dechallenge/rechallenge information, and the presence of alternative causes. The causality assessment should be clearly documented.

#### 3.3 Assessment and Classification

**3.3.1 Seriousness and Expectedness Assessment:**

Each adverse event must be assessed for seriousness (as defined in 3.2.1) and expectedness. Expectedness refers to whether the adverse event is consistent with the known adverse reactions listed in the product's approved labeling. Unexpected serious adverse events require expedited reporting to regulatory authorities.

**3.3.2 Coding of Adverse Events:**

Adverse events will be coded using a standardized veterinary medical terminology system (e.g., VeDDRA - Veterinary Dictionary for Drug Related Affairs) to ensure consistency and facilitate data analysis and regulatory reporting.

**3.3.3 Case Narrative Development:**

A concise and accurate case narrative summarizing all relevant information about the adverse event, including the product, animal, event description, causality assessment, and outcome, will be developed for each case. This narrative will form a key part of the regulatory submission.

#### 3.4 Reporting to Regulatory Authorities

**3.4.1 Regulatory Reporting Requirements and Timelines:**

[Company Name] is committed to complying with all regulatory reporting requirements for adverse events. Key regulations include:

*   **FDA (U.S. Food and Drug Administration):** For approved new animal drugs, adverse events are reported to the FDA's Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM) as per 21 CFR Part 514. This includes expedited reporting for serious and unexpected adverse experiences (e.g., 15-day Alert Reports) and periodic reports for all other adverse events.
*   **USDA (U.S. Department of Agriculture):** For veterinary biologics (e.g., vaccines, antitoxins, diagnostic kits), adverse events are reported to the USDA's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS), Center for Veterinary Biologics (CVB). Reporting requirements are outlined in 9 CFR Part 108.
*   **cGMP (current Good Manufacturing Practices):** While cGMP primarily focuses on manufacturing quality, product quality defects that lead to adverse events must be investigated and reported in accordance with cGMP principles, ensuring that manufacturing processes are controlled and products are fit for their intended use.

**3.4.2 Submission Process:**

The AEC is responsible for preparing and submitting adverse event reports to the appropriate regulatory authorities within the prescribed timelines. This typically involves:

*   **Electronic Submission:** Utilizing electronic submission gateways (e.g., FDA's CVM Electronic Submissions Gateway, USDA's PV Express II) for efficient and secure transmission of reports.
*   **Form Completion:** Accurately completing all required fields on regulatory forms (e.g., FDA Form 1932a for veterinary adverse experience reports).
*   **Attachment of Supporting Documentation:** Including all relevant supporting documentation, such as veterinary records, laboratory results, and case narratives.

**3.4.3 Communication with Regulatory Authorities:**

All communications with regulatory authorities regarding adverse event reports must be handled by the Regulatory Affairs Department in coordination with the AEC. This ensures consistent and accurate information exchange.

#### 3.5 Follow-up and Closure

**3.5.1 Ongoing Monitoring and Follow-up:**

Adverse event cases remain open until all relevant information has been collected, investigations are complete, and all required regulatory reports have been submitted. The AEC will conduct ongoing follow-up with reporters and healthcare professionals to obtain updated information on the animal's condition or product usage.

**3.5.2 Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA):**

If an adverse event investigation identifies a product quality defect, manufacturing deviation, or other systemic issue, a Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) process will be initiated. The QA department will oversee the CAPA process, ensuring that root causes are identified, effective corrective actions are implemented, and preventive measures are put in place to avoid recurrence. All CAPA activities must be thoroughly documented.

**3.5.3 Case Closure:**

Once all investigations are complete, all necessary information has been gathered, all regulatory reporting obligations have been met, and any associated CAPAs have been addressed or initiated, the adverse event case can be formally closed. The closure must be documented in the pharmacovigilance system.

**3.5.4 Trend Analysis and Signal Detection:**

Periodically, the AEC and QA department will conduct trend analysis of adverse event data to identify potential safety signals, emerging patterns, or product-related issues. This proactive approach helps in early detection of potential problems and informs risk management strategies. Findings from trend analysis will be reported to management and used for continuous improvement of product safety and quality.




### 4.0 Documentation Requirements

Accurate, complete, and timely documentation is paramount for effective adverse event reporting and regulatory compliance. All documentation must adhere to cGMP principles, ensuring data integrity, traceability, and retrievability.

**4.1 Adverse Event Report Form (AERF):**

*   All initial adverse event reports, regardless of source, must be documented on the official AERF (or within the electronic pharmacovigilance system). This form must capture all essential information as outlined in Section 3.1.3.
*   Each AERF must have a unique identification number.
*   All entries must be legible, accurate, and made in real-time. Any corrections must be initialed and dated, with the original entry remaining visible.

**4.2 Investigation Records:**

*   Detailed records of all investigation activities, including follow-up communications, interviews, veterinary records obtained, product sample analysis results, and batch record reviews, must be maintained.
*   These records should clearly demonstrate the steps taken during the investigation and the rationale for any decisions made.

**4.3 Causality Assessment Documentation:**

*   The rationale and conclusion of the causality assessment (Section 3.2.3) must be clearly documented for each adverse event case.

**4.4 Regulatory Submission Documents:**

*   Copies of all submitted regulatory reports (e.g., FDA Form 1932a, electronic submission confirmations) must be retained.
*   All supporting documentation submitted to regulatory authorities must also be archived.

**4.5 Correspondence:**

*   All correspondence with reporters, veterinarians, regulatory authorities, and internal departments related to adverse events must be documented and filed.

**4.6 Training Records:**

*   Records of all training provided to personnel on this SOP and adverse event reporting procedures must be maintained (refer to Section 5.0).

**4.7 Quality Control Records:**

*   Documentation related to quality control measures, including audits, CAPA records, and trend analysis reports, must be kept.

**4.8 Retention of Records:**

*   All adverse event-related documentation must be retained for a period consistent with regulatory requirements (e.g., 10 years or as specified by FDA/USDA regulations) and company policy. Records must be stored in a secure and easily retrievable manner.




### 5.0 Training Requirements

Effective adverse event reporting relies on well-trained personnel who understand their roles and responsibilities. [Company Name] is committed to providing comprehensive training to all employees involved in the adverse event reporting process.

**5.1 Initial Training:**

*   All new employees whose roles involve potential exposure to or reporting of adverse events (e.g., manufacturing, quality, regulatory, sales, R&D) must receive initial training on this SOP within 30 days of their start date or assignment to a relevant role.
*   Initial training will cover the purpose and scope of the SOP, definitions of adverse events, reporting channels, initial information collection, and their specific responsibilities.

**5.2 Refresher Training:**

*   All relevant personnel must undergo refresher training on this SOP at least annually, or more frequently if significant changes to regulations, procedures, or systems occur.
*   Refresher training will reinforce key concepts, address common challenges, and communicate any updates or lessons learned from previous adverse event cases.

**5.3 Specialized Training:**

*   Personnel with specific responsibilities, such as the Adverse Event Coordinator, Quality Assurance personnel involved in investigations, and Regulatory Affairs personnel responsible for submissions, will receive specialized, in-depth training tailored to their roles.
*   This specialized training will cover advanced topics such as causality assessment, regulatory reporting timelines, electronic submission systems, and CAPA processes.

**5.4 Training Content:**

Training materials will include, but not be limited to:

*   This SOP document.
*   Relevant regulatory guidelines (FDA, USDA).
*   Case studies of actual adverse events.
*   Practical exercises on completing AERFs and using reporting systems.
*   Discussions on ethical considerations and the importance of patient safety.

**5.5 Documentation of Training:**

*   All training sessions must be documented, including the date of training, attendees, training materials used, and a record of successful completion (e.g., signed attendance sheets, quiz results).
*   Training records will be maintained by the Human Resources department and/or the Quality Assurance department and will be readily available for inspection.




### 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures are integral to ensuring the accuracy, completeness, and compliance of the adverse event reporting system. These measures are designed to identify and correct deficiencies, ensuring the integrity of the data and the effectiveness of the process.

**6.1 Internal Audits:**

*   The Quality Assurance (QA) department will conduct periodic internal audits of the adverse event reporting system to assess compliance with this SOP, regulatory requirements, and internal policies.
*   Audits will include a review of adverse event files, documentation, reporting timelines, and the effectiveness of corrective actions.
*   Audit findings will be documented, and any identified non-conformances will be addressed through the CAPA system.

**6.2 Data Review and Verification:**

*   The Adverse Event Coordinator (AEC) and QA personnel will regularly review adverse event data for accuracy, completeness, and consistency.
*   This includes verifying that all required fields are populated, coding is correct, and narratives accurately reflect the reported information.
*   Discrepancies will be investigated and corrected promptly.

**6.3 Performance Metrics and Trend Analysis:**

*   Key performance indicators (KPIs) related to adverse event reporting will be established and regularly monitored. These may include:
    *   Number of adverse events reported per product/batch.
    *   Timeliness of initial reporting.
    *   Timeliness of regulatory submissions.
    *   Number of incomplete reports.
    *   Number of reports requiring follow-up.
*   Trend analysis of adverse event data will be conducted quarterly to identify patterns, potential safety signals, or emerging product issues. This analysis will inform risk management activities and product lifecycle management.

**6.4 Management Review:**

*   The adverse event reporting system, including performance metrics, audit findings, and trend analysis results, will be reviewed by senior management at least annually as part of the overall Quality Management Review process.
*   This review will assess the effectiveness of the system and identify opportunities for continuous improvement.

**6.5 External Inspections and Audits:**

*   [Company Name] will cooperate fully with external regulatory inspections (e.g., FDA, USDA) and third-party audits of its adverse event reporting system.
*   Any findings from external inspections will be addressed promptly and thoroughly through the CAPA system.




### 7.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all aspects of animal pharmaceutical manufacturing and post-market surveillance. This section outlines the safety considerations relevant to adverse event reporting, ensuring the well-being of animals, personnel, and the environment.

**7.1 Animal Welfare:**

*   The primary consideration in adverse event reporting is the welfare of the animal. All efforts should be made to ensure that reported adverse events are promptly investigated and, where appropriate, measures are taken to mitigate harm to the affected animal.
*   Information gathered during adverse event investigations should contribute to a better understanding of product safety profiles, leading to improved product development and responsible use.

**7.2 Personnel Safety:**

*   Personnel involved in handling adverse event reports, particularly those who may come into contact with product samples or animal tissues, must adhere to all relevant safety protocols, including the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) as required.
*   When investigating product quality defects, personnel must be aware of potential hazards associated with the product and take appropriate precautions.

**7.3 Environmental Safety:**

*   In cases where adverse events involve potential environmental contamination (e.g., due to product spills or improper disposal), appropriate environmental safety procedures must be followed in accordance with local, national, and international regulations.
*   The disposal of returned or affected product samples must comply with environmental protection guidelines.

**7.4 Data Security and Confidentiality:**

*   All adverse event data, especially information pertaining to animal owners or veterinarians, must be handled with the utmost confidentiality and in compliance with data privacy regulations.
*   Access to adverse event databases and records must be restricted to authorized personnel only.
*   Robust cybersecurity measures must be in place to protect electronic data from unauthorized access, loss, or alteration.

**7.5 Risk Management:**

*   Information derived from adverse event reports is crucial for ongoing risk management activities. This includes identifying potential risks, assessing their likelihood and severity, and implementing strategies to minimize or eliminate them.
*   The pharmacovigilance system should feed into the overall risk management plan for each product, contributing to a continuous cycle of safety assessment and improvement.




### 8.0 Regulatory Compliance References

This Standard Operating Procedure is developed and maintained in strict adherence to the following key regulatory frameworks and guidelines, ensuring comprehensive compliance for adverse event reporting in animal pharmaceuticals:

**8.1 U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA):**

*   **21 CFR Part 514 – New Animal Drug Applications:** This regulation outlines the requirements for new animal drug applications, including post-marketing safety reporting. It mandates the reporting of adverse drug experiences and product defects for approved new animal drugs. Specific attention is paid to:
    *   **21 CFR 514.80 – Post-marketing reporting of adverse drug experiences:** This section details the requirements for submitting adverse drug experience reports, including the types of reports (e.g., 15-day Alert Reports, periodic reports), content, and timelines. It emphasizes the importance of reporting all adverse events, whether serious or non-serious, expected or unexpected.
    *   **FDA Form 1932a – Veterinary Adverse Experience Report:** This is the primary form used for submitting adverse event reports to the FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM). Compliance with the accurate and complete submission of this form is critical.
*   **Guidance for Industry:** The FDA issues various guidance documents that provide recommendations on how to comply with regulations. Relevant guidance documents related to pharmacovigilance and adverse event reporting for animal drugs are consulted and followed.

**8.2 U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA):**

*   **9 CFR Part 108 – Biological Products; Adverse Event Reporting:** This regulation governs the reporting of adverse events for veterinary biological products (e.g., vaccines, bacterins, diagnostic kits) regulated by the USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS), Center for Veterinary Biologics (CVB). Key aspects include:
    *   Requirements for manufacturers to report adverse events associated with their licensed biological products.
    *   Specific timelines and formats for submitting adverse event reports to the CVB.
*   **USDA APHIS CVB Industry Guidance:** The CVB provides specific guidance documents and electronic reporting systems (e.g., PV Express II) to assist manufacturers in fulfilling their adverse event reporting obligations for veterinary biologics.

**8.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP):**

*   **21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 – Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Processing, Packing, or Holding of Drugs; General and Finished Pharmaceuticals:** While cGMP primarily focuses on the quality systems for manufacturing, processing, packing, and holding of drugs, its principles are fundamental to adverse event reporting. Specifically:
    *   **Quality System:** cGMP mandates a robust quality system that includes procedures for handling complaints, investigating product quality deviations, and implementing corrective and preventive actions (CAPA). Adverse events often stem from or reveal product quality issues, making cGMP compliance essential for their investigation and resolution.
    *   **Documentation:** cGMP emphasizes comprehensive and accurate documentation for all aspects of drug manufacturing and quality control. This principle extends to adverse event reporting, requiring meticulous record-keeping for all reported events, investigations, and regulatory submissions.
    *   **Personnel Training:** cGMP requires that all personnel involved in manufacturing and quality control be adequately trained. This applies directly to personnel involved in adverse event reporting, ensuring they have the necessary knowledge and skills to perform their duties in compliance with regulatory standards.

**8.4 International Harmonization:**

*   Where applicable, this SOP also considers international guidelines and best practices for pharmacovigilance, such as those from the International Cooperation on Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Registration of Veterinary Medicinal Products (VICH), to ensure alignment with global standards and facilitate international market access.

Adherence to these regulatory frameworks is continuously monitored and updated to reflect any changes in laws, regulations, or guidance documents, ensuring ongoing compliance and the highest standards of product safety and quality.




### 9.0 Change Control Procedures

To maintain the integrity and effectiveness of the adverse event reporting system, any changes to this Standard Operating Procedure, or to related processes, systems, or forms, must be managed through a formal change control procedure. This ensures that all modifications are documented, reviewed, approved, and communicated to relevant personnel.

**9.1 Scope of Change Control:**

Change control applies to, but is not limited to, the following:

*   Revisions to this SOP (e.g., updates to procedures, responsibilities, or regulatory references).
*   Changes to the Adverse Event Report Form (AERF) or electronic pharmacovigilance system.
*   Modifications to data collection methods or reporting templates.
*   Updates to regulatory reporting requirements or timelines.
*   Changes to personnel roles or departmental responsibilities related to adverse event reporting.

**9.2 Change Request Initiation:**

Any employee can initiate a change request by submitting a formal Change Request Form (CRF) to the Quality Assurance (QA) department. The CRF must clearly describe the proposed change, the reason for the change, and its potential impact on the adverse event reporting process.

**9.3 Review and Assessment of Change:**

*   The QA department, in consultation with the Adverse Event Coordinator (AEC) and other relevant stakeholders (e.g., Regulatory Affairs, IT), will review and assess the proposed change.
*   This assessment will evaluate the necessity of the change, its potential impact on product safety, regulatory compliance, data integrity, and operational efficiency.
*   A risk assessment will be conducted to identify any new risks introduced by the change or any existing risks that may be exacerbated or mitigated.

**9.4 Approval of Change:**

*   All proposed changes must be reviewed and approved by designated personnel, including the QA Manager, AEC, and relevant department heads, as appropriate for the scope and impact of the change.
*   For significant changes, senior management approval may be required.
*   Approval signifies that the change has been thoroughly evaluated and is deemed necessary and appropriate.

**9.5 Implementation of Change:**

*   Once approved, the change will be implemented according to a defined plan. This may include:
    *   Revising the SOP document and assigning a new version number.
    *   Updating electronic systems or forms.
    *   Developing new training materials or updating existing ones.
    *   Communicating the change to all affected personnel.
*   The implementation plan will include a timeline and assigned responsibilities.

**9.6 Training on Changes:**

*   All personnel affected by the change must receive adequate training on the revised procedures or systems before the change becomes effective. This training will be documented as per Section 5.0.

**9.7 Effectiveness Check:**

*   Following implementation, the QA department will conduct an effectiveness check to verify that the change has achieved its intended outcome and has not introduced any unforeseen negative impacts.
*   The effectiveness check will be documented, and any issues identified will be addressed through the CAPA system.

**9.8 Documentation of Change Control:**

*   All change control activities, including CRFs, review records, approval signatures, implementation plans, training records, and effectiveness checks, must be thoroughly documented and archived. This documentation provides a complete audit trail of all modifications to the adverse event reporting system.
