### 1.1 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the detailed procedures for the chemical testing of drug substances used in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The purpose of this SOP is to ensure that all drug substances meet established quality, purity, and potency specifications before their use in the manufacturing process. Adherence to these procedures is critical for maintaining product quality, ensuring patient safety, and complying with national and international regulatory requirements, including those set forth by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), United States Department of Agriculture (USDA), and current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP).

### 1.2 Scope

This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the receipt, sampling, testing, and release of drug substances at [Company Name/Facility Name] for large animal pharmaceutical products. This includes, but is not limited to, Quality Control (QC) laboratory personnel, Quality Assurance (QA) personnel, and warehouse staff responsible for handling incoming materials. The procedures described herein cover the chemical identity, purity, strength, and other critical quality attributes of drug substances as defined by their respective monographs and internal specifications. This SOP specifically addresses the analytical methodologies, equipment calibration, data interpretation, and documentation practices associated with chemical testing. It does not cover microbiological testing or physical testing unless explicitly stated as part of a chemical assay.



## 2.0 Responsibilities

### 2.1 Quality Control (QC) Department

- **QC Manager:** Responsible for the overall management and oversight of the chemical testing laboratory. Ensures that all testing procedures are performed in accordance with this SOP, regulatory requirements, and cGMP guidelines. Approves test results, OOS investigations, and ensures adequate training of QC personnel.
- **QC Analysts:** Responsible for the accurate and timely execution of chemical testing procedures as described in this SOP. This includes sample preparation, instrument operation, data acquisition, data analysis, and initial review of results. Ensures proper maintenance and calibration of laboratory equipment. Reports any deviations or out-of-specification (OOS) results to the QC Manager.
- **QC Sample Custodian:** Responsible for the proper receipt, storage, and management of drug substance samples. Ensures sample integrity and traceability throughout the testing process.

### 2.2 Quality Assurance (QA) Department

- **QA Manager:** Responsible for reviewing and approving this SOP and any subsequent revisions. Ensures that all chemical testing activities comply with cGMP regulations and internal quality systems. Oversees OOS investigations and approves final disposition of drug substances.
- **QA Specialists:** Responsible for auditing QC laboratory practices, reviewing batch records, and ensuring data integrity. Participates in OOS investigations and provides guidance on regulatory compliance.

### 2.3 Warehouse/Receiving Department

- **Warehouse Personnel:** Responsible for the proper receipt, identification, and storage of incoming drug substances. Ensures that materials are quarantined appropriately until released by the QC and QA departments. Notifies QC of incoming shipments requiring testing.

### 2.4 Training Department

- **Training Coordinator:** Responsible for developing and maintaining training programs related to this SOP. Ensures that all personnel involved in chemical testing receive adequate and documented training before performing any tasks outlined in this SOP.



## 3.0 Detailed Procedures

This section outlines the step-by-step procedures for the chemical testing of drug substances. All procedures must be performed in accordance with cGMP guidelines, relevant pharmacopeial monographs (e.g., USP, EP, BP), and internal specifications. Any deviations from these procedures must be documented and approved by the QC Manager and QA.

### 3.1 Sample Receipt and Handling

1.  **Receipt of Samples:** Upon arrival, the Warehouse/Receiving Department will notify the QC Sample Custodian of incoming drug substance shipments. The QC Sample Custodian will verify the integrity of the container and the accuracy of the labeling against the accompanying documentation (e.g., Certificate of Analysis, packing list).
2.  **Sample Log-in:** Each received sample will be assigned a unique QC sample number. This number, along with the drug substance name, lot number, manufacturer, date of receipt, and quantity, will be recorded in the QC Sample Logbook (electronic or hard copy). The logbook entry must be complete and accurate, ensuring full traceability of the sample.
3.  **Quarantine Storage:** All incoming drug substances, including the retained samples, must be immediately placed in a designated quarantine area within the QC laboratory or warehouse. This area must be clearly labeled to prevent accidental use of unapproved materials.
4.  **Sample Preparation for Testing:**
    *   **Aseptic Sampling (if applicable):** For sterile drug substances, sampling must be performed in a certified laminar airflow hood or isolator to prevent contamination. All sampling tools must be sterile and appropriate for the material being sampled.
    *   **Representative Sampling:** A statistically representative sample must be taken from each lot of drug substance. The sampling plan will be based on the material's homogeneity, quantity, and regulatory requirements. Refer to SOP-QC-005: Sampling Procedures for Raw Materials for detailed instructions.
    *   **Labeling:** Each sample container must be clearly labeled with the drug substance name, lot number, QC sample number, date of sampling, and initials of the sampler.
    *   **Storage of Samples:** Samples for testing should be stored under conditions specified by the drug substance's stability profile (e.g., refrigerated, room temperature, protected from light). Retained samples must be stored for at least one year beyond the expiration date of the finished product.

### 3.2 General Laboratory Practices

1.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** All personnel must wear appropriate PPE, including laboratory coats, safety glasses, and gloves, at all times while in the laboratory. Additional PPE may be required depending on the specific hazards of the drug substance or reagents being handled.
2.  **Equipment Calibration and Maintenance:** All analytical instruments (e.g., HPLC, GC, UV-Vis spectrophotometer, pH meter, analytical balance) must be calibrated and maintained according to their respective SOPs and manufacturer's recommendations. Calibration records must be current and readily available.
3.  **Reagent and Standard Management:**
    *   **Preparation and Labeling:** All reagents and standards must be prepared according to documented procedures. Each container must be clearly labeled with the name, concentration, preparation date, expiration date, and initials of the preparer.
    *   **Storage:** Reagents and standards must be stored under appropriate conditions to maintain their stability and integrity.
    *   **Traceability:** All reagents and standards used for testing must be traceable to their source (e.g., Certificate of Analysis for reference standards).
4.  **Waste Disposal:** All chemical waste must be disposed of in accordance with local, state, and federal regulations. Specific waste streams (e.g., hazardous waste, non-hazardous waste) must be segregated and labeled appropriately.

### 3.3 Specific Chemical Testing Procedures

The following are general categories of chemical tests. Specific test methods for each drug substance will be detailed in individual Test Methods (TMs) or analytical specifications, which are referenced in this SOP. QC Analysts must refer to the current version of the relevant TM for precise instructions.

#### 3.3.1 Identity Testing

**Purpose:** To confirm that the drug substance is indeed what it purports to be.

**Common Techniques:**

*   **Infrared (IR) Spectroscopy:**
    1.  Prepare the sample as a KBr pellet, Nujol mull, or thin film, as specified in the TM.
    2.  Place the prepared sample in the IR spectrophotometer.
    3.  Acquire the IR spectrum. Ensure proper background subtraction.
    4.  Compare the sample spectrum to a reference standard spectrum or a pharmacopeial reference spectrum. The characteristic peaks and overall spectral fingerprint must match.
    5.  Document the spectral overlay and any observations.
*   **Ultraviolet-Visible (UV-Vis) Spectroscopy:**
    1.  Prepare a solution of the drug substance at the specified concentration using the appropriate solvent.
    2.  Transfer the solution to a quartz cuvette.
    3.  Measure the absorbance at the specified wavelengths (λmax) using a UV-Vis spectrophotometer. Perform a solvent blank.
    4.  Compare the absorbance values and spectral profile to those of a reference standard or pharmacopeial requirements.
    5.  Calculate the ratio of absorbances at two specified wavelengths if required for identity confirmation.
*   **High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) - Retention Time Comparison:**
    1.  Prepare sample and reference standard solutions as per the TM.
    2.  Inject the reference standard solution into the HPLC system. Record the retention time of the main peak.
    3.  Inject the sample solution. Record the retention time of the main peak.
    4.  Compare the retention time of the main peak in the sample chromatogram to that of the reference standard. The retention times must be within the acceptance criteria (typically ±2% or as specified).
    5.  Document the chromatograms and retention time comparison.
*   **Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC):**
    1.  Prepare sample and reference standard solutions.
    2.  Spot appropriate volumes of sample and standard solutions onto a TLC plate.
    3.  Develop the plate in the specified mobile phase.
    4.  Visualize the spots using the prescribed detection method (e.g., UV light, chemical spray).
    5.  Compare the Rf values and appearance of the sample spots to those of the reference standard. The Rf values must match, and no extraneous spots should be present.

#### 3.3.2 Purity Testing

**Purpose:** To determine the presence and quantity of impurities in the drug substance.

**Common Techniques:**

*   **HPLC for Related Substances/Impurities:**
    1.  Prepare sample, reference standard, and impurity standard solutions as per the TM. A system suitability solution containing known impurities is often required.
    2.  Set up the HPLC system with the specified column, mobile phase, flow rate, and detector wavelength.
    3.  Inject the system suitability solution to ensure the system meets resolution, tailing factor, and reproducibility requirements.
    4.  Inject the reference standard solution to quantify the main component.
    5.  Inject the sample solution. Integrate all peaks in the chromatogram.
    6.  Calculate the percentage of each individual impurity and total impurities using the appropriate response factors or area normalization methods.
    7.  Ensure that individual and total impurity levels are within the specified acceptance criteria.
*   **Gas Chromatography (GC) for Residual Solvents:**
    1.  Prepare sample and residual solvent standard solutions (e.g., using headspace technique).
    2.  Set up the GC system with the specified column, carrier gas, oven temperature program, and detector (e.g., FID, ECD).
    3.  Inject the standard solution to establish response factors for each solvent.
    4.  Inject the sample solution.
    5.  Identify and quantify residual solvents based on retention time and peak area comparison with standards.
    6.  Ensure that residual solvent levels are below the limits specified in ICH Q3C guidelines or pharmacopeial requirements.
*   **Karl Fischer Titration for Water Content:**
    1.  Calibrate the Karl Fischer titrator using a certified water standard.
    2.  Add the specified amount of drug substance to the titration vessel.
    3.  Titrate with Karl Fischer reagent until the endpoint is reached.
    4.  Record the volume of reagent consumed and calculate the water content (%).
    5.  Ensure the water content is within the specified acceptance criteria.
*   **Loss on Drying (LOD):**
    1.  Weigh a clean, dry weighing bottle.
    2.  Add a specified amount of drug substance to the weighing bottle and weigh accurately.
    3.  Place the sample in a drying oven at the specified temperature and duration, or in a vacuum oven.
    4.  Cool the sample in a desiccator and reweigh.
    5.  Calculate the percentage loss on drying.
    6.  Ensure the LOD is within the specified acceptance criteria.

#### 3.3.3 Assay (Strength) Testing

**Purpose:** To determine the quantitative amount of the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) in the drug substance.

**Common Techniques:**

*   **HPLC Assay:**
    1.  Prepare accurately weighed sample and reference standard solutions at known concentrations.
    2.  Set up the HPLC system as specified in the TM.
    3.  Inject multiple replicates of the reference standard solution to establish a calibration curve or response factor.
    4.  Inject multiple replicates of the sample solution.
    5.  Quantify the API in the sample by comparing its peak area to the calibration curve or response factor of the reference standard.
    6.  Calculate the percentage assay (potency) of the drug substance.
    7.  Ensure the assay result is within the specified acceptance criteria (e.g., 98.0% - 102.0% on an anhydrous basis).
*   **Titrimetry:**
    1.  Prepare a standardized titrant solution (e.g., acid, base, redox reagent).
    2.  Accurately weigh a specified amount of drug substance and dissolve it in the appropriate solvent.
    3.  Titrate the sample solution with the standardized titrant to a visually observed endpoint or potentiometric endpoint.
    4.  Record the volume of titrant consumed.
    5.  Calculate the assay of the drug substance based on the stoichiometry of the reaction and the concentration of the titrant.
    6.  Ensure the assay result is within the specified acceptance criteria.
*   **UV-Vis Spectrophotometric Assay:**
    1.  Prepare accurately weighed sample and reference standard solutions at specified concentrations.
    2.  Measure the absorbance of both sample and standard solutions at the maximum wavelength (λmax).
    3.  Calculate the assay of the drug substance using the Beer-Lambert Law (A = εbc) or by direct comparison of absorbances with the reference standard.
    4.  Ensure the assay result is within the specified acceptance criteria.

#### 3.3.4 Other Critical Quality Attribute Testing

*   **pH Determination:**
    1.  Calibrate the pH meter using at least two standard buffer solutions (e.g., pH 4.01, 7.00, 10.00).
    2.  Prepare a solution of the drug substance as specified in the TM.
    3.  Immerse the electrode in the sample solution and record the stable pH reading.
    4.  Ensure the pH is within the specified acceptance criteria.
*   **Specific Rotation/Optical Rotation:**
    1.  Calibrate the polarimeter using a certified quartz plate or sucrose solution.
    2.  Prepare a solution of the drug substance at a specified concentration in a suitable solvent.
    3.  Measure the optical rotation at the specified wavelength (e.g., sodium D line, 589 nm) and temperature.
    4.  Calculate the specific rotation using the formula: [α] = (100 * α) / (l * c), where α is observed rotation, l is path length in dm, and c is concentration in g/100 mL.
    5.  Ensure the specific rotation is within the specified acceptance criteria.
*   **Heavy Metals Test:**
    1.  Prepare the sample solution as specified in the pharmacopeia (e.g., USP <231> or <233>).
    2.  Prepare a standard solution of lead or other heavy metals.
    3.  Perform the colorimetric comparison or instrumental analysis (e.g., ICP-OES, AAS) as per the method.
    4.  Ensure the heavy metals content is below the specified limit.

### 3.4 Out-of-Specification (OOS) and Out-of-Trend (OOT) Investigations

1.  **Initial Assessment:** If a test result is OOS, the QC Analyst will immediately notify the QC Manager. An initial assessment will be performed to determine if a clear assignable cause (e.g., instrument malfunction, sample preparation error) can be identified.
2.  **Phase I Investigation:** If no assignable cause is found during the initial assessment, a formal Phase I OOS investigation will be initiated. This involves re-examining the original data, reviewing instrument calibration records, and re-testing the original sample (if appropriate and justified). All steps must be documented.
3.  **Phase II Investigation:** If Phase I does not identify an assignable cause, a Phase II investigation will be initiated. This may involve testing new samples from the same lot, using different analysts or equipment, and evaluating the manufacturing process. This phase requires cross-functional collaboration with QA and Manufacturing.
4.  **Documentation and Conclusion:** All OOS/OOT investigations must be thoroughly documented, including the scope, findings, root cause, corrective actions, and preventive actions (CAPA). The investigation must conclude with a clear decision regarding the disposition of the affected drug substance lot (e.g., release, reprocess, reject).
5.  **QA Review and Approval:** All OOS/OOT investigation reports must be reviewed and approved by the QA Department to ensure compliance with regulatory requirements and internal quality standards. Refer to SOP-QA-003: Out-of-Specification (OOS) and Out-of-Trend (OOT) Investigations for comprehensive details.



## 4.0 Documentation Requirements

Accurate, complete, and contemporaneous documentation is a critical component of cGMP compliance and ensures the traceability and integrity of all chemical testing activities. All documentation must adhere to the principles of ALCOA (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate).

### 4.1 Laboratory Notebooks/Electronic Records

1.  **Test Records:** All raw data, calculations, observations, and results generated during chemical testing must be recorded directly into an approved laboratory notebook or electronic laboratory notebook (ELN) system. Each entry must be dated and signed/initialed by the QC Analyst performing the test.
2.  **Instrument Printouts:** All instrument printouts (e.g., chromatograms, spectra, raw data files) must be securely attached to the laboratory notebook or electronically linked to the ELN entry. These printouts must be clearly identified with the sample number, test date, and analyst initials.
3.  **Calculations:** All calculations performed must be clearly documented and verifiable. If using a spreadsheet or software for calculations, the validated version must be used, and the input parameters and results must be recorded.
4.  **Deviations:** Any deviations from this SOP or the specific Test Method must be immediately documented, including the nature of the deviation, the reason, and the impact on the test results. Deviations must be reviewed and approved by the QC Manager and QA.

### 4.2 Certificate of Analysis (CoA)

1.  **Preparation:** Upon completion of all required chemical tests and review of results, a Certificate of Analysis (CoA) will be prepared for each lot of drug substance. The CoA must include:
    *   Drug substance name
    *   Lot number
    *   Manufacturer
    *   Date of manufacture and expiration date
    *   List of all tests performed
    *   Test results and acceptance criteria
    *   Analytical method references
    *   Date of release
    *   Signature of authorized QC and QA personnel
2.  **Review and Approval:** The CoA must be reviewed by the QC Manager and approved by the QA Manager before the drug substance lot can be released for use in manufacturing.

### 4.3 Data Archiving

1.  **Retention:** All laboratory notebooks, electronic records, CoAs, and supporting documentation must be archived and retained for a period consistent with regulatory requirements (e.g., typically one year after the expiration date of the finished product or as specified by local regulations).
2.  **Accessibility:** Archived data must be readily retrievable for inspection by regulatory authorities or for internal review. The archiving system must ensure data integrity and prevent unauthorized access or alteration.



## 5.0 Training Requirements

All personnel involved in the chemical testing of drug substances must be adequately trained and qualified to perform their assigned tasks. Training records must be maintained and readily available for inspection.

### 5.1 Initial Training

1.  **SOP Review:** All new QC Analysts and other relevant personnel must read and understand this SOP, as well as all referenced Test Methods and related quality system documents (e.g., OOS investigation SOP, equipment calibration SOPs).
2.  **On-the-Job Training (OJT):** New personnel must receive hands-on training from a qualified and experienced QC Analyst. OJT must cover all aspects of chemical testing, including sample handling, instrument operation, data analysis, and documentation practices.
3.  **Competency Assessment:** After completing OJT, personnel must demonstrate proficiency in performing the assigned tasks. This may include practical demonstrations, written examinations, or a combination thereof. Competency must be assessed and documented by the QC Manager or a designated trainer.

### 5.2 Retraining and Continuing Education

1.  **SOP Revisions:** All personnel must be retrained on any revisions made to this SOP or related Test Methods. Retraining must occur prior to the implementation of the revised document.
2.  **Periodic Retraining:** Periodic retraining and competency assessments will be conducted at a frequency determined by the Training Department, typically every one to two years, to ensure continued proficiency and adherence to current practices.
3.  **Continuing Education:** QC personnel are encouraged to participate in continuing education programs, workshops, and seminars to stay abreast of new analytical techniques, regulatory updates, and industry best practices.



## 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Robust quality control measures are essential to ensure the accuracy, precision, and reliability of chemical testing results. These measures are integral to maintaining product quality and regulatory compliance.

### 6.1 System Suitability Testing (SST)

1.  **Purpose:** SST is an integral part of chromatographic and other analytical procedures, performed before or during the analysis of samples, to ensure that the complete analytical system (instrument, reagents, column, and analyst) is functioning correctly and capable of generating results of acceptable quality.
2.  **Frequency:** SST must be performed at the beginning of each analytical run, after major instrument maintenance, or as specified in the individual Test Method.
3.  **Parameters:** Typical SST parameters include:
    *   **Resolution:** The ability of the system to separate two components.
    *   **Tailing Factor:** A measure of peak symmetry.
    *   **Theoretical Plates:** A measure of column efficiency.
    *   **Relative Standard Deviation (RSD):** A measure of the precision of replicate injections.
    *   **Signal-to-Noise Ratio:** For detection limits.
4.  **Acceptance Criteria:** All SST parameters must meet the predefined acceptance criteria specified in the individual Test Method. If any parameter fails, the system must be troubleshooted, and the issue resolved before sample analysis can proceed. All SST failures and corrective actions must be documented.

### 6.2 Calibration and Qualification of Equipment

1.  **Calibration:** All critical analytical instruments (e.g., HPLC, GC, UV-Vis, balances, pH meters) must be calibrated at defined intervals using certified reference materials or standards traceable to national or international standards. Calibration procedures and frequencies are detailed in specific equipment SOPs.
2.  **Qualification (IQ/OQ/PQ):** New or significantly modified equipment must undergo Installation Qualification (IQ), Operational Qualification (OQ), and Performance Qualification (PQ) to ensure it is installed correctly, operates according to specifications, and performs consistently for its intended use. Re-qualification may be required after major repairs or relocations.
3.  **Maintenance:** Preventive maintenance schedules must be established and followed for all analytical equipment to minimize downtime and ensure optimal performance. Maintenance activities must be documented.

### 6.3 Use of Reference Standards and Reagents

1.  **Certified Reference Standards (CRSs):** Whenever available, CRSs (e.g., USP Reference Standards, EP Reference Standards) must be used for identity, purity, and assay testing. CRSs must be stored and handled according to their certificates of analysis and manufacturer's instructions.
2.  **Working Standards:** If working standards are prepared from CRSs, their potency and purity must be accurately determined and documented. Working standards must be re-qualified periodically.
3.  **Reagent Quality:** All reagents, solvents, and media used in chemical testing must be of appropriate grade (e.g., analytical reagent grade, HPLC grade) and meet specified quality requirements. Expiration dates must be monitored, and expired materials must not be used.

### 6.4 Control Samples and Blanks

1.  **Positive Controls:** Used to confirm the proper functioning of the analytical method and the detection of the analyte. A positive control containing a known concentration of the analyte should be run periodically.
2.  **Negative Controls/Blanks:** Used to detect contamination or interference from reagents, solvents, or the environment. Solvent blanks, reagent blanks, and matrix blanks should be run as appropriate.
3.  **Spiked Samples:** Used to assess method accuracy and recovery. Known amounts of analyte or impurities are added to a sample matrix, and the recovery is measured.

### 6.5 Data Review and Approval

1.  **Primary Review:** All raw data, calculations, and results must be reviewed by a second qualified QC Analyst or the QC Manager to ensure accuracy, completeness, and compliance with the SOP and Test Method.
2.  **Secondary Review (QA):** The QA Department performs a secondary review of critical data, such as CoAs and OOS investigation reports, to ensure overall compliance with cGMP and internal quality systems.

### 6.6 Trend Analysis

1.  **Purpose:** Regular trend analysis of chemical testing data (e.g., assay results, impurity levels, OOS rates) helps identify potential shifts or drifts in product quality or analytical method performance. This proactive approach allows for early detection of issues and implementation of corrective actions.
2.  **Frequency:** Trend analysis should be performed periodically (e.g., quarterly, annually) and reviewed by QC and QA management.
3.  **Action Limits:** Establish appropriate action limits for key parameters. If trends approach or exceed these limits, an investigation should be initiated to determine the root cause and implement preventive measures.



## 7.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in the chemical testing laboratory. All personnel must be aware of and adhere to safety protocols to prevent accidents, injuries, and exposure to hazardous materials. This section outlines general safety considerations; specific hazards and precautions for individual chemicals will be detailed in their respective Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and Test Methods.

### 7.1 General Laboratory Safety

1.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Always wear appropriate PPE, including laboratory coats, safety glasses or goggles, and chemical-resistant gloves. Additional PPE, such as face shields, respirators, or specialized clothing, may be required depending on the specific task or chemical being handled.
2.  **Chemical Handling:**
    *   **Read SDS:** Before handling any chemical, thoroughly read and understand its Safety Data Sheet (SDS). Pay close attention to information regarding hazards, safe handling, storage, and emergency procedures.
    *   **Fume Hoods:** Perform all procedures involving volatile or hazardous chemicals in a certified chemical fume hood to ensure adequate ventilation and minimize exposure to fumes and vapors.
    *   **Dispensing:** Dispense chemicals carefully to avoid spills. Use appropriate dispensing equipment (e.g., pipettes, graduated cylinders) and work over a spill tray.
    *   **Labeling:** Ensure all chemical containers are clearly labeled with the chemical name, hazard warnings, and expiration date.
3.  **Emergency Procedures:**
    *   **Eyewash and Safety Showers:** Know the location and proper use of eyewash stations and safety showers. In case of chemical splash to the eyes or skin, immediately flush with copious amounts of water for at least 15 minutes.
    *   **Spill Kits:** Familiarize yourself with the location and contents of chemical spill kits. In the event of a spill, follow the established spill response procedure. Contain the spill, neutralize if necessary, and absorb the material. Dispose of contaminated materials properly.
    *   **Fire Extinguishers:** Know the location and proper use of fire extinguishers. In case of fire, activate the fire alarm and evacuate the area if the fire cannot be safely extinguished.
    *   **Emergency Contacts:** Keep a list of emergency contact numbers (e.g., emergency services, poison control, facility safety officer) readily accessible.
4.  **Waste Disposal:** Dispose of all chemical waste in designated, properly labeled waste containers. Segregate hazardous waste from non-hazardous waste. Never pour chemicals down the drain unless specifically permitted.
5.  **Housekeeping:** Maintain a clean and organized work area. Keep aisles clear and free of obstructions. Immediately clean up any spills.

### 7.2 Specific Chemical Hazards

QC Analysts must be aware of the specific hazards associated with the drug substances, reagents, and solvents used in chemical testing. This includes, but is not limited to:

*   **Flammable Solvents:** Store flammable solvents in approved flammable liquid storage cabinets. Eliminate ignition sources (e.g., open flames, sparks) when working with these solvents.
*   **Corrosive Chemicals:** Handle acids and bases with extreme care. Always add acid to water slowly, never water to acid. Use appropriate secondary containment.
*   **Toxic Substances:** Handle highly toxic substances in a fume hood with appropriate gloves and respiratory protection if necessary. Minimize exposure and ensure proper disposal.
*   **Sensitizers:** Be aware of chemicals that can cause allergic reactions upon repeated exposure. Use appropriate controls to prevent sensitization.
*   **Drug Substance Specific Hazards:** Some drug substances may have inherent pharmacological activity or toxicity. Consult the SDS for specific handling precautions related to the drug substance itself, especially for highly potent compounds.

### 7.3 Accident and Incident Reporting

Any accident, injury, near-miss, or unsafe condition must be immediately reported to the QC Manager and the facility safety officer. All incidents will be investigated to determine the root cause and implement corrective and preventive actions to prevent recurrence.



## 8.0 Regulatory Compliance References

Adherence to national and international regulatory guidelines is critical for pharmaceutical manufacturing. This SOP is developed in accordance with the following key regulatory frameworks and guidelines, ensuring that all chemical testing procedures meet the highest standards of quality and safety.

### 8.1 Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

*   **21 CFR Part 210 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Processing, Packing, or Holding of Drugs; General:** This regulation outlines the minimum cGMP requirements for the methods, facilities, and controls used in the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug to assure that such drug meets the requirements of the Act as to safety, and has the identity and strength, and meets the quality and purity characteristics, which it purports or is represented to possess.
*   **21 CFR Part 211 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation specifically details cGMP for finished pharmaceuticals, including requirements for laboratory controls (Subpart I), which cover the establishment of specifications, standards, sampling plans, test procedures, and the testing of components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, and drug products.
*   **FDA Guidance for Industry: Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production (2006):** This guidance provides recommendations for how to investigate OOS test results, including the responsibilities of laboratory personnel and management, the phases of an OOS investigation, and documentation requirements. [1]
*   **FDA Guidance for Industry: Analytical Procedures and Methods Validation for Drugs and Biologics (2015):** This guidance provides recommendations on submitting analytical procedures and methods validation data in investigational new drug applications (INDs), new drug applications (NDAs), abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs), and biologics license applications (BLAs). It covers aspects such as accuracy, precision, specificity, detection limit, quantitation limit, linearity, and range. [2]

### 8.2 United States Department of Agriculture (USDA)

While the primary oversight for drug substances in pharmaceuticals is typically FDA, the USDA plays a crucial role in regulating veterinary biologics and certain animal-derived products used in pharmaceuticals. For large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing, especially those involving animal-derived components or veterinary biologics, USDA regulations may apply.

*   **9 CFR Part 101 - Definitions:** Provides definitions for terms used in the regulations governing veterinary biologics.
*   **9 CFR Part 104 - Permits for Biological Products:** Outlines requirements for permits for the manufacture, importation, and interstate shipment of veterinary biological products.
*   **9 CFR Part 113 - Standard Requirements for Veterinary Biological Products:** Details general requirements for the testing of veterinary biological products, including sterility, purity, potency, and safety tests. While not directly chemical testing, the principles of quality control and documentation are aligned with cGMP.

### 8.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP)

cGMP refers to the Current Good Manufacturing Practice regulations enforced by the FDA. cGMPs provide for systems that assure proper design, monitoring, and control of manufacturing processes and facilities. Adherence to the cGMP regulations assures the identity, strength, quality, and purity of drug products by requiring that manufacturers adequately control manufacturing operations. This includes establishing strong quality management systems, obtaining appropriate quality raw materials, establishing robust operating procedures, detecting and investigating product quality deviations, and maintaining reliable testing laboratories.

*   **ICH Q2(R1) - Validation of Analytical Procedures: Text and Methodology:** This International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) guideline provides guidance on the validation of analytical procedures, including specificity, linearity, range, accuracy, precision, detection limit, quantitation limit, and robustness. It is widely adopted by regulatory authorities worldwide, including the FDA. [3]
*   **ICH Q7 - Good Manufacturing Practice Guide for Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients:** This guideline provides guidance regarding good manufacturing practice for the manufacturing of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) under an appropriate quality management system. It covers all aspects from the starting materials to the release of the API. [4]

## 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), or to any associated Test Methods or specifications, must be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are properly evaluated, documented, approved, and implemented, maintaining the integrity and compliance of the quality system.

### 9.1 Initiation of Change

1.  **Change Request Form:** Any personnel identifying a need for a change to this SOP or a related document must complete a Change Request Form (CRF). The CRF must clearly describe the proposed change, the reason for the change, and its potential impact on product quality, safety, or regulatory compliance.
2.  **Justification:** A clear justification for the proposed change must be provided, supported by data or regulatory requirements.

### 9.2 Evaluation of Change

1.  **Cross-Functional Review:** The CRF will be reviewed by a cross-functional team, including representatives from QC, QA, Manufacturing, Regulatory Affairs, and other relevant departments. The team will assess the impact of the proposed change on:
    *   Product quality and stability
    *   Analytical method performance
    *   Regulatory compliance
    *   Training requirements
    *   Equipment and facilities
    *   Documentation (e.g., batch records, specifications, CoAs)
2.  **Risk Assessment:** A risk assessment will be conducted to identify and evaluate any potential risks associated with the proposed change. Mitigation strategies will be developed for identified risks.

### 9.3 Approval of Change

1.  **Management Approval:** All proposed changes must be approved by the QC Manager, QA Manager, and other designated management personnel. Approval signifies that the change has been thoroughly evaluated and is deemed acceptable.
2.  **Regulatory Notification (if applicable):** For significant changes that may impact product quality or regulatory submissions, notification to or approval from relevant regulatory authorities (e.g., FDA, USDA) may be required prior to implementation. This will be determined by the Regulatory Affairs department.

### 9.4 Implementation of Change

1.  **Revision of Documents:** Once approved, the SOP and any affected documents (e.g., Test Methods, forms) will be revised to incorporate the change. The revised document will be assigned a new version number and effective date.
2.  **Training:** All personnel affected by the change must be trained on the revised document before its effective date. Training records must be maintained.
3.  **Communication:** The change and its implications will be communicated to all relevant departments.
4.  **Effectiveness Check:** After implementation, an effectiveness check may be performed to confirm that the change has achieved its intended outcome and has not introduced any unforeseen negative impacts.

### 9.5 Version Control

This SOP will be maintained under strict version control. Each revision will be assigned a unique version number, and a revision history will be maintained to track all changes made to the document. Only the current, approved version of this SOP shall be used for chemical testing procedures.
