### 1.0 Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the comprehensive program for stability testing of large animal pharmaceutical products manufactured by [Company Name]. The purpose of this SOP is to provide detailed guidelines for designing, executing, and evaluating stability studies to establish the shelf-life, re-test periods, and recommended storage conditions for drug substances and medicinal products. This ensures the quality, safety, and efficacy of large animal pharmaceuticals throughout their intended lifecycle, from manufacturing to the end-user.

The scope of this SOP covers all aspects of stability testing for new veterinary drug substances and medicinal products, including but not limited to:

*   **Drug Substances:** Active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) used in the formulation of large animal pharmaceuticals.
*   **Medicinal Products:** Finished dosage forms intended for administration to large animals, such as cattle, horses, swine, and poultry.
*   **Stress Testing:** Studies conducted to identify likely degradation products and pathways, and to validate the stability-indicating power of analytical procedures.
*   **Long-term, Accelerated, and Intermediate Stability Studies:** Studies performed under various controlled environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, light) to assess the physical, chemical, biological, and microbiological attributes of the products over time.
*   **In-use Stability Studies:** Where applicable, studies to evaluate the stability of multi-dose injectable animal drug products after their initial opening and during their period of use.
*   **Post-approval Stability Monitoring:** Ongoing stability testing of commercial batches to confirm the established shelf-life and detect any unexpected changes.

This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the development, manufacturing, quality control, and regulatory affairs of large animal pharmaceuticals at [Company Name]. It is designed to ensure compliance with relevant regulatory guidelines and current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) for veterinary medicinal products, specifically those outlined by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA). This document does not cover stability testing for veterinary diagnostic products.




### 2.0 Responsibilities

Effective implementation of the Stability Testing Program requires clear delineation of responsibilities across various departments and personnel. All individuals involved must be adequately trained and understand their roles as defined below:

*   **Quality Control (QC) Department:**
    *   Develop, validate, and maintain stability-indicating analytical methods.
    *   Execute all stability studies (long-term, accelerated, intermediate, in-use) according to approved protocols.
    *   Monitor storage conditions of stability chambers and ensure their calibration and qualification.
    *   Analyze stability samples at specified time points and generate accurate, reliable data.
    *   Interpret stability data, identify trends, and report any out-of-specification (OOS) or out-of-trend (OOT) results.
    *   Prepare stability reports and summaries for regulatory submissions and internal review.
    *   Manage and maintain stability samples and reference standards.
    *   Ensure compliance with cGMP regulations and internal quality systems related to stability testing.

*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Department:**
    *   Review and approve all stability protocols, reports, and associated documentation.
    *   Ensure that stability studies are conducted in compliance with this SOP, regulatory guidelines, and cGMP.
    *   Conduct audits of the stability testing program and facilities to ensure adherence to established procedures.
    *   Oversee change control procedures related to stability studies and product dating.
    *   Provide final approval for product shelf-life and re-test periods based on stability data.
    *   Manage and approve deviations and investigations related to stability testing.

*   **Research and Development (R&D) Department:**
    *   Provide preliminary stability data and information during product development.
    *   Collaborate with QC to develop and validate stability-indicating analytical methods.
    *   Provide scientific rationale and support for stability study design and interpretation of results.
    *   Assist in the investigation of stability-related issues and product degradation pathways.

*   **Manufacturing Department:**
    *   Produce representative batches of drug substances and medicinal products for stability studies.
    *   Ensure that manufacturing processes are consistent and controlled to produce stable products.
    *   Provide manufacturing batch records and relevant production information for stability studies.
    *   Implement corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) based on stability findings.

*   **Regulatory Affairs Department:**
    *   Prepare and submit stability data and reports to regulatory authorities (e.g., FDA, USDA) as part of registration applications and post-approval variations.
    *   Stay updated on current and emerging regulatory requirements for stability testing of veterinary pharmaceuticals.
    *   Respond to regulatory inquiries and deficiencies related to stability data.

*   **Management:**
    *   Provide necessary resources (personnel, equipment, budget) for the effective operation of the stability testing program.
    *   Ensure that all personnel involved in stability testing are adequately trained and qualified.
    *   Review and approve major stability-related decisions and policies.
    *   Promote a culture of quality and compliance within the organization.

Each individual involved in the stability testing program is responsible for adhering to the procedures outlined in this SOP and reporting any deviations or issues to their immediate supervisor and the QA department.




### 3.0 Detailed Procedures

This section describes the detailed procedures for conducting stability studies for large animal pharmaceutical products. Adherence to these procedures is critical for generating reliable data that supports product shelf-life and re-test periods.

#### 3.1 Stability Study Design

Stability studies are designed to provide evidence on how the quality of a drug substance or medicinal product varies with time under the influence of various environmental factors. The design should consider the product's characteristics, dosage form, and intended use.

##### 3.1.1 Selection of Batches

For formal stability studies, data should be provided on at least three primary batches of the drug substance or medicinal product. These batches should be manufactured to a minimum of pilot scale, using the same synthetic route and manufacturing process that simulates the final production process. The quality of these batches should be representative of the material to be produced on a commercial scale [1].

For veterinary biological products, a minimum of three (3) serials for each product code produced in a manufacturing facility according to the Outline of Production should be used. For product lines, a minimum of three (3) serials of the largest combination and at least one (1) serial from each fallout product should be included. Each serial should be created from a different lot of bulk antigen for each fraction [2].

##### 3.1.2 Container Closure System

Stability studies must be conducted on the drug substance or medicinal product packaged in the container closure system proposed for storage and distribution. This ensures that the packaging does not adversely affect the product's stability [1].

##### 3.1.3 Specification

Stability studies should include testing of those attributes of the drug substance or medicinal product that are susceptible to change during storage and are likely to influence quality, safety, and/or efficacy. This includes physical, chemical, biological, and microbiological attributes. Validated stability-indicating analytical procedures must be applied [1].

#### 3.2 Testing Frequency

The frequency of testing should be sufficient to establish the stability profile of the drug substance or medicinal product.

##### 3.2.1 Long-Term Studies

For drug substances or medicinal products with a proposed re-test period or shelf-life of at least 12 months, the frequency of testing at the long-term storage condition should normally be every 3 months over the first year, every 6 months over the second year, and annually thereafter through the proposed re-test period or shelf-life [1].

##### 3.2.2 Accelerated Studies

At the accelerated storage condition, a minimum of three time points, including the initial and final time points (e.g., 0, 3, and 6 months), from a 6-month study is recommended. If it is expected that results from accelerated studies are likely to approach significant change criteria, increased testing should be conducted [1].

##### 3.2.3 Intermediate Studies

When testing at the intermediate storage condition is required due to significant change at the accelerated storage condition, a minimum of four time points, including the initial and final time points (e.g., 0, 6, 9, 12 months), from a 12-month study is recommended [1].

#### 3.3 Storage Conditions

Drug substances and medicinal products should be evaluated under storage conditions that test their thermal stability and, if applicable, their sensitivity to moisture and light. The chosen storage conditions and study lengths should cover storage, shipment, and subsequent use [1].

##### 3.3.1 General Case

| Study        | Storage Condition             | Minimum Time Period Covered by Data at Submission |
| :----------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| Long-term    | 25°C ± 2°C/60% RH ± 5% RH or 30°C ± 2°C/65% RH ± 5% RH | 12 months                                         |
| Intermediate | 30°C ± 2°C/65% RH ± 5% RH     | 6 months                                          |
| Accelerated  | 40°C ± 2°C/75% RH ± 5% RH     | 6 months                                          |

*Note: If 30°C ± 2°C/65% RH ± 5% RH is the long-term condition, there is no intermediate condition [1].*

##### 3.3.2 Photostability Testing

Photostability testing should be an integral part of stress testing. The standard conditions for photostability testing are described in VICH GL5 [1].

#### 3.4 Analytical Procedures

Stability studies must be conducted using validated stability-indicating analytical procedures. These methods should be capable of detecting changes over time in pertinent properties of the product.

##### 3.4.1 Stability-Indicating Assay

A stability-indicating assay is a validated quantitative analytical procedure that can detect changes over time in a pertinent property of the product. Quantitative assays (e.g., live bacterial count, live virus titration) and relative potency assays can be considered stability-indicating if validated appropriately [2].

##### 3.4.2 Degradation Products

Examining degradation products under stress conditions is useful in establishing degradation pathways and developing and validating suitable analytical procedures. It may not be necessary to examine specifically for certain degradation products if it has been demonstrated that they are not formed under accelerated or long-term storage conditions [1].

#### 3.5 Evaluation of Stability Data

Stability data should be evaluated to determine the shelf-life or re-test period and to establish recommended storage conditions. This involves statistical analysis of the data to determine the rate of degradation and to predict the time at which the product will no longer meet its specifications.

##### 3.5.1 Mean Potency Loss (MPL)

For veterinary biological products, data should be analyzed to obtain a final estimate of the mean potency loss (MPL) and to verify the appropriateness of the practical standard deviation (PSD). A suitable regression model should be fitted to the potency values to describe the potency degradation profile [2].

##### 3.5.2 Submission to CVB (for USDA-regulated products)

For USDA-regulated products, a report describing the study design, analysis, and final conclusions should be submitted to the Center for Veterinary Biologics (CVB). This report should include all raw data and calculated potency test results in accordance with the CVB Data Guide. Reports should be submitted within eight (8) months of the final potency test [2].

#### 3.6 Post-Approval Stability Monitoring

For products licensed after April 13, 2018, product stability must be monitored. This involves testing a percentage of serials produced in a calendar year (e.g., three percent, with a minimum of one and a maximum of three serials). These serials should be tested within thirty (30) days of expiration to monitor the product's stability. A monitoring plan describing serial selection should be submitted to the CVB, and all data should be submitted cumulatively every three (3) years for review [2].




### 4.0 Documentation Requirements

Accurate and comprehensive documentation is essential for demonstrating compliance with regulatory requirements and for ensuring the traceability and integrity of stability data. All documentation related to stability studies must be maintained in a controlled and readily accessible manner.

Key documentation requirements include:

*   **Stability Study Protocols:** Detailed protocols must be prepared and approved prior to initiating any stability study. These protocols should include:
    *   SOP number and title.
    *   Product name and code.
    *   Batch numbers and manufacturing dates of the materials to be tested.
    *   Study design, including storage conditions (temperature, humidity, light), and duration.
    *   Testing schedule and time points.
    *   List of attributes to be tested (physical, chemical, biological, microbiological).
    *   References to validated analytical methods.
    *   Acceptance criteria for each attribute.
    *   Sample size and sampling plan.
    *   Statistical methods for data analysis.
    *   Responsibilities of personnel involved.
    *   Procedures for handling deviations and OOS/OOT results.
    *   Approval signatures and dates.

*   **Raw Data:** All raw data generated during stability testing must be meticulously recorded. This includes, but is not limited to:
    *   Laboratory notebooks and data sheets.
    *   Instrument printouts and electronic data files.
    *   Chromatograms, spectra, and other analytical outputs.
    *   Environmental monitoring records for stability chambers.
    *   Calibration and maintenance records for equipment.

*   **Stability Study Reports:** Comprehensive reports must be prepared upon completion of each stability study or at pre-defined intervals (e.g., annual reports). These reports should summarize the study design, present all generated data, analyze trends, and draw conclusions regarding product stability. Key elements of a stability report include:
    *   SOP number and title.
    *   Product name and code.
    *   Study period.
    *   Summary of results for all tested attributes at each time point.
    *   Statistical analysis of data, including shelf-life determination and re-test periods.
    *   Discussion of any observed trends, OOS/OOT results, or deviations.
    *   Conclusions and recommendations for storage conditions and shelf-life.
    *   References to supporting documentation.
    *   Approval signatures and dates.

*   **Certificates of Analysis (CoA):** CoAs for all batches used in stability studies must be maintained. These documents confirm the quality and purity of the starting materials.

*   **Change Control Records:** Any changes to the product, manufacturing process, analytical methods, or packaging that could impact product stability must be documented through the change control system. Stability studies may be required to assess the impact of such changes.

*   **Training Records:** Records of all personnel training related to stability testing procedures and cGMP requirements must be maintained.

*   **Archiving:** All stability-related documentation, including protocols, raw data, and reports, must be archived in a secure and retrievable manner for the entire retention period as required by regulatory agencies (e.g., FDA, USDA) and internal policies.

### 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Robust quality control measures are integral to the reliability and integrity of the Stability Testing Program. These measures ensure that all data generated is accurate, precise, and scientifically sound, thereby supporting valid conclusions regarding product stability.

Key quality control measures include:

*   **Validation of Analytical Methods:** All analytical methods used for stability testing must be fully validated according to ICH/VICH guidelines and cGMP principles. Method validation should demonstrate:
    *   **Specificity:** The ability to unequivocally assess the analyte in the presence of components that may be expected to be present, including impurities, degradation products, and matrix components.
    *   **Accuracy:** The closeness of agreement between the value which is accepted either as a true value or an accepted reference value and the value found.
    *   **Precision:** The closeness of agreement among a series of measurements obtained from multiple sampling of the same homogeneous sample under the prescribed conditions.
    *   **Repeatability (Intra-assay precision):** Precision under the same operating conditions over a short interval of time.
    *   **Intermediate Precision (Inter-assay precision):** Precision within the same laboratory over different days, or with different analysts or equipment.
    *   **Reproducibility:** Precision between different laboratories.
    *   **Detection Limit (DL):** The lowest amount of analyte in a sample that can be detected but not necessarily quantified.
    *   **Quantitation Limit (QL):** The lowest amount of analyte in a sample that can be quantitatively determined with suitable precision and accuracy.
    *   **Linearity:** The ability of the analytical method to elicit test results that are directly, or by a well-defined mathematical transformation, proportional to the concentration of analyte in samples within a given range.
    *   **Range:** The interval between the upper and lower concentration of analyte in the sample for which it has been demonstrated that the analytical procedure has a suitable level of precision, accuracy, and linearity.
    *   **Robustness:** The capacity of an analytical method to remain unaffected by small, but deliberate, variations in method parameters.

*   **Calibration and Qualification of Equipment:** All equipment used in stability testing, including stability chambers, analytical instruments (e.g., HPLC, spectrophotometers), and environmental monitoring devices, must be regularly calibrated, qualified (IQ/OQ/PQ), and maintained according to established schedules and procedures. Calibration records must be meticulously kept.

*   **Use of Reference Standards:** Certified reference standards and working standards must be used for all quantitative analytical tests. These standards must be properly stored, handled, and their purity and potency verified periodically.

*   **Control Samples and System Suitability:** Appropriate control samples (e.g., positive controls, negative controls, system suitability samples) must be included in each analytical run to ensure the proper functioning of the analytical system and the validity of the results.

*   **Data Review and Approval:** All raw data, calculations, and analytical results must undergo a thorough independent review by a second qualified individual. Any discrepancies or errors must be investigated and resolved before data approval. Final stability reports must be reviewed and approved by designated QA personnel.

*   **Trend Analysis:** Stability data should be subjected to ongoing trend analysis to identify any potential shifts or drifts in product attributes over time. Early detection of adverse trends allows for timely investigation and corrective actions, preventing potential quality issues.

*   **Deviation Management:** Any deviation from approved stability protocols or procedures must be documented, investigated, and its impact on the study assessed. Appropriate corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) must be implemented to prevent recurrence.

*   **Sample Management:** Stability samples must be properly identified, stored under specified conditions, and handled to prevent contamination, mix-ups, or degradation. A robust sample management system, including inventory control and chain of custody, must be in place.

*   **Audit and Self-Inspection:** Regular internal audits and self-inspections of the Stability Testing Program should be conducted to assess compliance with this SOP, cGMP regulations, and other applicable quality standards. Findings from these audits should lead to continuous improvement initiatives.

By implementing these quality control measures, [Company Name] ensures the generation of high-quality, reliable stability data that forms the basis for sound scientific decisions regarding the shelf-life and storage conditions of large animal pharmaceutical products.




### 7.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all operations, including stability testing. All personnel involved in the Stability Testing Program must adhere to general laboratory safety rules and specific safety precautions related to the handling of pharmaceutical products, chemicals, and equipment. This section outlines key safety considerations.

*   **General Laboratory Safety:**
    *   Always wear appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), including laboratory coats, safety glasses, and gloves, as required by the specific task and risk assessment.
    *   Familiarize yourself with the location of safety showers, eyewash stations, fire extinguishers, and emergency exits.
    *   Maintain a clean and organized work area to prevent accidents.
    *   Do not eat, drink, or apply cosmetics in the laboratory.
    *   Wash hands thoroughly after handling chemicals or biological materials and before leaving the laboratory.

*   **Chemical Handling Safety:**
    *   Handle all chemicals, especially active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and reagents, with care and according to their Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) or Safety Data Sheets (SDS).
    *   Ensure proper ventilation when working with volatile or hazardous chemicals.
    *   Use fume hoods for operations that may generate hazardous fumes or dust.
    *   Dispose of chemical waste according to established hazardous waste disposal procedures.
    *   Label all containers clearly with their contents, hazard warnings, and dates.

*   **Biological Material Handling Safety:**
    *   When handling biological samples (e.g., live vaccines, microbial cultures), follow established biosafety protocols and work in appropriate biosafety cabinets (BSCs) if required.
    *   Dispose of biological waste in designated biohazard containers.
    *   Be aware of potential allergens or sensitizers in pharmaceutical products and take appropriate precautions.

*   **Equipment Safety:**
    *   Operate all laboratory equipment, including stability chambers, analytical instruments, and centrifuges, according to the manufacturer's instructions and established SOPs.
    *   Ensure that all electrical equipment is properly grounded and maintained.
    *   Report any malfunctioning equipment immediately to the supervisor and maintenance personnel.
    *   Be aware of potential hazards associated with high-pressure gases, vacuum systems, and extreme temperatures (hot/cold) in stability chambers.

*   **Emergency Procedures:**
    *   Familiarize yourself with emergency contact numbers and procedures for chemical spills, fires, injuries, or other incidents.
    *   In case of a spill, follow the spill containment and cleanup procedures. For major spills, evacuate the area and notify emergency personnel.
    *   In case of exposure to hazardous materials, immediately flush the affected area with water and seek medical attention.

*   **Ergonomics:**
    *   Practice good ergonomic habits to prevent musculoskeletal injuries, especially during repetitive tasks or when lifting heavy items.
    *   Adjust workstations and chairs to promote comfortable and safe posture.

*   **Product-Specific Hazards:**
    *   Be aware of any specific hazards associated with the large animal pharmaceutical products being tested, such as toxicity, flammability, or biological activity. Refer to product-specific safety data and take necessary precautions.

All personnel are responsible for understanding and adhering to these safety considerations. Any safety concerns or incidents must be reported immediately to the supervisor and the Safety Officer.




### 8.0 Regulatory Compliance References

This Stability Testing Program is designed to comply with the following key regulatory guidelines and standards applicable to large animal pharmaceutical products. Adherence to these regulations ensures that products are safe, effective, and meet quality attributes throughout their shelf-life.

*   **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA):**
    *   **21 CFR Part 211 – Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation outlines the minimum cGMP requirements for the methods, facilities, and controls used in the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product. Subpart C, Buildings and Facilities, and Subpart I, Laboratory Controls, are particularly relevant to stability testing.
    *   **CVM GFI #73 (VICH GL3(R)) Stability Testing of New Veterinary Drug Substances and Medicinal Products:** This guidance provides recommendations regarding the stability data package for new veterinary drug substances and medicinal products to be included in a registration application submitted within the three regions of the European Union (EU), Japan, and the United States [1].
    *   **CVM GFI #259 (VICH GL58) Stability Testing of New Veterinary Drug Substances and Medicinal Products in Climatic Zones III and IV:** This guidance provides recommendations for stability testing in specific climatic zones.
    *   **CVM GFI #5 Drug Stability Guidelines:** Provides general guidance on drug stability.
    *   **VICH GL4 Stability Testing of New Veterinary Dosage Forms:** Provides guidance on stability testing for new dosage forms.
    *   **VICH GL5 Photostability Testing of New Veterinary Drug Substances and Medicinal Products:** Provides guidance on photostability testing [1].
    *   **VICH GL8 Stability Testing for Medicated Premixes:** Provides guidance on stability testing for medicated premixes.
    *   **VICH GL10(R) Impurities in New Veterinary Drug Substances:** Addresses impurities in new veterinary drug substances.
    *   **VICH GL17 Stability Testing of Biotechnological/Biological Veterinary Medicinal Products:** Provides guidance on stability testing for biotechnological/biological products.

*   **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA):**
    *   **9 CFR – Animals and Animal Products:** This regulation covers the requirements for veterinary biologics. Specifically, 9 CFR 114.13 requires the establishment of a dating period for every veterinary biological product in accordance with the stability of each of its fractions.
    *   **VS Memorandum (VSM) 800.127 Guidelines for Conducting Product Stability Studies:** This memorandum provides guidance for studies intended to support the stability criteria of biological products regulated by the USDA Center for Veterinary Biologics (CVB) [2].
    *   **VSM 800.300 Stability Testing of New Biotechnological/Biological Veterinary Medicinal Products:** Provides information and recommendations about the stability testing of veterinary medicinal products (both drugs and biologics).

*   **Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP):**
    *   cGMP regulations, as enforced by the FDA and USDA, require that stability testing be conducted to ensure that drug products meet quality standards throughout their shelf-life. This includes requirements for a written stability testing program, validated test methods, proper documentation, and ongoing monitoring.
    *   **ICH (International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use) Guidelines:** While primarily for human pharmaceuticals, many ICH guidelines, particularly those related to stability (e.g., Q1A(R2) Stability Testing of New Drug Substances and Products), are often referenced and considered best practices in the veterinary pharmaceutical industry, especially where VICH guidelines align or are derived from ICH principles.

This SOP incorporates the principles and requirements from these regulatory documents to ensure a robust and compliant Stability Testing Program for large animal pharmaceuticals.




### 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Changes to products, processes, equipment, or analytical methods can significantly impact the stability of large animal pharmaceutical products. Therefore, a robust change control system is essential to manage and assess the impact of such changes on product quality and stability. All changes must be formally documented, reviewed, and approved before implementation.

Key aspects of change control procedures related to stability testing include:

*   **Identification of Change:** Any proposed change that could potentially affect the identity, strength, quality, purity, or potency of a drug substance or medicinal product, or the reliability of stability data, must be identified and documented.

*   **Change Request Initiation:** A formal change request must be initiated, detailing:
    *   Description of the proposed change.
    *   Reason for the change.
    *   Scope of the change.
    *   Potential impact on product quality, safety, efficacy, and stability.
    *   Proposed implementation plan.

*   **Impact Assessment:** A cross-functional team, including representatives from Manufacturing, Quality Control, Quality Assurance, and Research & Development, must conduct a thorough impact assessment of the proposed change. This assessment should determine:
    *   Whether the change necessitates new stability studies (e.g., full stability studies, reduced studies, or comparative studies).
    *   The type and extent of stability data required to support the change.
    *   Any potential risks to product stability or shelf-life.
    *   The need for re-validation of analytical methods.

*   **Stability Study Requirements for Changes:**
    *   **Major Changes:** Changes to the formulation, manufacturing process (e.g., critical process parameters, equipment), container closure system, or site of manufacture typically require full stability studies on at least three production batches to demonstrate that the change does not adversely affect product stability.
    *   **Minor Changes:** Changes to excipient suppliers, minor process adjustments, or changes to analytical methods may require reduced stability studies or comparative stability data to confirm no adverse impact.
    *   **No Impact:** In some cases, a scientific justification may be provided to demonstrate that a change has no impact on product stability, and therefore, no additional stability studies are required. This justification must be thoroughly documented and approved by QA.

*   **Approval of Change Request:** The change request, along with the impact assessment and proposed stability studies (if any), must be reviewed and approved by relevant departmental heads and Quality Assurance. Approval must be obtained before the change is implemented.

*   **Implementation and Verification:** Once approved, the change is implemented. Any required stability studies are initiated and conducted according to approved protocols. The results of these studies are used to verify that the change has not negatively impacted product stability.

*   **Documentation:** All aspects of the change control process, including the change request, impact assessment, stability study protocols and reports, and approval records, must be meticulously documented and maintained as part of the product's quality history file.

*   **Regulatory Notification:** Depending on the nature and impact of the change, regulatory authorities (FDA, USDA) may need to be notified or a variation application submitted. The Regulatory Affairs department is responsible for determining and executing these notifications.

This change control procedure ensures that product stability is continuously maintained and verified throughout the product lifecycle, even when changes are introduced.
