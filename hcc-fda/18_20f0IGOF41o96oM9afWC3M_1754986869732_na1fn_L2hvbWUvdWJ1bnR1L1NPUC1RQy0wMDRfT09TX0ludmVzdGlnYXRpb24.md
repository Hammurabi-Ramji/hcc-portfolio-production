# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

## SOP-QC-004: Out-of-Specification Investigation Procedures

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 2025-12-08

## 1.0 Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the guidelines and procedures for the investigation of Out-of-Specification (OOS) test results obtained during the testing of raw materials, in-process materials, bulk drug substances, and finished drug products in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The purpose of this SOP is to ensure that all OOS results are thoroughly investigated to determine the root cause, implement appropriate corrective and preventive actions (CAPA), and prevent recurrence. This SOP applies to all personnel involved in quality control testing, manufacturing, quality assurance, and any other departments that may be impacted by or involved in OOS investigations within the large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing facility. This includes adherence to current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) as well as relevant regulations from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) pertaining to veterinary medicinal products.




## 2.0 Responsibilities

**2.1 Quality Control (QC) Department:**
*   Initiate OOS investigations upon identification of an OOS result.
*   Conduct initial laboratory investigations to identify potential analytical errors.
*   Ensure all testing and retesting are performed according to approved methods and documented accurately.
*   Provide analytical data and support to the investigation team.
*   Review and approve laboratory investigation reports.

**2.2 Quality Assurance (QA) Department:**
*   Oversee and approve all OOS investigations.
*   Ensure compliance with cGMP regulations, FDA, and USDA guidelines throughout the investigation process.
*   Approve investigation plans, reports, and CAPA plans.
*   Make final disposition decisions for affected materials or products.
*   Ensure timely closure of OOS investigations.

**2.3 Manufacturing Department:**
*   Provide process-related information and support during OOS investigations.
*   Assist in identifying potential manufacturing deviations or root causes.
*   Implement agreed-upon corrective and preventive actions related to manufacturing processes.

**2.4 Research and Development (R&D) / Technical Services:**
*   Provide technical expertise and product knowledge as required during complex OOS investigations.
*   Assist in evaluating the impact of OOS results on product quality and stability.

**2.5 All Personnel:**
*   Adhere to this SOP and all related procedures.
*   Report any deviations or potential OOS results immediately to their supervisor and the QC department.



## 3.0 Detailed Procedures

### 3.1 Initial Notification and Assessment

**3.1.1 Identification of OOS Result:**
*   Any analyst or personnel who obtains an OOS test result must immediately notify their supervisor and the Quality Control (QC) Manager. This notification should occur as soon as the OOS result is confirmed, typically after the initial test and any immediate re-checks performed as part of the analytical method's system suitability or control procedures.
*   The OOS result must be clearly documented in the laboratory notebook or electronic data system, including the date, time, test performed, specification limit, actual result, and initial assessment of the potential impact.

**3.1.2 Preliminary Laboratory Investigation (Phase I):**
*   The QC analyst, in conjunction with their supervisor, will initiate a preliminary laboratory investigation to determine if the OOS result is due to an assignable laboratory error. This phase is critical and must be completed promptly to minimize delays in product disposition.
*   **Review of Analytical Method and Equipment:**
    *   Verify that the correct analytical method was followed precisely as written. Check for any deviations from the SOP, such as incorrect reagent preparation, improper instrument calibration, or incorrect sample preparation.
    *   Inspect the instrument used for the test. Check calibration records, maintenance logs, and performance verification checks. Ensure the instrument is functioning within its validated parameters. Look for any signs of malfunction, such as power fluctuations, unusual noises, or error messages.
    *   Confirm that all reagents, standards, and volumetric glassware used were within their expiry dates and properly prepared and stored. Check certificates of analysis for reagents and standards to ensure they meet specifications.
    *   Review the analyst's technique. Observe the analyst performing the test if possible, or review their training records to ensure they are qualified to perform the specific test. Look for any inconsistencies in pipetting, weighing, or other critical steps.
*   **Review of Raw Data:**
    *   Examine all raw data, including chromatograms, spectra, printouts, and manual calculations. Look for anomalies such as unexpected peaks, baseline disturbances, integration errors, or transcription errors.
    *   Verify all calculations performed by the analyst. This includes dilution factors, concentration calculations, and final result computations.
*   **Sample Integrity:**
    *   Confirm that the sample was properly identified, stored, and handled. Check for any signs of contamination, degradation, or improper sampling techniques.
    *   Verify that the correct sample was tested and that there were no mix-ups.

**3.1.3 Decision Point for Phase I:**
*   Based on the preliminary laboratory investigation, a decision must be made:
    *   **If an assignable laboratory error is identified (e.g., instrument malfunction, analyst error, contaminated reagent):** The OOS result can be invalidated. The investigation should document the identified error, the corrective action taken (e.g., repair instrument, retrain analyst, prepare new reagent), and the retesting plan. The retesting must be performed by a different, qualified analyst using a newly prepared sample if the original sample was compromised, or a new aliquot from the original sample if the error was not sample-related. All retesting must be fully documented.
    *   **If no assignable laboratory error is identified:** The OOS result is considered valid, and a full-scale investigation (Phase II) must be initiated. This indicates that the OOS result is likely due to a manufacturing or raw material issue, or an inherent variability in the product or process.



### 3.2 Full-Scale Investigation (Phase II)

**3.2.1 Formation of Investigation Team:**
*   If no assignable laboratory error is found in Phase I, a cross-functional investigation team will be formed. This team typically includes representatives from QC, QA, Manufacturing, and potentially R&D or Technical Services, depending on the nature of the OOS.
*   The QA department will lead the investigation and ensure its impartiality and thoroughness.

**3.2.2 Investigation Plan Development:**
*   The investigation team will develop a comprehensive investigation plan. This plan should outline:
    *   The scope of the investigation.
    *   The potential root causes to be investigated (e.g., raw material issues, manufacturing process deviations, equipment malfunction, analytical method issues).
    *   Specific tests to be performed (e.g., retesting, re-sampling, additional analytical tests, in-process checks).
    *   Samples to be tested (e.g., retained samples, new batches, stability samples).
    *   Personnel responsible for each action.
    *   A timeline for completion.

**3.2.3 Manufacturing Process Review:**
*   The manufacturing department, in conjunction with QA, will conduct a thorough review of the manufacturing process for the affected batch(es). This includes:
    *   Reviewing batch records for any deviations, anomalies, or out-of-trend data.
    *   Checking equipment logs for malfunctions, maintenance issues, or calibration failures during the production of the affected batch.
    *   Reviewing raw material certificates of analysis and incoming inspection records for the materials used in the affected batch.
    *   Interviewing operators and supervisors involved in the manufacturing process to gather insights and identify potential issues.
    *   Assessing environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, etc.) during manufacturing and storage.

**3.2.4 Additional Laboratory Testing and Evaluation:**
*   Based on the investigation plan, additional laboratory testing may be performed. This could include:
    *   **Retesting:** Retesting should be performed on the original sample, if sufficient and untainted, or on a new, representative sample from the same batch. The number of retests should be scientifically justified and documented. Retesting should not be used to 


retest into compliance. All retest results, whether within or outside specification, must be documented and considered in the investigation.
    *   **Resampling:** If the original sample is deemed compromised or insufficient, resampling from the same batch may be necessary. Resampling must be justified and performed according to a documented procedure, ensuring the new sample is representative of the batch.
    *   **Additional Tests:** Other analytical tests not initially performed may be conducted to gain further insight into the OOS result or to confirm a suspected root cause.
    *   **Reference Standard Verification:** Verify the integrity and validity of reference standards used in the testing.

**3.2.5 Root Cause Analysis:**
*   Once all relevant data has been collected, the investigation team will conduct a thorough root cause analysis using appropriate tools (e.g., Fishbone Diagram, 5 Whys, Fault Tree Analysis). The goal is to identify the fundamental reason for the OOS result, not just the symptom.
*   Consider all potential factors: personnel, equipment, materials, methods, environment, and management systems.

**3.2.6 Impact Assessment:**
*   Assess the impact of the OOS result on the quality, safety, and efficacy of the affected batch(es) and potentially other batches (e.g., those manufactured immediately before or after, or using the same raw material lot).
*   Determine if the OOS result indicates a systemic issue that could affect other products or processes.

**3.2.7 Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA):**
*   Based on the root cause analysis and impact assessment, develop a CAPA plan. This plan should include:
    *   **Corrective Actions:** Actions taken to eliminate the identified root cause and prevent recurrence of the OOS result.
    *   **Preventive Actions:** Actions taken to prevent similar OOS results from occurring in the future across different products or processes.
    *   **Effectiveness Checks:** A plan to verify the effectiveness of the implemented CAPA over time.

**3.2.8 Investigation Report:**
*   A comprehensive OOS investigation report must be prepared. The report should include:
    *   SOP number and title.
    *   Product name, batch number, and test parameter.
    *   Summary of the OOS result and initial investigation.
    *   Detailed description of the full-scale investigation, including all data reviewed and tests performed.
    *   Root cause identified.
    *   Impact assessment.
    *   Conclusion regarding the disposition of the affected material/product.
    *   Proposed CAPA plan with assigned responsibilities and timelines.
    *   Signatures of all relevant personnel (QC, QA, Manufacturing, etc.).

**3.2.9 Disposition of Affected Material/Product:**
*   Based on the investigation findings and impact assessment, the QA department will make a final disposition decision for the affected material or product (e.g., release, reprocess, reject, quarantine).
*   This decision must be scientifically sound and documented.



## 4.0 Documentation Requirements

All aspects of the Out-of-Specification (OOS) investigation, from initial notification to final disposition and CAPA implementation, must be thoroughly documented. This documentation serves as a critical record for regulatory compliance, internal audits, and continuous improvement.

**4.1 Laboratory Records:**
*   **Initial OOS Notification:** Document the date, time, analyst, test performed, specification limits, OOS result, and initial assessment in the laboratory notebook or electronic laboratory information management system (LIMS).
*   **Preliminary Laboratory Investigation (Phase I) Records:** Detailed records of all steps taken during Phase I, including:
    *   Review of analytical method, instrument calibration, and maintenance logs.
    *   Verification of reagents, standards, and glassware.
    *   Review of raw data (chromatograms, spectra, printouts, calculations).
    *   Observations of analyst technique.
    *   Any identified assignable laboratory errors and corrective actions taken.
    *   Results of any retesting performed during Phase I, including justification for retesting and documentation of all results (both within and outside specification).

**4.2 OOS Investigation File:**
*   A dedicated OOS investigation file must be created for each OOS event. This file will serve as a central repository for all documentation related to the investigation.
*   **Investigation Plan:** A copy of the approved investigation plan, detailing the scope, potential root causes, tests to be performed, responsibilities, and timelines.
*   **Manufacturing Records Review:** Documentation of all manufacturing batch records reviewed, including any identified deviations, anomalies, or relevant process parameters.
*   **Raw Material Records:** Copies of Certificates of Analysis (CoA) for raw materials used in the affected batch, and any relevant incoming inspection records.
*   **Additional Testing Results:** All results from additional laboratory testing (retesting, resampling, other analytical tests) performed during Phase II, with clear identification of samples and methods used.
*   **Root Cause Analysis Documentation:** Records of the root cause analysis methodology used (e.g., Fishbone Diagram, 5 Whys) and the identified root cause(s).
*   **Impact Assessment Documentation:** Records of the assessment of the OOS result's impact on product quality, safety, and efficacy, and potential impact on other batches.
*   **CAPA Plan:** The approved Corrective and Preventive Action plan, including assigned responsibilities, timelines, and effectiveness check criteria.
*   **Communication Records:** Records of all internal and external communications related to the OOS investigation (e.g., emails, meeting minutes).

**4.3 OOS Investigation Report:**
*   The final, comprehensive OOS investigation report must be formally documented and approved. This report should summarize all findings and conclusions as detailed in Section 3.2.8.

**4.4 Disposition Records:**
*   Documentation of the final disposition decision for the affected material or product, including the rationale for the decision and any associated material movements (e.g., release, quarantine, destruction).

**4.5 Version Control:**
*   All OOS investigation documents, including the SOP itself, investigation plans, and reports, must be subject to strict version control. Each revision must be clearly identified with a version number, effective date, and a summary of changes.

**4.6 Record Retention:**
*   All OOS investigation records must be retained according to the company's record retention policy and regulatory requirements (e.g., FDA 21 CFR Part 211.180, USDA regulations).



## 5.0 Training Requirements

All personnel involved in the execution, review, or approval of Out-of-Specification (OOS) investigations must receive comprehensive training on this SOP and related procedures. Effective training ensures consistent application of the OOS investigation process and compliance with regulatory expectations.

**5.1 Initial Training:**
*   All new employees whose roles involve OOS investigations (e.g., QC analysts, QA specialists, manufacturing supervisors) must complete initial training on this SOP before performing any related tasks.
*   Training should cover:
    *   The definition of an OOS result and its significance.
    *   The step-by-step OOS investigation process (Phase I and Phase II).
    *   Documentation requirements for OOS investigations.
    *   Roles and responsibilities of different departments and personnel.
    *   Understanding of root cause analysis methodologies.
    *   Introduction to relevant regulatory guidelines (FDA, USDA, cGMP) pertaining to OOS investigations.

**5.2 Refresher Training:**
*   Periodic refresher training must be conducted at a defined frequency (e.g., annually or biennially) to ensure continued understanding and compliance with this SOP.
*   Refresher training should also be provided whenever there are significant revisions to this SOP, changes in regulatory requirements, or identified deficiencies in the OOS investigation process.

**5.3 Training Records:**
*   All training conducted must be thoroughly documented. Training records should include:
    *   The name of the trainee.
    *   The date of training.
    *   The title and version of the SOP or training material.
    *   The name of the trainer.
    *   Evidence of understanding (e.g., quiz results, practical demonstration).
*   Training records must be maintained by the Human Resources or Training Department and be readily available for audit and inspection.

**5.4 Competency Assessment:**
*   Beyond formal training, the competency of personnel involved in OOS investigations should be periodically assessed. This can include:
    *   Direct observation of work performance.
    *   Review of completed OOS investigation reports.
    *   Participation in mock OOS investigations or case studies.
*   Any identified competency gaps should be addressed through additional training or coaching.



## 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Effective quality control measures are integral to minimizing the occurrence of Out-of-Specification (OOS) results and ensuring the reliability of laboratory data. These measures extend beyond the immediate OOS investigation to encompass proactive steps in the quality system.

**6.1 Laboratory System Controls:**
*   **Instrument Calibration and Maintenance:** All laboratory instruments used for testing must be regularly calibrated, qualified, and maintained according to established schedules and procedures. Calibration records and maintenance logs must be meticulously kept and readily available.
*   **Reagent and Standard Management:** All reagents, standards, and media must be properly labeled, stored, and used within their expiry dates. Certificates of Analysis for all critical reagents and reference standards must be reviewed and approved upon receipt.
*   **Method Validation and Verification:** All analytical methods used for testing must be validated or verified to ensure they are suitable for their intended purpose, demonstrating accuracy, precision, specificity, linearity, and robustness. Any changes to validated methods must undergo proper change control and revalidation.
*   **System Suitability Tests (SST):** Analytical methods should include appropriate System Suitability Tests (SSTs) to ensure the analytical system is functioning correctly at the time of analysis. Failure to meet SST criteria should prevent the release of results and trigger immediate investigation.
*   **Control Samples:** Regular analysis of quality control (QC) samples, proficiency testing samples, and spiked samples should be performed to monitor the performance of analytical methods and the competency of analysts.

**6.2 Data Integrity:**
*   Ensure the integrity of all laboratory data, whether generated manually or electronically. This includes adherence to ALCOA+ principles (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate, Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available).
*   Electronic data systems must have appropriate controls for audit trails, access security, and data backup.

**6.3 Trend Analysis:**
*   Regularly perform trend analysis of OOS results to identify recurring issues, specific products, tests, or manufacturing processes that frequently yield OOS results. This proactive approach can help identify systemic problems and drive continuous improvement.
*   Trend analysis should also include Out-of-Trend (OOT) results, which, while not OOS, may indicate a shift in process or product performance that warrants investigation.

**6.4 Internal Audits and Self-Inspections:**
*   Conduct periodic internal audits and self-inspections of the QC laboratory and manufacturing operations to assess compliance with cGMP, this SOP, and other relevant quality system documents. Audit findings should be used to identify areas for improvement and initiate CAPA.

**6.5 Supplier Qualification:**
*   Ensure that all suppliers of raw materials, components, and critical services are qualified and regularly audited to ensure the quality and consistency of incoming materials, thereby reducing the likelihood of OOS results originating from supplier issues.



## 7.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all pharmaceutical operations, including the investigation of Out-of-Specification (OOS) results. Personnel involved in OOS investigations must adhere to all applicable safety regulations and company safety policies to prevent accidents, injuries, and exposure to hazardous materials.

**7.1 General Laboratory Safety:**
*   All laboratory personnel must follow standard laboratory safety practices, including wearing appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) such as lab coats, safety glasses, and gloves, as required by the specific test or material being handled.
*   Familiarity with the location and proper use of safety equipment, including eyewash stations, safety showers, and fire extinguishers, is mandatory.
*   Proper handling and disposal of chemical and biological waste must be strictly followed according to established procedures.

**7.2 Handling of Hazardous Materials:**
*   When investigating OOS results involving hazardous raw materials, in-process materials, or finished products, personnel must consult the Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for each substance. The SDS provides critical information on potential hazards, safe handling procedures, emergency measures, and disposal requirements.
*   Work with highly potent or toxic substances must be conducted in designated areas (e.g., fume hoods, isolators) with appropriate engineering controls to minimize exposure.

**7.3 Equipment Safety:**
*   Ensure that all laboratory equipment used during OOS investigations is in good working order and has undergone regular safety checks and maintenance. Do not operate faulty equipment.
*   Follow manufacturer guidelines and internal SOPs for the safe operation of all instruments.

**7.4 Emergency Procedures:**
*   All personnel involved in OOS investigations must be familiar with emergency procedures, including spill response, fire evacuation plans, and first aid protocols.
*   Report any safety incidents, near misses, or unsafe conditions immediately to their supervisor and the Safety Officer.

**7.5 Biosafety (for large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing):**
*   Given the context of large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing, specific biosafety considerations may apply, especially when dealing with biological samples or products. Personnel must adhere to established biosafety levels (BSL) and practices relevant to the agents being handled.
*   Proper disinfection and sterilization procedures must be followed for equipment and work surfaces that come into contact with biological materials.
*   Vaccination protocols, where applicable, should be followed for personnel working with specific animal pathogens.



## 8.0 Regulatory Compliance References

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Out-of-Specification (OOS) investigations is developed in accordance with, and references, the following key regulatory guidelines and standards applicable to pharmaceutical manufacturing, particularly for large animal pharmaceuticals:

**8.1 U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Regulations and Guidance:**
*   **21 CFR Part 211 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation outlines the minimum cGMP requirements for the methods, facilities, and controls used in the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product. Several sections are particularly relevant to OOS investigations:
    *   **21 CFR 211.160(b):** Requires the establishment of scientifically sound and appropriate specifications, standards, sampling plans, and test procedures designed to assure that components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, and drug products conform to appropriate standards of identity, strength, quality, and purity.
    *   **21 CFR 211.165(f):** Mandates that any drug product failing to meet established specifications must be rejected unless investigation demonstrates that the drug product meets appropriate standards of quality and purity and the failure to meet specifications is not indicative of a systemic problem.
    *   **21 CFR 211.192: Production Record Review:** Requires a thorough investigation of any unexplained discrepancy or the failure of a batch or any of its components to meet any of its specifications, whether or not the batch has already been distributed. Such investigation shall extend to other batches of the same drug product and other drug products that may have been associated with the specific failure or discrepancy.
*   **Guidance for Industry: Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production (FDA, 2006, updated 2022):** This guidance provides the FDA's current thinking on how to evaluate OOS test results. It details the responsibilities of laboratory personnel, the importance of a two-phase investigation (laboratory and full-scale), root cause analysis, retesting, resampling, and the documentation requirements for OOS investigations [1].
*   **FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM):** CVM is responsible for regulating animal drugs, feeds, and veterinary devices. Regulations and guidance specific to veterinary medicinal products should be consulted, particularly regarding product quality and manufacturing controls.

**8.2 U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) Regulations:**
*   **9 CFR - Animals and Animal Products:** The USDA, particularly through its Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS), regulates certain animal biological products (e.g., vaccines, bacterins, diagnostic kits) to ensure their purity, safety, potency, and efficacy. While FDA primarily regulates animal drugs, there can be overlapping jurisdiction or specific USDA requirements for certain biologicals used in large animals. Relevant sections may include those pertaining to:
    *   **9 CFR Part 101-123:** Regulations governing the preparation, testing, and distribution of veterinary biological products.
    *   **USDA APHIS Veterinary Services Memoranda (VSMs):** These memoranda provide specific guidance and policy interpretations related to the regulation of veterinary biological products.

**8.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP):**
*   Beyond specific FDA and USDA regulations, adherence to general cGMP principles is fundamental. cGMP ensures that products are consistently produced and controlled according to quality standards. Key cGMP principles relevant to OOS investigations include:
    *   **Quality Management System:** A robust quality system that includes procedures for handling deviations, investigations, and CAPA.
    *   **Personnel:** Qualified and trained personnel to perform testing and investigations.
    *   **Facilities and Equipment:** Suitable facilities and properly maintained and calibrated equipment.
    *   **Documentation:** Comprehensive and accurate documentation of all activities.
    *   **Change Control:** A system for managing changes to processes, equipment, and methods.

## 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Any changes to this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), or to any processes, equipment, or analytical methods that could impact the Out-of-Specification (OOS) investigation process, must be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are evaluated, approved, implemented, and documented in a controlled manner to maintain product quality and regulatory compliance.

**9.1 Scope of Change Control:**
*   Changes to this SOP (e.g., revisions to procedures, responsibilities, or documentation requirements).
*   Changes to analytical methods used for testing raw materials, in-process materials, or finished products.
*   Changes to laboratory equipment or manufacturing equipment that could affect product quality or test results.
*   Changes to raw material specifications or manufacturing processes.
*   Changes to software or electronic systems used in the laboratory or manufacturing.

**9.2 Change Control Process:**
*   **Initiation:** Any proposed change must be formally initiated by completing a Change Request Form. This form should clearly describe the proposed change, the reason for the change, and its potential impact.
*   **Assessment:** The Change Request will be assessed by a cross-functional team, typically including representatives from QA, QC, Manufacturing, Engineering, and Regulatory Affairs. The assessment will evaluate the potential impact of the change on product quality, safety, efficacy, regulatory compliance, and validated states.
*   **Approval:** Based on the assessment, the change will be approved or rejected by authorized personnel. Major changes may require multiple levels of approval.
*   **Implementation:** Once approved, the change will be implemented according to a detailed implementation plan. This may include:
    *   Updating relevant documentation (SOPs, batch records, specifications).
    *   Retraining personnel.
    *   Requalification or revalidation of equipment or processes.
    *   Performing additional testing to confirm the impact of the change.
*   **Verification of Effectiveness:** After implementation, the effectiveness of the change must be verified to ensure that it has achieved its intended outcome and has not introduced any new undesirable effects. This may involve monitoring data, conducting audits, or performing specific tests.
*   **Closure:** Once the change has been successfully implemented and its effectiveness verified, the Change Request will be formally closed.

**9.3 Documentation:**
*   All change control activities, from initiation to closure, must be thoroughly documented. This includes the Change Request Form, assessment records, approval signatures, implementation plans, and verification of effectiveness reports.
*   The change control documentation must be retained according to the company's record retention policy.

**9.4 Training:**
*   Personnel affected by a change must be trained on the revised procedures or new systems before the change is implemented. Training records must be maintained as per Section 5.0.
