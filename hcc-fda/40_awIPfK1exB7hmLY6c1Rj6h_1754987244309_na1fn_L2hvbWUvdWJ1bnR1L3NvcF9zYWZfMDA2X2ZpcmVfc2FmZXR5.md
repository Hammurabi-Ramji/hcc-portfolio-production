# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

## SOP-SAF-006: Fire Safety and Prevention in Manufacturing Areas

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 2025-12-08

## 1.0 Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the procedures and guidelines for fire safety and prevention within the manufacturing areas of our large animal pharmaceutical facility. The purpose of this SOP is to establish a comprehensive framework to minimize the risk of fire, ensure the safety of personnel and animals, protect facility assets, and maintain compliance with all applicable regulatory requirements, including those set forth by the FDA, USDA, and cGMP guidelines. This SOP applies to all personnel, contractors, and visitors working within or accessing the manufacturing areas, including but not limited to production, packaging, warehousing, and quality control laboratories where pharmaceutical products for large animals are manufactured, processed, or stored. It covers fire prevention measures, emergency response protocols, and post-fire incident procedures.

## 2.0 Responsibilities

**2.1 Management:**
*   Ensure adequate resources are allocated for the implementation and maintenance of fire safety programs.
*   Provide leadership and support for fire safety initiatives.
*   Review and approve this SOP and any subsequent revisions.
*   Ensure compliance with all relevant fire safety regulations and standards.

**2.2 Fire Safety Officer (FSO):**
*   Develop, implement, and maintain the facility's fire safety program.
*   Conduct regular fire risk assessments and recommend corrective actions.
*   Coordinate fire safety training for all personnel.
*   Ensure all fire detection, suppression, and alarm systems are regularly inspected, tested, and maintained.
*   Serve as the primary contact for external fire department personnel during emergencies.
*   Investigate all fire incidents and near-misses, and prepare detailed reports.

**2.3 Department Heads/Supervisors:**
*   Ensure all personnel under their supervision are trained on and adhere to this SOP.
*   Conduct regular departmental fire safety inspections and address any identified hazards.
*   Ensure proper storage and handling of flammable materials within their respective areas.
*   Lead and account for personnel during fire drills and actual evacuations.

**2.4 All Employees:**
*   Adhere to all fire safety policies and procedures outlined in this SOP.
*   Participate in all required fire safety training and drills.
*   Report any fire hazards, faulty equipment, or unsafe conditions immediately to their supervisor or the FSO.
*   Know the location of fire exits, fire extinguishers, and alarm pull stations.
*   Understand and follow evacuation procedures.

**2.5 Contractors and Visitors:**
*   Comply with all fire safety regulations and procedures while on site.
*   Be aware of emergency exits and assembly points.
*   Follow instructions from facility personnel during emergencies.



## 3.0 Detailed Procedures

**3.1 Fire Prevention Measures:**

**3.1.1 Housekeeping and Storage:**
*   Maintain clean and orderly manufacturing areas, free from clutter and combustible waste. Regularly remove trash, dust, and debris that could fuel a fire. Ensure all waste containers are emptied regularly and are made of non-combustible materials [1].
*   Store flammable and combustible materials in approved, clearly labeled containers and cabinets, away from ignition sources and in designated storage areas. Segregate incompatible materials to prevent hazardous reactions [2].
*   Ensure proper ventilation in areas where flammable liquids or gases are used or stored to prevent the accumulation of hazardous vapors. Ventilation systems must be regularly inspected and maintained [3].
*   Keep aisles and exits clear and unobstructed at all times to ensure safe evacuation in an emergency. Mark exit routes clearly and ensure emergency lighting is functional [4].
*   Control the accumulation of dust, especially in areas where combustible dusts (e.g., feed ingredients, powdered pharmaceuticals) are present. Implement regular cleaning schedules and use appropriate dust collection systems to prevent dust explosions [5].

**3.1.2 Ignition Source Control:**
*   Prohibit open flames, smoking, and unauthorized heat-producing equipment in manufacturing areas. Implement a strict 'Hot Work' permit system for any operations involving welding, cutting, grinding, or other spark-producing activities. Ensure fire watches are in place during hot work operations [6].
*   Regularly inspect and maintain all electrical equipment, wiring, and outlets to prevent shorts, overloads, and other electrical hazards. Ensure all equipment is properly grounded and that extension cords are not used as permanent wiring [7].
*   Control static electricity buildup, especially in areas where flammable liquids or powders are handled. Implement grounding and bonding procedures for containers and equipment, and consider using static-dissipative flooring and footwear [8].
*   Ensure heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems are regularly inspected and maintained to prevent overheating and ensure proper airflow. Filters should be cleaned or replaced as per manufacturer guidelines [9].

**3.1.3 Fire Detection and Suppression Systems:**
*   Ensure all fire detection systems (smoke detectors, heat detectors, flame detectors) are fully operational and regularly tested as per manufacturer specifications and regulatory requirements. Maintain a schedule for calibration and maintenance [10].
*   Verify that automatic fire suppression systems (e.g., sprinklers, clean agent systems) are in good working order and undergo routine inspections and testing by qualified personnel. Ensure water supply to sprinkler systems is adequate and unobstructed [11].
*   Ensure fire extinguishers are readily accessible, properly charged, and of the appropriate type for the potential fire hazards in the area (e.g., Class A for ordinary combustibles, Class B for flammable liquids, Class C for electrical fires). Conduct monthly visual inspections and annual professional servicing [12].
*   Maintain clear access to fire alarm pull stations and ensure all personnel know their locations and how to activate them. Test alarm systems regularly to ensure functionality [13].

**3.2 Emergency Response Procedures:**

**3.2.1 Discovering a Fire:**
*   **R.A.C.E. Protocol:**
    *   **R**escue: Rescue anyone in immediate danger, if it can be done safely. Do not attempt to rescue if it puts your life at risk [14].
    *   **A**larm: Activate the nearest fire alarm pull station immediately. Shout 


“Fire! Fire!” to alert others.
    *   **C**ontain: Close doors and windows to contain the fire and smoke, if safe to do so. This helps prevent the spread of fire and smoke [15].
    *   **E**xtinguish/Evacuate: Attempt to extinguish the fire with a suitable fire extinguisher only if the fire is small, you are trained, and you can do so safely without putting yourself or others at risk. If the fire is large or spreading, or if you are unsure, evacuate immediately [16].

**3.2.2 Evacuation Procedures:**
*   Upon hearing the fire alarm, all personnel must immediately stop work and proceed to the nearest emergency exit. Do not use elevators during a fire emergency [17].
*   Follow designated evacuation routes to the primary assembly point. If the primary assembly point is unsafe or inaccessible, proceed to the secondary assembly point. Assembly points will be clearly marked and communicated during training [18].
*   Department heads or their designees must conduct a headcount of all personnel from their respective departments at the assembly point. Report any missing personnel to the Fire Safety Officer or emergency responders [19].
*   Do not re-enter the building until the Fire Safety Officer or emergency services personnel have declared it safe to do so [20].

**3.2.3 Interaction with Emergency Services:**
*   The Fire Safety Officer (FSO) will meet the fire department upon their arrival and provide them with critical information, including the location of the fire, type of materials involved, and any missing personnel [21].
*   All personnel must cooperate fully with emergency services personnel and follow their instructions [22].

**3.3 Post-Incident Procedures:**
*   After a fire incident, the Fire Safety Officer will lead an investigation to determine the cause of the fire, assess damages, and identify corrective and preventive actions. All personnel involved will cooperate with the investigation [23].
*   A detailed incident report will be prepared, documenting the fire, response, and any lessons learned. This report will be shared with relevant management and used for continuous improvement of fire safety protocols [24].
*   Damaged areas will be secured and assessed by qualified personnel before any re-entry or resumption of operations. Necessary repairs and clean-up will be conducted in accordance with safety guidelines [25].

## 4.0 Documentation Requirements

All fire safety-related activities, including inspections, maintenance, training, drills, and incidents, must be thoroughly documented. This documentation is crucial for demonstrating compliance with regulatory requirements and for continuous improvement of fire safety programs. All records must be legible, accurate, and readily retrievable.

**4.1 Inspection and Maintenance Records:**
*   **Fire Extinguisher Inspection Logs:** Monthly visual inspection records and annual professional servicing certificates.
*   **Fire Alarm System Test Records:** Documentation of regular testing and maintenance of fire alarm panels, smoke detectors, heat detectors, and pull stations.
*   **Sprinkler System Inspection and Test Records:** Records of quarterly, annual, and five-year inspections and tests of sprinkler systems, including water flow tests and backflow prevention device tests.
*   **Emergency Lighting and Exit Signage Logs:** Records of monthly and annual tests of emergency lighting and exit signs.
*   **Hot Work Permits:** Copies of all issued hot work permits, including details of the work performed, precautions taken, and fire watch personnel.
*   **Ventilation System Maintenance Logs:** Records of inspection and maintenance of HVAC and dust collection systems.

**4.2 Training Records:**
*   **Fire Safety Training Attendance Records:** Documentation of all personnel attending fire safety training sessions, including dates, topics covered, and trainer information.
*   **Fire Drill Participation Records:** Records of employee participation in fire drills, including evacuation times and any identified areas for improvement.

**4.3 Incident Reports:**
*   **Fire Incident Reports:** Detailed reports for all fire incidents, including date, time, location, cause, extent of damage, actions taken, and injuries (if any). These reports should include photographs and witness statements where applicable.
*   **Near-Miss Reports:** Documentation of all near-miss incidents related to fire safety, including potential causes and preventive actions taken.

**4.4 Regulatory Compliance Documentation:**
*   **Risk Assessment Reports:** Regular fire risk assessment reports identifying potential hazards and mitigation strategies.
*   **Emergency Action Plans (EAP):** Current and approved Emergency Action Plans, including evacuation routes, assembly points, and emergency contact information.
*   **Regulatory Audit Reports:** Records of any audits or inspections conducted by regulatory bodies (e.g., FDA, OSHA, local fire department) related to fire safety, including findings and corrective actions.

**4.5 SOP Review and Revision History:**
*   Maintain a record of all revisions to this SOP, including version numbers, dates of revision, summaries of changes, and approval signatures.

## 5.0 Training Requirements

All personnel working in or accessing manufacturing areas must receive comprehensive fire safety training. Training will be conducted upon initial employment and periodically thereafter to ensure all employees are knowledgeable about fire prevention, emergency procedures, and the proper use of fire safety equipment.

**5.1 Initial Training:**
*   All new employees must complete a mandatory fire safety orientation within their first week of employment. This training will cover:
    *   Overview of this SOP, including purpose, scope, and responsibilities.
    *   Identification of fire hazards specific to pharmaceutical manufacturing areas.
    *   Location and proper use of fire extinguishers, fire alarms, and emergency exits.
    *   Evacuation routes and assembly points.
    *   R.A.E.C.E. protocol (Rescue, Alarm, Contain, Extinguish/Evacuate).
    *   Reporting procedures for fire hazards and incidents.

**5.2 Annual Refresher Training:**
*   All employees must attend annual refresher training sessions to reinforce fire safety knowledge and address any updates to procedures or regulations. This training will include:
    *   Review of key fire safety principles and procedures.
    *   Practical demonstrations on the use of fire extinguishers.
    *   Discussion of recent fire incidents or near-misses and lessons learned.
    *   Updates on new fire safety technologies or regulatory changes.

**5.3 Specialized Training:**
*   Personnel designated as Fire Safety Wardens or members of the Emergency Response Team will receive specialized training on advanced fire suppression techniques, first aid, and coordination with external emergency services.
*   Personnel involved in hot work operations will receive specific training on hot work permit procedures and fire watch responsibilities.

**5.4 Training Documentation:**
*   All fire safety training sessions, including initial, refresher, and specialized training, will be documented. Records will include:
    *   Date and duration of training.
    *   Topics covered.
    *   Names and signatures of attendees.
    *   Name and qualifications of the trainer.
    *   Training materials used.

## 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures for fire safety ensure that all fire prevention and protection systems are maintained in optimal working condition and that procedures are consistently followed to minimize fire risks. These measures are integral to maintaining a safe manufacturing environment and ensuring product quality.

**6.1 Regular Inspections and Audits:**
*   **Internal Audits:** Conduct regular internal audits of fire safety procedures and systems to ensure compliance with this SOP and regulatory requirements. These audits will be performed by qualified internal personnel or a third-party auditor.
*   **External Inspections:** Cooperate with and facilitate inspections by external regulatory bodies (e.g., local fire department, OSHA, FDA) and address any findings promptly.
*   **Equipment Calibration and Maintenance:** Ensure all fire detection and suppression equipment is calibrated and maintained according to manufacturer specifications and a predetermined schedule. This includes fire alarms, smoke detectors, sprinkler systems, and fire extinguishers.

**6.2 Performance Monitoring:**
*   **Fire Drill Performance:** Monitor the effectiveness of fire drills by tracking evacuation times, identifying bottlenecks, and assessing personnel's understanding of procedures. Use these observations to refine evacuation plans and training programs.
*   **Incident Rate Analysis:** Analyze fire incident and near-miss data to identify trends, root causes, and areas requiring additional attention or corrective actions. This data will be used for continuous improvement.

**6.3 Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA):**
*   Implement a robust CAPA system for all identified fire safety deficiencies, non-conformances, or incidents. This system will ensure that root causes are identified, corrective actions are implemented, and their effectiveness is verified.
*   Preventive actions will be proactively identified and implemented based on risk assessments, industry best practices, and lessons learned from incidents.

**6.4 Management Review:**
*   Fire safety performance and the effectiveness of the fire safety program will be reviewed by management at least annually. This review will include:
    *   Review of audit findings and CAPA status.
    *   Analysis of incident and near-miss data.
    *   Assessment of training effectiveness.
    *   Evaluation of compliance with regulatory requirements.
    *   Allocation of resources for fire safety improvements.

## 7.0 Safety Considerations

Fire safety in a large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing facility presents unique safety considerations due to the presence of animals, specialized equipment, and potentially hazardous materials. All safety measures must prioritize the well-being of personnel and animals.

**7.1 Personnel Safety:**
*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Ensure appropriate PPE, such as fire-resistant clothing, safety glasses, and respirators, is available and used when handling flammable or hazardous materials, or during emergency response activities.
*   **Emergency Showers and Eyewash Stations:** Ensure these facilities are readily accessible and functional in areas where hazardous chemicals are used or stored.
*   **Confined Space Entry:** Implement strict procedures for confined space entry, as these areas can pose increased fire and explosion risks due to limited ventilation and potential accumulation of flammable gases or dusts.
*   **Lockout/Tagout (LOTO):** Implement LOTO procedures for equipment maintenance to prevent accidental startup and potential ignition sources.

**7.2 Animal Safety:**
*   **Evacuation of Animals:** Develop and regularly practice specific procedures for the safe evacuation of large animals during a fire emergency. This includes designated animal assembly points and methods for animal handling and transport.
*   **Ventilation in Animal Housing:** Ensure adequate ventilation in animal housing areas to prevent the buildup of flammable gases (e.g., methane from animal waste) and to remove smoke in case of fire.
*   **Emergency Animal Care:** Establish protocols for providing emergency care to animals affected by smoke inhalation or injuries during a fire incident.

**7.3 Material Handling Safety:**
*   **Flammable and Combustible Liquids:** Adhere to strict guidelines for the storage, handling, and dispensing of flammable and combustible liquids, including proper labeling, grounding, and bonding.
*   **Combustible Dusts:** Implement comprehensive dust control programs to prevent dust explosions, including regular cleaning, proper ventilation, and use of explosion-proof equipment.
*   **Oxidizers:** Store oxidizers separately from flammable and combustible materials to prevent exacerbation of fires.

## 8.0 Regulatory Compliance References

This SOP is developed in accordance with, and aims to ensure compliance with, the following regulatory bodies and guidelines relevant to pharmaceutical manufacturing and fire safety:

**8.1 U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA):**
*   **21 CFR Part 211 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation outlines the minimum current good manufacturing practice for the preparation of drug products for administration to animals or humans. While not directly fire safety, it mandates adequate facilities and controls to prevent contamination and ensure product quality, which indirectly relates to facility safety and integrity [26].
*   **21 CFR Part 210 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Processing, Packing, or Holding of Drugs; General:** This part covers general provisions for cGMP, emphasizing facility design and maintenance to prevent adulteration and ensure safety [27].

**8.2 U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA):**
*   The USDA primarily regulates animal health and welfare. While they do not have specific fire safety regulations for pharmaceutical manufacturing facilities, their oversight of animal care and handling necessitates safe environments for animals, which includes fire safety considerations. Compliance with USDA standards for animal welfare indirectly supports robust fire safety protocols in facilities housing animals for pharmaceutical production [28].

**8.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP):**
*   cGMP regulations are enforced by the FDA and ensure that drug products are consistently produced and controlled according to quality standards. Key aspects relevant to fire safety include:
    *   **Facility Design and Construction:** Facilities must be designed to prevent contamination and facilitate cleaning, maintenance, and proper operations, which includes considerations for fire-resistant materials and safe layouts [29].
    *   **Maintenance:** Equipment and facilities must be maintained in a good state of repair to prevent malfunctions that could lead to fire hazards [30].
    *   **Environmental Controls:** Proper ventilation and environmental controls are essential to prevent the accumulation of hazardous substances that could contribute to fire [31].

**8.4 Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA):**
*   **29 CFR 1910 Subpart L - Fire Protection:** This subpart provides general industry standards for fire protection, including fire brigades, fire prevention plans, portable fire extinguishers, and fixed fire suppression systems [32].
*   **29 CFR 1910.106 - Flammable and Combustible Liquids:** Specific requirements for the storage, handling, and use of flammable and combustible liquids [33].
*   **29 CFR 1910.303 - General Requirements (Electrical):** Standards for electrical wiring and equipment to prevent electrical fires [34].
*   **29 CFR 1910.1200 - Hazard Communication:** Requires employers to provide information to employees about hazardous chemicals to which they may be exposed, including fire hazards [35].

**8.5 National Fire Protection Association (NFPA):**
*   While not a regulatory body, NFPA codes and standards are widely adopted and referenced by regulatory agencies and are considered best practices for fire safety. Relevant NFPA standards include:
    *   **NFPA 1: Fire Code:** Provides comprehensive requirements for fire prevention and protection [36].
    *   **NFPA 10: Standard for Portable Fire Extinguishers:** Guidelines for the selection, installation, inspection, maintenance, and testing of portable fire extinguishers [37].
    *   **NFPA 13: Standard for the Installation of Sprinkler Systems:** Requirements for the design and installation of automatic sprinkler systems [38].
    *   **NFPA 70: National Electrical Code (NEC):** Standards for safe electrical installation [39].
    *   **NFPA 72: National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code:** Requirements for fire alarm systems [40].
    *   **NFPA 652: Standard on the Fundamentals of Combustible Dust:** Provides general requirements for managing combustible dust fire and explosion hazards [41].

## 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Any changes or revisions to this SOP must follow a formal change control procedure to ensure that all modifications are properly reviewed, approved, documented, and communicated. This ensures the integrity and effectiveness of the fire safety program.

**9.1 Initiation of Change:**
*   Any personnel may initiate a request for change to this SOP by submitting a Change Request Form to the Fire Safety Officer (FSO) or their supervisor. The form must clearly state the proposed change, the reason for the change, and its potential impact.

**9.2 Review and Assessment:**
*   The FSO, in consultation with relevant department heads and subject matter experts, will review the proposed change. The review will assess the necessity, feasibility, and potential impact of the change on fire safety, operations, and regulatory compliance.
*   For significant changes, a risk assessment will be conducted to identify any new hazards or increased risks introduced by the change.

**9.3 Approval:**
*   All proposed changes must be approved by the FSO and relevant management (e.g., Plant Manager, Quality Assurance Head). For critical changes, approval from the Quality Assurance department is mandatory.
*   The approval signatures will be documented on the Change Request Form.

**9.4 Implementation:**
*   Once approved, the FSO will be responsible for implementing the change. This includes updating the SOP document, assigning a new version number, and updating the effective date.
*   All affected personnel must be trained on the revised SOP before the changes become effective. Training records will be maintained as per Section 5.4.

**9.5 Communication:**
*   The revised SOP will be communicated to all relevant personnel through formal channels, such as email notifications, team meetings, and posting on the company's internal document management system.

**9.6 Documentation of Change:**
*   All changes, including the Change Request Form, review comments, approvals, and training records, will be documented and maintained as part of the SOP's revision history.
