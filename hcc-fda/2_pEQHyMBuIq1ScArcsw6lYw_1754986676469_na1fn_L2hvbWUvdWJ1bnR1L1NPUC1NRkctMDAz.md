### 1.1 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the guidelines and procedures for in-process controls and testing during the manufacturing of large animal pharmaceutical products. The primary purpose of this SOP is to ensure that all intermediate and bulk products conform to established quality attributes, specifications, and regulatory requirements throughout the manufacturing process. This includes maintaining product quality, safety, and efficacy, and ensuring compliance with current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) as mandated by regulatory bodies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) [1, 2]. Adherence to these controls minimizes the risk of product deviations, ensures batch consistency, and facilitates the timely release of safe and effective veterinary pharmaceuticals.

### 1.2 Scope

This SOP applies to all manufacturing operations involving in-process controls and testing of large animal pharmaceutical products at [Company Name/Facility Name]. This encompasses all stages from raw material dispensing through to final bulk product packaging, including but not limited to: compounding, mixing, granulation, compression, filling, and primary packaging. The scope includes both sterile and non-sterile dosage forms, and covers all personnel involved in manufacturing, quality control, quality assurance, and warehousing activities that directly impact in-process materials. This SOP is applicable to all products manufactured for commercial distribution and clinical trials, ensuring that all in-process materials meet predefined critical quality attributes (CQAs) before proceeding to subsequent manufacturing steps [3].

## 2.0 Responsibilities

Effective implementation of in-process controls and testing requires clear delineation of responsibilities among various departments and personnel. This section outlines the key roles and their respective duties to ensure compliance with this SOP and regulatory requirements.

### 2.1 Manufacturing Department

The Manufacturing Department is responsible for executing all production activities in accordance with approved batch records and this SOP. Their responsibilities include, but are not limited to:

*   **Adherence to Procedures:** Ensuring that all in-process control and testing procedures, including sampling, are strictly followed as outlined in this SOP and the respective batch production records.
*   **In-Process Monitoring:** Performing routine in-process checks as specified in the batch record, such as visual inspections, weight checks, and temperature monitoring, and accurately recording all observations and data.
*   **Sample Collection:** Collecting in-process samples at designated stages and frequencies, ensuring proper labeling, handling, and timely submission to the Quality Control (QC) Laboratory.
*   **Equipment Maintenance:** Ensuring that all manufacturing equipment used for in-process operations is calibrated, maintained, and cleaned according to established procedures to prevent contamination and ensure accurate processing.
*   **Deviation Reporting:** Promptly identifying and reporting any deviations, anomalies, or out-of-specification (OOS) results encountered during manufacturing to the Quality Assurance (QA) Department and initiating appropriate corrective actions.
*   **Personnel Training:** Ensuring that all manufacturing personnel involved in in-process operations are adequately trained on this SOP and relevant batch record instructions.

### 2.2 Quality Control (QC) Department

The Quality Control Department is responsible for conducting all specified in-process tests and ensuring the quality of in-process materials. Their responsibilities include:

*   **Sample Receipt and Logging:** Receiving, logging, and securely storing in-process samples submitted by the Manufacturing Department.
*   **In-Process Testing:** Performing all required physical, chemical, and microbiological tests on in-process samples using validated methods and calibrated equipment.
*   **Data Analysis and Reporting:** Accurately analyzing test data, interpreting results against established specifications, and reporting findings in a timely manner to the Manufacturing and Quality Assurance Departments.
*   **Investigation of OOS Results:** Initiating and conducting thorough investigations into any out-of-specification (OOS) or out-of-trend (OOT) results, identifying root causes, and recommending corrective and preventive actions (CAPA).
*   **Method Validation:** Ensuring that all analytical methods used for in-process testing are validated and suitable for their intended purpose.
*   **Equipment Calibration:** Maintaining and calibrating all QC laboratory equipment used for in-process testing according to established schedules and procedures.

### 2.3 Quality Assurance (QA) Department

The Quality Assurance Department provides oversight and ensures compliance with cGMP regulations and internal quality systems. Their responsibilities include:

*   **SOP Approval and Review:** Reviewing, approving, and periodically revising this SOP to ensure its continued relevance and compliance with regulatory requirements.
*   **Batch Record Review:** Reviewing in-process control data and test results documented in batch records to ensure accuracy, completeness, and adherence to specifications before batch release.
*   **Deviation Management:** Overseeing the investigation of deviations, OOS, and OOT results, and approving CAPA plans to prevent recurrence.
*   **Training Oversight:** Ensuring that training programs related to in-process controls and testing are adequate and effectively implemented across relevant departments.
*   **Internal Audits:** Conducting internal audits to assess compliance with this SOP and cGMP regulations, and identifying areas for improvement.
*   **Regulatory Compliance:** Staying abreast of changes in regulatory requirements (e.g., FDA 21 CFR Part 211, USDA guidelines) and ensuring that in-process control procedures are updated accordingly [4, 5].

### 2.4 Research and Development (R&D) Department

The R&D Department is responsible for developing and validating in-process testing methods and specifications during product development. Their responsibilities include:

*   **Method Development:** Developing robust and reliable analytical methods for in-process testing that are suitable for the specific large animal pharmaceutical products.
*   **Specification Setting:** Establishing appropriate in-process specifications based on product characteristics, process understanding, and regulatory requirements.
*   **Method Transfer:** Ensuring smooth transfer of validated analytical methods to the QC Department for routine testing.

## 3.0 Detailed Procedures

This section outlines the detailed procedures for conducting in-process controls and testing during the manufacturing of large animal pharmaceutical products. Adherence to these procedures is critical for maintaining product quality, ensuring batch consistency, and complying with regulatory standards.

### 3.1 General Principles

In-process controls (IPCs) are tests conducted during the manufacturing process to monitor and adjust the process to ensure that the intermediate product conforms to its specifications. These controls are essential for preventing deviations and ensuring the quality of the final product. The general principles governing IPCs include:

*   **Process Understanding:** A thorough understanding of the manufacturing process is fundamental to establishing effective IPCs. This includes identifying critical process parameters (CPPs) and critical quality attributes (CQAs) that need to be monitored and controlled [6].
*   **Risk-Based Approach:** The frequency and type of in-process testing should be determined based on a risk assessment of the manufacturing process and the product. Higher-risk steps or attributes may require more frequent or stringent controls.
*   **Real-Time Monitoring:** Where feasible, in-process controls should incorporate real-time or near real-time monitoring to allow for immediate adjustments and interventions, minimizing the risk of producing out-of-specification material.
*   **Validated Methods:** All analytical methods used for in-process testing must be validated to ensure they are accurate, precise, specific, linear, and robust for their intended purpose [7].
*   **Trained Personnel:** Only qualified and trained personnel are authorized to perform in-process sampling and testing. Training records must be maintained and regularly updated.
*   **Documentation:** All in-process control activities, including sampling, testing, results, and any deviations, must be thoroughly documented in the batch production record or associated forms. Documentation must be accurate, legible, and contemporaneous.
*   **Calibration and Maintenance:** All equipment used for in-process testing and monitoring must be regularly calibrated, maintained, and qualified according to established schedules and procedures to ensure accurate and reliable results.
*   **Environmental Control:** In-process testing areas must be maintained under appropriate environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity, particulate control) to prevent contamination and ensure sample integrity, especially for sterile products.
*   **Sample Integrity:** Samples collected for in-process testing must be handled, stored, and transported in a manner that maintains their integrity and prevents degradation or contamination.

### 3.2 In-Process Sampling Procedures

Accurate and representative sampling is crucial for effective in-process control. This section details the procedures for collecting in-process samples.

#### 3.2.1 General Sampling Requirements

*   **Sampling Plan:** Each batch production record will specify the exact locations, times, frequencies, and quantities for in-process sampling. This plan is developed based on process validation data and risk assessment.
*   **Aseptic Technique:** For sterile products, all sampling must be performed using strict aseptic techniques to prevent microbial contamination. This includes working in classified areas, using sterilized sampling tools, and wearing appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE).
*   **Sampling Tools:** Use only clean, sterile, and appropriate sampling tools for each material. Ensure that sampling tools do not react with or contaminate the product.
*   **Sample Containers:** Use clean, sterile, and appropriate containers for collecting samples. Containers must be compatible with the product and sealed immediately after sampling to prevent contamination or degradation.
*   **Labeling:** Each sample container must be clearly and indelibly labeled immediately after collection with the following information:
    *   Product Name
    *   Batch Number
    *   Sample Point/Location
    *   Date and Time of Sampling
    *   Sampler's Initials
    *   Test(s) to be performed
*   **Sample Integrity:** Samples must be transported to the QC laboratory promptly and under conditions that maintain their integrity (e.g., refrigerated for temperature-sensitive samples).
*   **Retain Samples:** A portion of each in-process sample may be retained as a reference or for future investigation, as specified in the batch record.

#### 3.2.2 Specific Sampling Points and Frequencies

Sampling points and frequencies will vary depending on the specific manufacturing process and product. Examples include:

*   **Blending/Mixing:** Samples taken at defined intervals (e.g., every 15 minutes) during blending to assess homogeneity (e.g., blend uniformity testing).
*   **Granulation:** Samples taken after granulation to assess granule size distribution, moisture content, and flow properties.
*   **Compression/Encapsulation:** Samples taken at regular intervals (e.g., every 30 minutes) during tablet compression or capsule filling to assess weight variation, hardness, thickness, and disintegration time.
*   **Liquid Filling:** Samples taken at the beginning, middle, and end of the filling run to assess fill volume accuracy and product appearance.
*   **Sterile Filtration:** Samples taken before and after sterile filtration to confirm bioburden reduction or sterility.
*   **Bulk Product Holding:** Samples taken from bulk holding tanks to confirm stability and quality before further processing or filling.

#### 3.2.3 Sample Submission to QC

*   **Submission Form:** Complete a sample submission form for each sample or set of samples, detailing the product, batch number, sample point, tests required, and any special handling instructions.
*   **Timely Delivery:** Deliver samples to the QC laboratory within the specified timeframe to ensure timely testing and prevent degradation.
*   **Logging:** QC personnel will log in the samples upon receipt, verifying the labeling and completeness of the submission form.




### 3.3 In-Process Testing Procedures

In-process testing is performed by the Quality Control (QC) Department to evaluate the quality attributes of materials at various stages of manufacturing. All tests must be performed according to validated analytical methods.

#### 3.3.1 Physical Tests

*   **Appearance:** Visual inspection for color, clarity, presence of foreign particles, and other physical characteristics. This is often performed at multiple stages, including after mixing, during filling, and after packaging.
*   **pH:** Measurement of pH for liquid and semi-solid formulations using a calibrated pH meter. This is critical for product stability and efficacy.
*   **Viscosity:** Measurement of viscosity for liquid and semi-solid formulations using a viscometer. This ensures proper flow characteristics during manufacturing and administration.
*   **Density/Specific Gravity:** Measurement for liquid formulations to ensure correct concentration and fill volume.
*   **Moisture Content:** Determination of moisture content in powders, granules, and tablets using a Karl Fischer titrator or loss on drying (LOD) method. This is crucial for stability and prevention of microbial growth.
*   **Particle Size Distribution:** Analysis of particle size for powders and granules using sieving or laser diffraction. This impacts blend uniformity, dissolution, and bioavailability.
*   **Hardness/Friability (for Tablets):** Measurement of tablet hardness using a tablet hardness tester and friability using a friabilator. These tests ensure mechanical integrity during handling and packaging.
*   **Disintegration Time (for Tablets/Capsules):** Measurement of the time required for a tablet or capsule to break down into smaller particles under specified conditions. This is an indicator of drug release.
*   **Weight Variation/Content Uniformity (for Dosage Units):** Individual unit weight checks for tablets, capsules, and filled vials/syringes to ensure consistent dosing. Content uniformity testing ensures that each dosage unit contains the specified amount of active pharmaceutical ingredient (API).
*   **Fill Volume/Net Content:** Verification of the volume or weight of product filled into containers to ensure accurate dosing and compliance with labeling.

#### 3.3.2 Chemical Tests

*   **Assay of Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API):** Quantitative determination of the API concentration in in-process samples using validated analytical techniques such as High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC), Gas Chromatography (GC), or UV-Vis Spectrophotometry. This ensures that the product maintains the correct strength throughout the process.
*   **Impurity/Degradant Testing:** Monitoring for the presence of impurities or degradants that may form during the manufacturing process. This is typically performed using chromatographic methods (HPLC, GC) and ensures product purity.
*   **Dissolution Testing (for Solid Oral Dosage Forms):** Measurement of the rate and extent to which the API dissolves from the dosage form under specified conditions. This is a critical quality attribute related to bioavailability.
*   **Related Substances:** Testing for substances structurally related to the API, which may be process impurities or degradation products.

#### 3.3.3 Microbiological Tests (where applicable)

*   **Bioburden Testing:** Enumeration of viable microorganisms in non-sterile in-process materials to monitor microbial control throughout the manufacturing process.
*   **Endotoxin Testing:** Detection and quantification of bacterial endotoxins in sterile products or products where endotoxin control is critical, using methods such as the Limulus Amebocyte Lysate (LAL) assay.
*   **Sterility Testing:** For sterile products, testing of in-process samples (e.g., bulk solution prior to sterile filtration) to confirm the absence of viable microorganisms.

All testing must be performed by trained QC analysts following the specific analytical method SOPs and documented in the laboratory notebooks and LIMS (Laboratory Information Management System) as per cGMP requirements [8].

### 3.4 Acceptance Criteria and Deviations

#### 3.4.1 Acceptance Criteria

Each in-process test has predefined acceptance criteria, which are established based on product specifications, process capabilities, and regulatory requirements. These criteria are documented in the batch production record and the respective analytical method SOPs. In-process materials must meet all specified acceptance criteria before proceeding to the next manufacturing step. Examples of acceptance criteria include:

*   **pH:** Within a specified range (e.g., 6.0-7.0).
*   **Moisture Content:** Not more than a specified percentage (e.g., NMT 2.0%).
*   **Tablet Hardness:** Within a specified range (e.g., 8-12 kp).
*   **Weight Variation:** Individual tablet weights within ±5% of the target weight.
*   **API Assay:** Within a specified range (e.g., 98.0%-102.0% of label claim).

#### 3.4.2 Handling of Deviations and Out-of-Specification (OOS) Results

Any result that falls outside the established acceptance criteria is considered an Out-of-Specification (OOS) result. Any departure from the approved procedures or expected results is considered a deviation. All deviations and OOS results must be handled according to the following procedure:

*   **Immediate Notification:** The analyst or manufacturing personnel discovering the OOS result or deviation must immediately notify their supervisor and the Quality Assurance (QA) Department.
*   **Investigation Initiation:** QA, in conjunction with QC and Manufacturing, will initiate a formal investigation to determine the root cause of the OOS result or deviation. The investigation should be thorough, timely, and documented.
*   **Phase I Investigation (Laboratory Investigation):** The initial phase of the OOS investigation focuses on the laboratory. This includes:
    *   Review of analytical method and calculations.
    *   Inspection of the instrument and equipment used.
    *   Re-examination of the sample.
    *   Review of raw data and chromatograms.
    *   If a laboratory error is identified and confirmed, the OOS result can be invalidated, and the test re-performed.
*   **Phase II Investigation (Full-Scale Investigation):** If the Phase I investigation does not identify a laboratory error, a full-scale investigation into the manufacturing process is initiated. This includes:
    *   Review of batch production records.
    *   Review of raw material quality.
    *   Review of equipment performance and calibration records.
    *   Personnel interviews.
    *   Process parameters review.
    *   Additional sampling and testing may be performed if justified and documented.
*   **Impact Assessment:** Assess the potential impact of the OOS result or deviation on product quality, safety, and efficacy, as well as on other batches or products.
*   **Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA):** Based on the root cause identified, appropriate CAPA measures must be developed, implemented, and documented to prevent recurrence. This may include process adjustments, equipment repair, personnel retraining, or revision of procedures.
*   **Disposition of Affected Material:** Material associated with an OOS result or significant deviation must be quarantined and held until the investigation is complete and a disposition decision (e.g., rework, reprocess, reject) is made by QA.
*   **Documentation:** All aspects of the deviation or OOS investigation, including findings, conclusions, and CAPA, must be fully documented in a deviation report or OOS investigation report. These reports must be approved by QA.




## 4.0 Documentation Requirements

Accurate, complete, and contemporaneous documentation is a critical component of cGMP compliance and is essential for demonstrating control over the manufacturing process and product quality. All documentation related to in-process controls and testing must adhere to the following principles:

*   **Legibility and Permanence:** All entries must be legible, indelible, and made in black or blue ink. Pencil is not permitted.
*   **Accuracy and Contemporaneousness:** All data and observations must be recorded accurately at the time they are performed. Backdating or pre-dating entries is strictly prohibited.
*   **Attribution:** All entries must be signed and dated by the person making the entry. Any corrections must be made by drawing a single line through the incorrect entry, writing the correct entry, and then signing and dating the correction. The original entry must remain legible.
*   **Completeness:** All required fields on forms, logbooks, and batch records must be completed. No blank spaces should be left.
*   **Batch Production Records:** The primary document for recording in-process control activities is the Batch Production Record (BPR). The BPR must include:
    *   Product name and batch number.
    *   Dates and times of all in-process activities.
    *   Identification of major equipment used.
    *   Specific in-process tests performed, including methods and acceptance criteria.
    *   Results of all in-process tests.
    *   Signatures of personnel performing and verifying each step.
    *   Any deviations from the established procedure, with reference to a deviation report.
*   **Laboratory Records:** All raw data generated during in-process testing (e.g., chromatograms, spectra, instrument printouts, logbooks) must be retained and linked to the specific batch and test. Laboratory notebooks and LIMS (Laboratory Information Management System) entries must be complete and accurate.
*   **Equipment Logbooks:** Logbooks must be maintained for all equipment used in in-process manufacturing and testing, detailing usage, cleaning, maintenance, and calibration activities.
*   **Training Records:** Comprehensive training records for all personnel involved in in-process controls and testing must be maintained, including dates of training, topics covered, and assessment of effectiveness.
*   **SOPs and Specifications:** All relevant SOPs, specifications, and master batch records must be readily available to personnel and referenced in the BPR.
*   **Retention:** All records must be retained for a period as specified by regulatory requirements (e.g., typically one year after the expiration date of the batch or three years after distribution of the batch, whichever is shorter, as per FDA 21 CFR Part 211.180) [9].

## 5.0 Training Requirements

All personnel involved in the execution, supervision, or review of in-process controls and testing activities must receive comprehensive training on this SOP and all related procedures. Effective training ensures that personnel possess the necessary knowledge, skills, and abilities to perform their duties competently and in compliance with cGMP regulations.

*   **Initial Training:** All new employees whose responsibilities include in-process controls and testing must complete initial training before performing any tasks independently. This training must cover:
    *   The contents of this SOP, including purpose, scope, responsibilities, and detailed procedures.
    *   Relevant cGMP regulations, particularly those pertaining to in-process controls, documentation, and quality systems (e.g., FDA 21 CFR Part 211).
    *   Specific analytical methods and equipment operation procedures relevant to their role.
    *   Data integrity principles and documentation practices.
    *   Safety considerations and proper use of personal protective equipment (PPE).
    *   Deviation reporting and OOS investigation procedures.
*   **Refresher Training:** Periodic refresher training must be conducted to ensure ongoing competency and to communicate any updates to procedures, regulations, or equipment. The frequency of refresher training will be determined by the Quality Assurance (QA) Department, typically annually or as needed based on performance or regulatory changes.
*   **Job-Specific Training:** In addition to general training, personnel must receive specific training on the particular tasks they perform, including hands-on training for equipment operation, sampling techniques, and analytical testing.
*   **Training Effectiveness Assessment:** The effectiveness of training must be assessed through methods such as written examinations, practical demonstrations, or on-the-job performance evaluations. Only personnel who demonstrate proficiency should be authorized to perform the tasks.
*   **Training Records:** Detailed training records must be maintained for all personnel. These records must include:
    *   Employee name and identification.
    *   Date(s) of training.
    *   Topics covered.
    *   Trainer's name.
    *   Assessment of training effectiveness.
    *   Signature of the employee and trainer.
*   **SOP Acknowledgment:** All personnel must read, understand, and acknowledge their understanding of this SOP and any revisions prior to implementation.




## 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures are integral to ensuring that in-process materials and finished products consistently meet predefined quality standards. These measures encompass a range of activities designed to monitor, evaluate, and control the quality of materials and processes throughout manufacturing.

*   **In-Process Testing:** As detailed in Section 3.3, in-process testing is a primary quality control measure. It involves conducting physical, chemical, and microbiological tests at various stages of manufacturing to ensure that critical quality attributes are maintained.
*   **Calibration and Maintenance of Equipment:** All equipment used for manufacturing and testing, particularly those involved in critical process parameters or quality attribute measurements, must be regularly calibrated, qualified, and maintained according to established schedules. This ensures the accuracy and reliability of data generated.
*   **Validation of Analytical Methods:** All analytical methods used for in-process testing must be fully validated to demonstrate their suitability for their intended purpose. Validation parameters typically include accuracy, precision, specificity, linearity, range, and robustness.
*   **Control Charts and Statistical Process Control (SPC):** Where appropriate, statistical process control techniques, such as control charts, should be utilized to monitor in-process parameters and test results. This allows for early detection of trends or shifts in the process, enabling proactive intervention before out-of-specification conditions occur.
*   **Reference Standards and Reagents:** Only qualified and appropriately stored reference standards and reagents should be used for in-process testing. Their expiry dates must be monitored, and they should be replaced as necessary.
*   **Environmental Monitoring:** For certain manufacturing processes, particularly those involving sterile products, environmental monitoring (e.g., viable and non-viable particulate counts, surface and air sampling) is a critical quality control measure to ensure that the manufacturing environment does not compromise product quality.
*   **Personnel Qualification:** All personnel involved in quality control activities must be adequately qualified through education, training, and experience. Their competency should be regularly assessed.
*   **Internal Quality Audits:** Regular internal audits should be conducted by the Quality Assurance Department to assess compliance with this SOP, cGMP regulations, and other relevant quality system documents. Audit findings should lead to corrective actions and continuous improvement.
*   **Supplier Qualification:** While not directly an in-process control, the quality of incoming raw materials significantly impacts in-process quality. Therefore, a robust supplier qualification program is an essential upstream quality control measure.
*   **Retention Samples:** Retain samples of in-process materials and finished products should be stored under appropriate conditions for a specified period to allow for future investigation or retesting if required.




## 7.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all manufacturing and testing operations, especially when handling pharmaceutical ingredients and operating machinery. All personnel involved in in-process controls and testing must adhere to established safety protocols to prevent accidents, injuries, and exposure to hazardous materials. This section outlines key safety considerations.

*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Appropriate PPE must be worn at all times when handling materials or operating equipment. This includes, but is not limited to:
    *   **Gloves:** Chemical-resistant gloves (e.g., nitrile, latex) suitable for the materials being handled.
    *   **Eye Protection:** Safety glasses or goggles to protect against splashes or airborne particles.
    *   **Lab Coats/Gowns:** To protect personal clothing and prevent contamination.
    *   **Respiratory Protection:** Respirators or masks may be required when handling powdered materials or volatile chemicals, as indicated by Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) or risk assessments.
    *   **Footwear:** Closed-toe, non-slip shoes.
*   **Material Handling:**
    *   **Hazard Communication:** All personnel must be familiar with the hazards associated with the materials they are handling. MSDS for all chemicals and active pharmaceutical ingredients must be readily accessible.
    *   **Proper Storage:** Chemicals and materials must be stored according to their specific requirements (e.g., temperature, light sensitivity, segregation of incompatible materials).
    *   **Spill Procedures:** Personnel must be trained on proper spill containment and cleanup procedures for all materials used in the area.
*   **Equipment Safety:**
    *   **Operating Procedures:** All equipment must be operated strictly according to its operating manual and established SOPs. Unauthorized operation is prohibited.
    *   **Lock-Out/Tag-Out:** Before performing any maintenance, cleaning, or repair on equipment, proper lock-out/tag-out procedures must be followed to prevent accidental startup.
    *   **Guards and Safety Features:** Never operate equipment with safety guards removed or bypassed. Report any malfunctioning safety features immediately.
*   **Emergency Procedures:**
    *   **Emergency Exits:** Be aware of the location of all emergency exits and evacuation routes.
    *   **First Aid:** Know the location of first aid kits and emergency showers/eyewash stations.
    *   **Emergency Contacts:** Have emergency contact numbers readily available.
    *   **Fire Safety:** Be familiar with the location and proper use of fire extinguishers.
*   **Waste Disposal:** All waste materials, including chemical waste, biological waste, and general refuse, must be disposed of according to established company procedures and local, state, and federal regulations.
*   **Ergonomics:** Personnel should be mindful of ergonomic principles to prevent musculoskeletal injuries, especially during repetitive tasks or heavy lifting.

All safety incidents, near misses, or hazardous conditions must be reported immediately to supervision and documented according to the company's incident reporting procedure.

## 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to the manufacturing process, equipment, analytical methods, raw materials, or any aspect covered by this SOP that could impact the quality, safety, or efficacy of the large animal pharmaceutical product must be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are evaluated, approved, implemented, and documented in a controlled manner.

*   **Initiation of Change:** Any department or individual identifying a need for a change must initiate a Change Control Request (CCR) form. The CCR must clearly describe the proposed change, the reason for the change, and its potential impact.
*   **Assessment of Impact:** The Quality Assurance (QA) Department, in conjunction with relevant departments (e.g., Manufacturing, QC, R&D, Regulatory Affairs), will assess the potential impact of the proposed change on:
    *   Product quality attributes (identity, strength, purity, quality).
    *   Process performance and validation status.
    *   Analytical methods and their validation status.
    *   Regulatory filings and approvals.
    *   Stability of the product.
    *   Training requirements.
    *   Documentation (SOPs, batch records, specifications).
*   **Risk Assessment:** A risk assessment must be conducted for each proposed change to identify potential risks to product quality or patient safety and to determine the level of testing, validation, or documentation required to support the change.
*   **Approval of Change:** All proposed changes must be reviewed and approved by relevant departmental heads and, critically, by the Quality Assurance Department, prior to implementation. Approval signifies that the change has been adequately assessed and is deemed acceptable.
*   **Implementation Plan:** A detailed implementation plan must be developed for approved changes, outlining the steps to be taken, responsibilities, timelines, and any necessary re-validation or re-qualification activities.
*   **Documentation Update:** All relevant documents, including this SOP, batch records, specifications, and analytical methods, must be updated to reflect the approved change. The new version of the document must be controlled and distributed.
*   **Training:** Personnel affected by the change must be trained on the revised procedures or new processes before the change is implemented.
*   **Effectiveness Check:** After implementation, an effectiveness check should be performed to confirm that the change achieved its intended outcome and did not introduce any unforeseen negative impacts on product quality or process performance.
*   **Emergency Changes:** In rare cases where an emergency change is required to prevent a critical safety issue or significant product loss, an expedited change control process may be followed, but full documentation and retrospective approval must still be completed.
