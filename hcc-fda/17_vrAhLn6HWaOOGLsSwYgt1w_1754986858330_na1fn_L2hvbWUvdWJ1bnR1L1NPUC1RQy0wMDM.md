### 1.1 Purpose
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the guidelines and procedures for the proper management of laboratory samples, including their collection, receipt, labeling, storage, tracking, and disposal, within large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The primary purpose is to ensure the integrity, traceability, and quality of all samples throughout their lifecycle, from initial acquisition to final disposition. This SOP is critical for maintaining compliance with regulatory requirements, facilitating accurate analytical testing, and supporting the overall quality management system.

### 1.2 Scope
This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the handling of laboratory samples within the [Insert Department/Facility Name, e.g., Quality Control Laboratory, Research and Development Department] at [Insert Company Name]. This includes, but is not limited to, laboratory technicians, quality control analysts, research scientists, and any other personnel responsible for sample management in the context of large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing. The scope encompasses all types of samples, including raw materials, in-process materials, finished products, stability samples, environmental monitoring samples, and samples collected for research and development purposes related to large animal pharmaceuticals. This SOP covers the entire chain of custody for samples, from the point of generation or receipt through analysis, storage, and ultimate disposal.

## 2.0 Responsibilities

### 2.1 Quality Control (QC) Department
The Quality Control Department is responsible for the overall implementation and oversight of this SOP. This includes ensuring that all personnel involved in sample management are adequately trained, that appropriate resources are available for sample handling and storage, and that all sample-related activities comply with established procedures and regulatory requirements. The QC Department is also responsible for maintaining the integrity of the sample management system, conducting regular audits, and initiating corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) as needed.

### 2.2 Laboratory Personnel
All laboratory personnel, including analysts and technicians, are responsible for adhering to the procedures outlined in this SOP during all stages of sample handling. This includes accurate sample collection, proper labeling, correct storage, meticulous record-keeping, and timely transfer of samples. They are also responsible for reporting any deviations or anomalies encountered during sample management to their immediate supervisor and the Quality Control Department.

### 2.3 Research and Development (R&D) Department
The Research and Development Department is responsible for ensuring that samples generated during R&D activities are managed in accordance with this SOP. This includes providing accurate information regarding sample origin, composition, and specific handling requirements. R&D personnel are also responsible for coordinating with the QC Department for the transfer and analysis of R&D samples.

### 2.4 Production Department
The Production Department is responsible for the proper collection and initial handling of in-process and finished product samples, ensuring that samples are representative of the batch and are transferred to the laboratory in a timely manner and under appropriate conditions as specified in relevant production and sampling procedures.

### 2.5 Management
Management is responsible for providing the necessary resources, including personnel, equipment, and facilities, to ensure effective implementation of this SOP. They are also responsible for fostering a culture of quality and compliance within the organization and for reviewing and approving this SOP and any subsequent revisions.




## 3.0 Detailed Procedures

This section outlines the step-by-step procedures for managing laboratory samples, ensuring their integrity, traceability, and compliance with regulatory standards throughout their lifecycle in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations.

### 3.1 Sample Collection and Receipt

#### 3.1.1 Sample Collection

1.  **Preparation:** Before sample collection, ensure all necessary materials are available, including appropriate sterile containers, labels, collection devices (e.g., needles, syringes, swabs), and personal protective equipment (PPE). Verify that all equipment is calibrated and in good working order. For large animal samples, consider the specific animal species, its temperament, and the appropriate restraint methods to ensure both animal welfare and personnel safety. [1]
2.  **Aseptic Technique:** Maintain strict aseptic technique during sample collection to prevent contamination. This is particularly crucial for microbiological samples. Sterilize the collection site on the animal as appropriate for the sample type (e.g., skin disinfection for blood collection). [2]
3.  **Sample Volume:** Collect the appropriate volume of sample as specified by the analytical method or study protocol. Insufficient sample volume may compromise analytical accuracy or prevent retesting if necessary.
4.  **Immediate Transfer:** Immediately transfer collected samples into their designated containers. Avoid leaving samples exposed to the environment.

#### 3.1.2 Sample Receipt

1.  **Verification:** Upon receipt in the laboratory, verify that each sample is accompanied by a completed sample submission form or electronic record. Cross-reference the information on the sample container label with the submission form to ensure accuracy (e.g., sample ID, date/time of collection, sample type, source, and any special handling instructions). [3]
2.  **Condition Check:** Visually inspect each sample for any signs of damage, leakage, or improper handling during transit. Document any discrepancies or issues immediately. Samples compromised during transit should be quarantined and assessed for usability according to established deviation procedures.
3.  **Logging:** Log all received samples into the Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS) or a designated sample logbook. Assign a unique laboratory accession number to each sample. Record the date and time of receipt, the name of the receiving personnel, and the initial storage location. [4]
4.  **Prioritization:** Prioritize samples based on urgency or stability requirements. Unstable samples should be processed or stored appropriately without delay.

### 3.2 Sample Labeling and Identification

1.  **Unique Identification:** Each sample container MUST be clearly and indelibly labeled with a unique identifier that links it to the sample submission form and the LIMS record. This identifier should be robust enough to withstand storage conditions (e.g., freezing, refrigeration). [5]
2.  **Essential Information:** The label should include, at a minimum, the following information:
    *   Unique Sample ID (e.g., SOP-QC-003-YYMMDD-XXX)
    *   Date and Time of Collection
    *   Sample Type (e.g., blood, urine, tissue, raw material, finished product)
    *   Source (e.g., animal ID, batch number, lot number)
    *   Collector's Initials
    *   Any special handling or storage requirements (e.g., “Protect from Light,” “Store at -20°C”).
3.  **Legibility and Durability:** Ensure all labeling is legible, permanent, and resistant to environmental factors such as moisture, temperature extremes, and chemical exposure. Use labels specifically designed for laboratory use. [6]
4.  **Secondary Labeling:** For samples transferred to secondary containers (e.g., aliquots), ensure that the unique identifier is transferred to the new container, along with relevant information to maintain traceability.

### 3.3 Sample Storage and Preservation

1.  **Temperature Control:** Store samples at the specified temperature conditions immediately after receipt to maintain their stability and integrity. Common storage temperatures include ambient, refrigerated (2-8°C), frozen (-20°C), or ultra-low frozen (-70°C or colder). Monitor storage unit temperatures daily and record them. [7]
2.  **Storage Location:** Store samples in designated, secure storage units (e.g., refrigerators, freezers, controlled environment rooms) that are regularly monitored and maintained. Organize samples systematically to facilitate easy retrieval and minimize the risk of misplacement.
3.  **Protection from Degradation:** Protect samples from factors that can cause degradation, such as light, humidity, and atmospheric gases, as appropriate for the sample type. Use amber containers for light-sensitive samples and desiccant for moisture-sensitive materials.
4.  **Segregation:** Segregate samples to prevent cross-contamination or mix-ups. This includes separating different sample types, different studies, or samples from different animal groups, especially in large animal facilities where various species or health statuses may be present.
5.  **Inventory Management:** Maintain an accurate and up-to-date inventory of all stored samples, including their unique ID, storage location, quantity, and expiration date (if applicable). Regularly reconcile the physical inventory with the LIMS or logbook records.

### 3.4 Sample Tracking and Chain of Custody

1.  **Chain of Custody Form:** A Chain of Custody (COC) form MUST accompany each sample or batch of samples from the point of collection to final disposition. The COC form serves as a legal document that records the possession and transfer of samples. [8]
2.  **Information on COC Form:** The COC form should include:
    *   Sample ID(s)
    *   Date and Time of Collection
    *   Date and Time of Transfer
    *   Name and Signature of Relinquishing Personnel
    *   Name and Signature of Receiving Personnel
    *   Reason for Transfer (e.g., analysis, storage, disposal)
    *   Storage Conditions during Transfer (if applicable)
    *   Any observed discrepancies or comments.
3.  **Transfer Procedures:** When samples are transferred between individuals, departments, or laboratories, both the relinquishing and receiving parties MUST sign and date the COC form. This ensures an unbroken chain of accountability. [9]
4.  **LIMS Integration:** Utilize a LIMS to electronically track sample movement, storage locations, and chain of custody. The LIMS should provide an audit trail of all sample-related activities, including who accessed the sample, when, and for what purpose.
5.  **Security:** Implement physical and electronic security measures to prevent unauthorized access to samples and sample records. This includes restricted access to storage areas and password-protected LIMS access.

### 3.5 Sample Disposal

1.  **Authorization:** Dispose of samples only after all required analyses are completed, data reviewed, and retention periods fulfilled, and with proper authorization. [10]
2.  **Waste Classification:** Classify samples as hazardous or non-hazardous waste according to local, national, and international regulations. For large animal pharmaceutical samples, this may include biological waste, chemical waste, or pharmaceutical waste. [11]
3.  **Disposal Methods:** Dispose of samples using appropriate methods for their classification. This may include incineration, autoclaving, chemical neutralization, or licensed waste disposal contractors. Ensure that disposal methods comply with environmental regulations and biosafety guidelines. [12]
4.  **Documentation of Disposal:** Document all sample disposals, including the sample ID, date of disposal, method of disposal, and the name of the personnel performing the disposal. Maintain these records for the required retention period.

## 4.0 Documentation Requirements

Accurate and comprehensive documentation is paramount in pharmaceutical operations to ensure traceability, accountability, and compliance with regulatory standards. All records related to laboratory sample management and chain of custody must be maintained in a clear, concise, and readily retrievable manner. [1]

### 4.1 Sample Submission Forms
Each sample submitted to the laboratory must be accompanied by a completed sample submission form, either in hard copy or electronic format. This form must contain all pertinent information necessary for sample identification, processing, and analysis, including:
*   Unique Sample ID
*   Date and Time of Collection
*   Collector’s Name/ID
*   Sample Type and Matrix
*   Source of Sample (e.g., animal ID, batch number, lot number, study ID)
*   Test(s) Requested
*   Any special handling or storage instructions
*   Relevant clinical history or background information for animal samples.

### 4.2 Laboratory Logbooks and LIMS Records
All sample receipt, handling, storage, and disposal activities must be meticulously documented in designated laboratory logbooks or within the Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS). The LIMS should serve as the primary system for managing sample data, providing an electronic audit trail of all actions performed on each sample. Key information to be recorded includes:
*   Date and Time of Sample Receipt
*   Name/ID of Receiving Personnel
*   Assigned Laboratory Accession Number
*   Initial Storage Location
*   Dates and Times of Sample Transfers (internal and external)
*   Names/IDs of Personnel Involved in Transfers
*   Current Storage Location
*   Date and Method of Sample Disposal.

### 4.3 Chain of Custody Records
The Chain of Custody (COC) form is a critical document that tracks the possession and transfer of samples from the point of collection to final disposition. A continuous and unbroken chain of custody must be maintained for all samples. The COC form must include:
*   Unique Sample ID(s)
*   Date and Time of Collection
*   Date and Time of each Transfer
*   Name and Signature of Relinquishing Personnel
*   Name and Signature of Receiving Personnel
*   Reason for Transfer
*   Any observed discrepancies or comments.
Completed COC forms must be stored securely and be readily accessible for audit and inspection purposes.

### 4.4 Equipment Calibration and Maintenance Records
Records of calibration, maintenance, and performance verification for all equipment used in sample management (e.g., refrigerators, freezers, balances, pipettes) must be maintained. These records demonstrate that equipment is functioning correctly and that environmental conditions are controlled.

### 4.5 Deviation and CAPA Records
Any deviations from this SOP, or any other related procedures, must be documented, investigated, and resolved through the established Deviation and Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) system. All records pertaining to deviations and CAPAs must be maintained.

## 6.0 Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures are integral to ensuring the reliability and integrity of laboratory samples and the data derived from them. These measures help to minimize errors, prevent contamination, and ensure that samples are fit for their intended analytical purpose. [3]

### 6.1 Sample Integrity Checks
Regular checks must be performed to assess the integrity of samples throughout their lifecycle. This includes:
*   **Visual Inspection:** Upon receipt and prior to analysis, visually inspect samples for signs of degradation, contamination, or improper storage (e.g., hemolysis in blood samples, turbidity, discoloration, particulate matter).
*   **Temperature Monitoring:** Continuously monitor and record temperatures of all sample storage units (refrigerators, freezers). Alarms should be in place to alert personnel of temperature excursions.
*   **Storage Condition Verification:** Periodically verify that samples are stored under the specified conditions (e.g., light protection, appropriate container).

### 6.2 LIMS Data Integrity
The LIMS plays a crucial role in sample management. Quality control measures for LIMS data integrity include:
*   **Data Entry Verification:** Implement procedures for verifying the accuracy of data entered into the LIMS, ideally through a second-person review or automated checks.
*   **Audit Trails:** Ensure the LIMS maintains a comprehensive, unalterable audit trail of all data entries, modifications, and deletions, including the identity of the user, date, and time.
*   **System Validation:** The LIMS must be validated according to regulatory requirements to ensure it consistently performs its intended functions accurately and reliably.

### 6.3 Reagent and Consumable Quality
Ensure that all reagents, media, and consumables used in sample collection, processing, and storage are of appropriate quality and are not expired. Maintain records of lot numbers and expiration dates.

### 6.4 Internal Audits
Conduct regular internal audits of the sample management system to assess compliance with this SOP, identify areas for improvement, and ensure ongoing effectiveness. Audit findings should be documented, and any deficiencies addressed through the CAPA system.

## 7.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is a paramount concern when handling laboratory samples, particularly in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing where biological hazards, chemical hazards, and physical hazards may be present. All personnel must adhere to established safety protocols and use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE). [4]

### 7.1 General Laboratory Safety
*   **Hazard Communication:** All personnel must be trained on the hazards associated with the samples and chemicals they handle, as outlined in Safety Data Sheets (SDS).
*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Always wear appropriate PPE, including laboratory coats, safety glasses, gloves (e.g., nitrile, latex, or specialized animal handling gloves), and, when necessary, respiratory protection or face shields. The type of PPE should be commensurate with the risk associated with the sample.
*   **Hand Hygiene:** Practice frequent and thorough hand washing, especially after handling samples and before leaving the laboratory.
*   **No Eating/Drinking:** Eating, drinking, smoking, and applying cosmetics are strictly prohibited in sample handling and laboratory areas.

### 7.2 Biological Safety
When handling samples from large animals, there is a potential risk of exposure to zoonotic pathogens. [5]
*   **Biosafety Levels:** Handle samples according to their assigned biosafety level (BSL-1, BSL-2, BSL-3). Most routine large animal pharmaceutical samples will be handled at BSL-2. [6]
*   **Biological Safety Cabinets (BSCs):** Use Class II Biological Safety Cabinets for procedures that may generate aerosols or splashes involving biological materials.
*   **Spill Response:** Be prepared to respond to biological spills using appropriate disinfectants and spill kits. All spills must be cleaned up immediately and documented.
*   **Waste Disposal:** Dispose of biological waste in designated biohazard containers, which are then decontaminated (e.g., autoclaved) or incinerated according to regulatory requirements.

### 7.3 Chemical Safety
*   **Chemical Storage:** Store chemicals used for sample preservation or analysis according to their compatibility and hazard class. Ensure proper ventilation in chemical storage areas.
*   **Fume Hoods:** Perform procedures involving volatile or hazardous chemicals in a certified chemical fume hood.
*   **Chemical Spill Response:** Have appropriate chemical spill kits readily available and know how to use them. Report and clean up chemical spills immediately.
*   **Waste Disposal:** Dispose of chemical waste according to hazardous waste regulations. Do not dispose of chemicals down the drain unless specifically authorized.

### 7.4 Animal Handling Safety
For personnel involved in direct large animal sample collection:
*   **Animal Restraint:** Use appropriate and humane animal restraint techniques to ensure the safety of both the animal and the personnel. This may involve physical restraint, chemical sedation, or specialized chutes/pens. [7]
*   **Zoonotic Disease Awareness:** Be aware of potential zoonotic diseases associated with the animal species being sampled and take appropriate precautions.
*   **Sharps Safety:** Exercise extreme caution when handling needles, scalpels, and other sharps. Dispose of sharps immediately after use in puncture-resistant sharps containers.

## 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Any changes to this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) must be managed through a formal change control process to ensure that all revisions are documented, reviewed, approved, and communicated to affected personnel. This process ensures the integrity and currency of the SOP and maintains compliance with regulatory requirements. [1]

### 9.1 Initiation of Change
Any individual identifying a need for a change to this SOP (e.g., due to new regulations, process improvements, equipment changes, or identified deficiencies) must initiate a change request. The change request should clearly describe the proposed change, the reason for the change, and its potential impact.

### 9.2 Review and Assessment
All change requests must be formally reviewed and assessed by relevant stakeholders, including:
*   **Originator:** The person proposing the change.
*   **Department Head/Supervisor:** The head of the department responsible for the SOP.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA):** QA must assess the impact of the change on product quality, regulatory compliance, and other related systems.
*   **Subject Matter Experts (SMEs):** Individuals with expertise in the specific area affected by the change.

The assessment should determine the necessity of the change, its potential risks, and the resources required for implementation.

### 9.3 Approval of Change
Following a thorough review and assessment, the proposed change must be formally approved by designated authorities. This typically includes the Department Head, Quality Assurance, and potentially other senior management, depending on the significance of the change. Approval signifies that the change is justified, adequately assessed, and ready for implementation.

### 9.4 Implementation of Change
Once approved, the change must be implemented. This involves:
*   **Revision of SOP:** The SOP document must be revised to incorporate the approved changes. The version number must be updated, and a clear description of the change must be recorded in the Version Control section.
*   **Training:** All personnel affected by the change must be trained on the revised SOP. Training effectiveness must be assessed and documented.
*   **Communication:** The revised SOP and associated changes must be effectively communicated to all relevant personnel.
*   **Effective Date:** A clear effective date for the revised SOP must be established and communicated.

### 9.5 Documentation of Change Control
All aspects of the change control process, including the change request, review comments, assessment, approvals, implementation details, and training records, must be thoroughly documented and archived. This documentation provides an audit trail for all SOP revisions.
