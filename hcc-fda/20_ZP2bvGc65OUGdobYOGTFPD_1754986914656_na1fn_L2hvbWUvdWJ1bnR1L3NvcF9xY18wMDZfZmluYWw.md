### 1.1 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the systematic process for the calibration and maintenance of laboratory equipment used in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The primary purpose of this SOP is to ensure the accuracy, reliability, and precision of all laboratory equipment, thereby guaranteeing the integrity of analytical data, the quality of manufactured products, and compliance with national and international regulatory standards. Accurate calibration and consistent maintenance are critical for preventing deviations, reducing risks associated with product quality, and ensuring the safety and efficacy of veterinary pharmaceuticals.

### 1.2 Scope

This SOP applies to all laboratory equipment utilized within the Quality Control (QC) and Research & Development (R&D) departments involved in the manufacturing, testing, and release of large animal pharmaceutical products. This includes, but is not limited to, analytical balances, pH meters, spectrophotometers, high-performance liquid chromatographs (HPLC), gas chromatographs (GC), dissolution apparatus, incubators, centrifuges, and environmental chambers. The scope encompasses initial calibration upon installation, routine calibration checks, preventative maintenance, corrective maintenance, and documentation of all related activities. This SOP is applicable to all personnel involved in the operation, calibration, and maintenance of laboratory equipment within the designated facilities.




## 2. Responsibilities

### 2.1 Quality Control (QC) Manager

The QC Manager is ultimately responsible for the overall implementation and adherence to this SOP. This includes ensuring that adequate resources (personnel, equipment, training) are available for calibration and maintenance activities, reviewing and approving calibration schedules, overseeing corrective actions for out-of-calibration equipment, and ensuring compliance with all relevant regulatory requirements. The QC Manager is also responsible for the periodic review and update of this SOP.

### 2.2 Laboratory Supervisors

Laboratory Supervisors are responsible for the day-to-day oversight of calibration and maintenance activities within their respective sections. This includes scheduling and assigning calibration tasks, ensuring that all personnel are adequately trained, reviewing calibration and maintenance records for accuracy and completeness, initiating corrective actions for non-conforming equipment, and liaising with external service providers when necessary. They are also responsible for maintaining an up-to-date inventory of all laboratory equipment.

### 2.3 Laboratory Analysts/Technicians

Laboratory Analysts and Technicians are responsible for performing calibration and maintenance procedures as outlined in this SOP. This includes conducting routine calibration checks, performing preventative maintenance tasks, accurately recording all calibration and maintenance data, reporting any equipment malfunctions or deviations, and adhering to all safety protocols. They must ensure that equipment is properly cleaned and stored after use.

### 2.4 Metrology/Calibration Department (if applicable)

For organizations with a dedicated Metrology or Calibration Department, this department is responsible for performing complex calibrations, maintaining calibration standards, developing and validating calibration methods, and providing technical support and training to laboratory personnel. They are also responsible for managing external calibration services and ensuring that all external calibration certificates meet regulatory requirements.

### 2.5 Engineering/Maintenance Department

The Engineering/Maintenance Department is responsible for performing major repairs, complex troubleshooting, and specialized maintenance tasks that are beyond the scope of routine laboratory maintenance. They work in conjunction with the QC and Laboratory Supervisors to ensure that equipment is functioning optimally and that any necessary repairs are completed in a timely manner to minimize downtime.




## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 General Calibration Procedures

All laboratory equipment requiring calibration shall be identified and included in a master calibration schedule. This schedule shall specify the equipment name, unique identification number, calibration frequency, and the responsible party. All calibration activities shall be performed using certified reference materials (CRMs) or standards traceable to national or international standards (e.g., NIST). Prior to any calibration, the equipment must be cleaned and inspected for any visible damage or malfunction. Any issues identified must be addressed before proceeding with calibration.

#### 3.1.1 Calibration Frequency

Calibration frequencies shall be established based on manufacturer recommendations, regulatory requirements, the criticality of the equipment, and the historical performance of the equipment. Frequencies may be adjusted based on trend analysis of calibration data, but any adjustments must be justified and documented through the change control procedure (Section 9).

#### 3.1.2 Calibration Standards and Reference Materials

All calibration standards and reference materials must be properly stored, handled, and used within their expiry dates. Certificates of analysis and traceability documentation for all standards must be maintained and readily accessible. The suitability of calibration standards must be verified prior to use.

#### 3.1.3 Calibration Environment

Calibrations shall be performed in an environment that does not adversely affect the accuracy of the measurement. Environmental conditions such as temperature, humidity, and vibration shall be monitored and controlled as necessary, especially for sensitive equipment. Any deviations from specified environmental conditions during calibration must be documented and assessed for their potential impact on the calibration results.

#### 3.1.4 Calibration Procedure Steps

1.  **Preparation:** Ensure the equipment is clean, powered on, and has reached stable operating conditions (e.g., warm-up time). Gather all necessary tools, calibration standards, and documentation forms.
2.  **Verification of Standards:** Verify that all calibration standards are within their expiry dates and have valid traceability certificates.
3.  **Pre-calibration Check (if applicable):** Perform any manufacturer-recommended pre-calibration checks or diagnostic tests.
4.  **Execution of Calibration:** Follow the specific calibration procedure for the equipment as detailed in Section 3.2 or the manufacturer's manual. This typically involves applying known inputs and recording the equipment's response.
5.  **Data Recording:** Accurately record all raw data, environmental conditions, and any observations during the calibration process on the designated calibration log or form.
6.  **Acceptance Criteria:** Compare the recorded results against the established acceptance criteria. The equipment must meet these criteria to be considered in calibration.
7.  **Adjustment (if necessary):** If the equipment is found to be out of calibration, perform necessary adjustments according to the manufacturer's instructions or established internal procedures. After adjustment, a full re-calibration must be performed to confirm accuracy.
8.  **Post-calibration Labeling:** Affix a calibration label to the equipment indicating the calibration date, next due date, and the identity of the person who performed the calibration.
9.  **Documentation Review:** Ensure all calibration documentation is complete, accurate, and signed by the performing analyst and reviewed by the supervisor.




### 3.2 Specific Equipment Calibration Procedures

#### 3.2.1 Analytical Balances

Analytical balances are critical for accurate weighing of samples and reagents. Calibration typically involves using certified calibration weights (e.g., OIML Class E2 or ASTM Class 1) that cover the balance's operating range. The procedure includes:

1.  **Daily Check:** Perform a daily check using a single, appropriate calibration weight (e.g., 100g or 200g) to verify accuracy. Record the reading.
2.  **Monthly/Quarterly Calibration:** Perform a multi-point calibration using at least three certified weights (e.g., minimum, mid-range, and maximum capacity). Ensure the balance is level and stable. Record all readings and compare against acceptance criteria (e.g., ±0.01% of the applied load or manufacturer's specifications).
3.  **Linearity and Eccentricity:** Periodically check linearity across the weighing range and eccentricity (corner load test) to ensure consistent readings across the pan.

#### 3.2.2 pH Meters

pH meters are used for measuring the acidity or alkalinity of solutions. Calibration is performed using certified pH buffer solutions.

1.  **Daily Calibration:** Calibrate the pH meter daily before use, or more frequently if required by the assay. Use at least two, preferably three, certified pH buffer solutions (e.g., pH 4.00, 7.00, and 10.00).
2.  **Electrode Care:** Ensure the pH electrode is properly stored in storage solution and cleaned regularly to prevent clogging and ensure accurate readings.
3.  **Temperature Compensation:** Verify that the temperature compensation is correctly set, either automatically or manually, as pH is temperature-dependent.

#### 3.2.3 Spectrophotometers (UV/Vis)

Spectrophotometers are used for quantitative analysis of substances based on their light absorption properties. Calibration involves checking wavelength accuracy, photometric accuracy, and stray light.

1.  **Wavelength Accuracy:** Verify wavelength accuracy using certified reference materials such as Holmium Oxide glass filters or solutions with known absorption peaks (e.g., Potassium Dichromate solution). Scan the reference and compare observed peaks to certified values.
2.  **Photometric Accuracy:** Check photometric accuracy using certified neutral density filters or Potassium Dichromate solutions at specified wavelengths. Measure the absorbance and compare to certified values.
3.  **Stray Light:** Assess stray light using appropriate cut-off filters or solutions (e.g., Potassium Chloride solution at 200 nm).

#### 3.2.4 High-Performance Liquid Chromatographs (HPLC)

HPLCs are used for separating, identifying, and quantifying components in a mixture. Calibration involves checking flow rate accuracy, injector linearity, detector linearity, and column oven temperature accuracy.

1.  **Flow Rate Accuracy:** Verify pump flow rate accuracy using a calibrated flow meter. Compare the measured flow rate to the set flow rate.
2.  **Injector Linearity:** Assess injector linearity by injecting a series of known concentrations of a standard and plotting the peak area/height against concentration. The correlation coefficient (R²) should meet acceptance criteria (e.g., >0.999).
3.  **Detector Linearity:** Evaluate detector linearity similarly to injector linearity, ensuring the detector response is proportional to the analyte concentration over the working range.
4.  **Column Oven Temperature:** Verify the accuracy of the column oven temperature using a calibrated thermometer.




### 3.3 Maintenance Procedures

Regular maintenance is crucial for extending the lifespan of laboratory equipment, ensuring optimal performance, and preventing unexpected breakdowns. Maintenance activities are categorized into preventative maintenance and corrective maintenance.

#### 3.3.1 Preventative Maintenance (PM)

Preventative maintenance involves scheduled activities designed to prevent equipment failure and maintain performance. A PM schedule shall be established for each piece of equipment based on manufacturer recommendations, usage, and criticality.

1.  **Routine Cleaning:** All equipment surfaces, sample compartments, and accessible parts shall be cleaned regularly using appropriate cleaning agents to prevent contamination and buildup. This includes wiping down exteriors, cleaning spills immediately, and ensuring optical components (e.g., spectrophotometer cuvette holders) are free of dust and debris.
2.  **Filter Replacement:** Replace air filters, solvent filters, and other consumable filters according to manufacturer recommendations or as indicated by equipment performance. Document all filter replacements.
3.  **Lubrication:** Lubricate moving parts as specified by the manufacturer to reduce wear and tear.
4.  **Component Inspection:** Regularly inspect hoses, tubing, seals, and electrical cords for signs of wear, cracks, or damage. Replace any compromised components immediately.
5.  **Software/Firmware Updates:** Ensure equipment software and firmware are up-to-date to benefit from performance enhancements and bug fixes. All updates must be validated and documented.
6.  **Performance Verification:** After performing PM, conduct a brief performance verification check (e.g., a quick calibration check or system suitability test) to ensure the equipment is functioning correctly.

#### 3.3.2 Corrective Maintenance (CM)

Corrective maintenance is performed when equipment malfunctions or breaks down. The goal is to restore the equipment to proper working order as quickly and efficiently as possible.

1.  **Problem Identification:** When an equipment malfunction is observed, the user shall immediately report the issue to their supervisor and complete an Equipment Malfunction Report form.
2.  **Troubleshooting:** The supervisor or designated technician shall attempt to troubleshoot the issue using the equipment manual and established troubleshooting guides. Simple issues may be resolved in-house.
3.  **Repair/Service Request:** If the issue cannot be resolved through basic troubleshooting, a service request shall be initiated with the Engineering/Maintenance Department or an authorized external service provider. Provide a detailed description of the problem and any troubleshooting steps already taken.
4.  **Repair and Testing:** Once repairs are completed, the equipment must be thoroughly tested and re-calibrated (if necessary) to ensure it meets all performance specifications before being returned to service. All repair activities, parts replaced, and post-repair testing results must be documented.
5.  **Out-of-Service Tagging:** Equipment that is malfunctioning or awaiting repair shall be clearly tagged as "Out of Service" to prevent accidental use.




## 4. Documentation Requirements

Accurate and comprehensive documentation of all calibration and maintenance activities is paramount for regulatory compliance, traceability, and effective equipment management. All records must be legible, indelible, and readily retrievable.

### 4.1 Calibration Records

Each piece of calibrated equipment shall have a dedicated calibration record or logbook, either in physical or electronic format. This record must include:

*   **Equipment Identification:** Unique equipment ID, name, and manufacturer.
*   **Date of Calibration:** The date the calibration was performed.
*   **Calibration Performed By:** Name and signature/electronic signature of the person performing the calibration.
*   **Calibration Standard(s) Used:** Identification of all reference standards used, including their lot numbers, expiry dates, and traceability information.
*   **Environmental Conditions:** Temperature, humidity, and any other relevant environmental parameters during calibration.
*   **Raw Data:** All raw data generated during the calibration process.
*   **Results:** Calculated results and comparison against acceptance criteria.
*   **Pass/Fail Status:** Clear indication of whether the equipment passed or failed calibration.
*   **Adjustments Made:** Details of any adjustments made to the equipment.
*   **Next Calibration Due Date:** The date when the next calibration is due.
*   **Review and Approval:** Name and signature/electronic signature of the supervisor or designated reviewer, along with the review date.
*   **Deviation/Investigation Reference:** Reference to any deviations or investigations initiated due to out-of-calibration results.

### 4.2 Maintenance Records

Maintenance records shall be maintained for each piece of equipment and include:

*   **Equipment Identification:** Unique equipment ID, name, and manufacturer.
*   **Date of Maintenance:** The date the maintenance was performed.
*   **Maintenance Performed By:** Name and signature/electronic signature of the person performing the maintenance.
*   **Type of Maintenance:** Indication of whether it was preventative or corrective maintenance.
*   **Description of Work:** Detailed description of the maintenance performed, including parts replaced or repairs made.
*   **Troubleshooting Steps:** For corrective maintenance, a summary of troubleshooting steps taken.
*   **Post-Maintenance Testing:** Results of any functional tests or performance verifications conducted after maintenance.
*   **Downtime:** Duration the equipment was out of service.
*   **Review and Approval:** Name and signature/electronic signature of the supervisor or designated reviewer, along with the review date.

### 4.3 Equipment Logbooks

Each major piece of equipment shall have a dedicated logbook (physical or electronic) where daily usage, minor checks, and any unusual observations are recorded. This provides a continuous history of the equipment's operation.

### 4.4 Record Retention

All calibration and maintenance records shall be retained for a period consistent with regulatory requirements (e.g., 5 years or as specified by local regulations) and company policy. Records shall be stored in a secure location that protects them from damage, loss, or unauthorized access.




## 5. Training Requirements

All personnel involved in the calibration, maintenance, and operation of laboratory equipment must receive adequate training to ensure competency and adherence to this SOP. Training records shall be maintained for all personnel.

### 5.1 Initial Training

New personnel, or personnel newly assigned to roles involving laboratory equipment, must undergo initial training covering:

*   **SOP Review:** Thorough understanding of this SOP (SOP-QC-006) and other relevant equipment-specific SOPs.
*   **Equipment Operation:** Proper operation of each piece of equipment they will be using, including start-up, shut-down, and routine handling.
*   **Calibration Procedures:** Hands-on training on the specific calibration procedures for the equipment they will be calibrating, including the use of standards and data recording.
*   **Maintenance Procedures:** Training on routine preventative maintenance tasks relevant to their role.
*   **Documentation:** Proper completion of all calibration and maintenance forms and logbooks.
*   **Safety:** Awareness of all safety considerations related to the equipment and laboratory environment.
*   **Regulatory Awareness:** Basic understanding of relevant regulatory requirements (FDA, USDA, cGMP) pertaining to equipment qualification and data integrity.

### 5.2 Refresher Training

Refresher training shall be conducted periodically (e.g., annually or biennially) to ensure continued competency and to address any updates to SOPs, equipment, or regulatory requirements. The frequency of refresher training may vary based on the complexity of the equipment and the criticality of the tasks.

### 5.3 Training Documentation

All training activities must be documented. Training records shall include:

*   **Trainee Information:** Name, employee ID, and department of the trainee.
*   **Trainer Information:** Name and qualifications of the trainer.
*   **Training Date:** Date(s) of training.
*   **Training Content:** A clear description of the topics covered and the SOPs reviewed.
*   **Assessment:** Evidence of trainee comprehension and competency (e.g., written test, practical demonstration, or supervisor sign-off).
*   **Signatures:** Signatures of both the trainee and the trainer.




## 6. Quality Control Measures

Effective quality control measures are essential to ensure the ongoing accuracy and reliability of laboratory equipment and the data generated. These measures go beyond routine calibration and maintenance to include systematic monitoring and verification.

### 6.1 Calibration Verification Checks

In addition to full calibrations, routine calibration verification checks shall be performed at specified intervals (e.g., daily, weekly) using appropriate check standards. These checks provide an immediate assessment of equipment performance between full calibrations. If a verification check fails, the equipment must be taken out of service, investigated, and recalibrated if necessary.

### 6.2 Trend Analysis of Calibration Data

Calibration data shall be systematically collected and trended over time. This involves plotting key calibration parameters (e.g., slopes, intercepts, recovery percentages) to identify any gradual drifts or shifts in equipment performance. Trend analysis can help predict potential equipment failures, optimize calibration frequencies, and provide early warning of issues that may impact data quality. Any significant trends or out-of-trend conditions must be investigated and documented.

### 6.3 Inter-Instrument Comparison

Where multiple instruments of the same type are used (e.g., multiple HPLCs or balances), periodic inter-instrument comparison studies shall be conducted. This involves analyzing the same sample on different instruments and comparing the results to ensure consistency and comparability across the laboratory. Significant discrepancies must be investigated.

### 6.4 Proficiency Testing

Participation in external proficiency testing programs (if available and relevant) is highly recommended. These programs provide an independent assessment of the laboratory's analytical capabilities and the performance of its equipment. Results from proficiency tests shall be reviewed, and any unsatisfactory results investigated and corrective actions implemented.

### 6.5 System Suitability Tests (SST)

For analytical methods, System Suitability Tests (SSTs) are an integral part of quality control. SSTs are performed before or during the analysis of samples to ensure that the chromatographic system (e.g., HPLC, GC) is functioning correctly and capable of producing results of acceptable quality. SST parameters typically include resolution, tailing factor, plate count, and reproducibility of replicate injections. Equipment must pass SST criteria before sample analysis can proceed.

### 6.6 Audit and Self-Inspection

Regular internal audits and self-inspections shall be conducted to assess compliance with this SOP and other relevant quality system documents. These audits should include a review of calibration and maintenance records, equipment status, and personnel training. Findings from audits shall be documented, and corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) implemented as needed.




## 7. Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount during all laboratory operations, including equipment calibration and maintenance. All personnel must adhere to general laboratory safety rules and specific safety precautions related to the equipment and chemicals being handled.

### 7.1 General Laboratory Safety

*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Always wear appropriate PPE, including laboratory coats, safety glasses, and gloves, as required by the specific task or chemical being handled.
*   **Chemical Handling:** Follow all safety data sheet (SDS) guidelines for the safe handling, storage, and disposal of chemicals, including calibration standards and cleaning agents. Work in a fume hood when handling volatile or hazardous chemicals.
*   **Electrical Safety:** Exercise caution when working with electrical equipment. Ensure equipment is properly grounded and inspect power cords for damage. Disconnect power before performing any maintenance or repair work that involves opening equipment casings.
*   **Emergency Procedures:** Be familiar with the location of safety showers, eyewash stations, fire extinguishers, and emergency exits. Know the procedures for responding to chemical spills, fires, and other emergencies.
*   **Ergonomics:** Use proper lifting techniques when moving heavy equipment or calibration weights to prevent injuries. Arrange workstations ergonomically to minimize strain.

### 7.2 Equipment-Specific Safety

*   **High-Pressure Systems:** For equipment involving high-pressure gases (e.g., GC), ensure gas cylinders are secured, regulators are properly installed, and lines are leak-free. Follow procedures for safe handling of compressed gases.
*   **High-Temperature Equipment:** Exercise caution when working with ovens, incubators, or other high-temperature equipment. Allow equipment to cool before handling internal components.
*   **Moving Parts:** Be aware of moving parts in equipment (e.g., centrifuges, dissolution apparatus) and ensure all safety guards are in place before operation.
*   **UV Light:** When working with UV spectrophotometers or transilluminators, use appropriate UV protective eyewear and minimize exposure to UV radiation.
*   **Waste Disposal:** Dispose of all chemical waste, including spent calibration solutions and cleaning agents, according to established hazardous waste disposal procedures.




## 8. Regulatory Compliance References

This SOP is developed in accordance with, and references, the following key regulatory guidelines and standards applicable to pharmaceutical manufacturing, particularly for large animal pharmaceuticals. Adherence to these regulations ensures the quality, safety, and efficacy of products and the integrity of laboratory operations.

### 8.1 U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

*   **21 CFR Part 211 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation outlines the minimum current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) requirements for the methods, facilities, and controls used in the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product. Specific sections relevant to equipment include:
    *   **Subpart C - Buildings and Facilities (211.42):** Requires facilities to be designed to prevent contamination and facilitate cleaning, maintenance, and proper operations.
    *   **Subpart D - Equipment (211.63 - 211.72):** Details requirements for equipment design, construction, location, calibration, maintenance, and cleaning. It mandates that equipment be of appropriate design, adequate size, and suitably located to facilitate operations, cleaning, and maintenance. It also requires calibration at suitable intervals and the maintenance of written records of calibration and maintenance.
    *   **Subpart J - Records and Reports (211.180 - 211.198):** Specifies requirements for maintaining accurate and complete records, including equipment cleaning and maintenance logs, and calibration records.
*   **FDA Guidance for Industry: Analytical Procedures and Methods Validation for Drugs and Biologics (2015):** Provides recommendations on developing and validating analytical methods, which inherently relies on properly calibrated and maintained equipment.
*   **FDA Guidance for Industry: Process Validation: General Principles and Practices (2011):** Emphasizes the importance of equipment qualification as part of process validation.

### 8.2 U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)

*   **9 CFR Part 101 - 118 - Virus, Serum, Toxin, and Analogous Products; Organisms and Vectors:** For veterinary biologics, the USDA regulates the manufacture and testing of these products. While not as prescriptive on equipment as FDA cGMPs, the general principles of quality systems, including equipment control, are implicitly required to ensure product safety and efficacy. Manufacturers are expected to have adequate facilities and equipment to produce safe and effective products.

### 8.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP)

*   **General cGMP Principles:** Beyond specific regulations, the overarching principles of cGMP emphasize that manufacturing processes are well-controlled, that quality is built into the product, and that testing is performed to ensure product quality. This includes:
    *   **Equipment Qualification:** Ensuring that equipment is suitable for its intended use and performs as expected (Installation Qualification (IQ), Operational Qualification (OQ), Performance Qualification (PQ)).
    *   **Validation:** Calibration and maintenance are integral parts of the overall validation lifecycle for equipment and processes.
    *   **Data Integrity:** Ensuring that all data generated by equipment is accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and available (ALCOA+ principles).

### 8.4 International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

*   **ISO/IEC 17025:2017 - General requirements for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories:** While not a direct regulatory requirement for pharmaceutical manufacturing, adherence to ISO 17025 principles for calibration laboratories provides a robust framework for ensuring the competence and impartiality of laboratories, including their equipment management systems. Many pharmaceutical companies adopt elements of this standard for their internal QC labs.




## 9. Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to this SOP, or to the calibration and maintenance procedures for laboratory equipment, must be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are evaluated for their potential impact on product quality, data integrity, and regulatory compliance, and are properly documented, approved, and implemented.

### 9.1 Initiating a Change

Any personnel identifying a need for a change (e.g., new equipment, revised calibration frequency, updated regulatory requirement, improvement in procedure) shall initiate a Change Request form. The form shall include:

*   **Description of Change:** Clear and concise description of the proposed change.
*   **Reason for Change:** Justification for the proposed change.
*   **Impact Assessment:** Initial assessment of the potential impact of the change on equipment performance, data quality, and regulatory compliance.
*   **Proposed Implementation Plan:** Outline of how the change will be implemented.

### 9.2 Review and Approval of Changes

All Change Request forms shall be reviewed by relevant stakeholders, including the QC Manager, Laboratory Supervisors, Metrology/Calibration personnel, and Quality Assurance (QA). The review process shall assess:

*   **Necessity and Justification:** Is the change necessary and adequately justified?
*   **Risk Assessment:** What are the potential risks associated with the change, and how can they be mitigated?
*   **Validation/Qualification Requirements:** Does the change necessitate re-validation or re-qualification of equipment or processes?
*   **Training Requirements:** Will personnel require additional training to implement the change?
*   **Documentation Updates:** What documents (e.g., other SOPs, forms, specifications) will need to be updated as a result of the change?

Changes must be approved by all designated approvers before implementation. For significant changes, approval from Quality Assurance is mandatory.

### 9.3 Implementation of Changes

Once approved, the change shall be implemented according to the approved plan. This includes:

*   **Updating Documentation:** Revising this SOP and any other affected documents.
*   **Training Personnel:** Ensuring all affected personnel are trained on the revised procedures.
*   **Executing the Change:** Performing the actual change (e.g., installing new equipment, modifying a calibration method).
*   **Verification of Effectiveness:** Verifying that the implemented change is effective and does not introduce new problems. This may involve re-calibration, performance verification, or re-qualification.

### 9.4 Documentation of Changes

All change control activities, including the Change Request form, impact assessments, review comments, approvals, and verification of effectiveness, shall be thoroughly documented and maintained as part of the quality system records. A new version of the SOP shall be issued with each approved change, and the version history clearly documented.
