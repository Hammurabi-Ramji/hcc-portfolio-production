This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the guidelines and procedures for establishing and maintaining robust security and access control measures within pharmaceutical facilities, particularly those involved in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The purpose of this SOP is to safeguard personnel, assets, sensitive information, and proprietary processes from unauthorized access, theft, damage, or misuse. It aims to ensure a secure environment that complies with all applicable regulatory requirements, including those set forth by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), United States Department of Agriculture (USDA), and current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP). This SOP applies to all employees, contractors, visitors, and any other individuals requiring access to the pharmaceutical facility and its controlled areas. It covers physical security, logical access to systems and data, and the procedures for managing and monitoring access throughout the facility.



## 2. Responsibilities

**2.1. Facility Management:**
*   Responsible for the overall implementation and maintenance of physical security infrastructure, including access control systems, surveillance equipment, and perimeter security.
*   Ensures that all security systems are regularly inspected, maintained, and calibrated.
*   Collaborates with IT and Security departments to integrate physical and logical security measures.

**2.2. Security Department:**
*   Develops, implements, and enforces security policies and procedures, including this SOP.
*   Manages and monitors access control systems, issues and revokes access credentials, and maintains access logs.
*   Conducts regular security patrols, monitors surveillance systems, and responds to security incidents.
*   Investigates security breaches and incidents, and reports findings to relevant management and authorities.
*   Provides security awareness training to all personnel.

**2.3. Information Technology (IT) Department:**
*   Responsible for the security of all IT systems, networks, and data, including logical access controls.
*   Manages user accounts, passwords, and access permissions to computer systems and sensitive data.
*   Implements and maintains cybersecurity measures, such as firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and data encryption.
*   Ensures regular data backups and disaster recovery plans are in place and tested.
*   Collaborates with the Security department on integrated security solutions.

**2.4. Department Heads/Supervisors:**
*   Responsible for ensuring their personnel understand and adhere to all security and access control procedures.
*   Approves and reviews access requests for their respective departments.
*   Reports any security concerns, incidents, or suspicious activities to the Security Department immediately.
*   Ensures that new employees receive appropriate security training and that existing employees receive refresher training.

**2.5. All Employees, Contractors, and Visitors:**
*   Adhere to all security and access control policies and procedures outlined in this SOP.
*   Wear and display appropriate identification badges at all times within the facility.
*   Report any lost or stolen access credentials immediately to the Security Department.
*   Challenge unauthorized individuals and report their presence to the Security Department.
*   Participate in security training programs as required.
*   Do not share access credentials or allow unauthorized individuals to use their credentials.





## 3. Procedures

### 3.1. Access Control System Management

**3.1.1. System Administration:**
*   The Security Department, in conjunction with the IT Department, shall be responsible for the administration and maintenance of the electronic access control system (EACS) and associated hardware and software.
*   All EACS servers, databases, and network components shall be secured in a restricted-access server room with environmental controls and redundant power supplies.
*   Regular backups of the EACS database, including access logs and user profiles, shall be performed daily and stored off-site in a secure location. Backups shall be tested quarterly to ensure data integrity and recoverability.
*   Software updates and patches for the EACS shall be applied promptly to address security vulnerabilities and ensure optimal performance. All updates shall be tested in a non-production environment prior to deployment.

**3.1.2. Access Level Configuration:**
*   Access levels within the EACS shall be configured based on job function, department, and security clearance. The principle of least privilege shall be applied, granting individuals only the minimum access necessary to perform their duties.
*   Access level requests and modifications shall be submitted in writing (e.g., via an approved electronic request form) by the respective Department Head/Supervisor to the Security Department.
*   All access level changes shall be documented, including the date of change, the individual requesting the change, the reason for the change, and the individual implementing the change.

**3.1.3. Credential Management:**
*   All personnel requiring access to the facility shall be issued a unique access credential (e.g., proximity card, biometric data).
*   Access credentials shall be activated only after the individual has completed the required security awareness training and acknowledged understanding of this SOP.
*   Lost, stolen, or damaged access credentials shall be reported immediately to the Security Department. The compromised credential shall be deactivated within one (1) hour of notification.
*   Upon termination of employment or contract, access credentials shall be immediately deactivated by the Security Department. A documented process for the return or destruction of physical credentials shall be followed.
*   Access credentials shall be subject to periodic review (at least annually) to ensure that assigned access levels remain appropriate for current job functions.





### 3.2. Personnel Access Procedures

**3.2.1. New Employee Access:**
*   Upon hiring, new employees shall complete a security clearance process, which may include background checks, as per company policy.
*   Prior to receiving facility access, new employees must complete mandatory security awareness training, covering topics such as this SOP, emergency procedures, and reporting security incidents.
*   The Human Resources (HR) Department shall notify the Security Department of new hires, providing necessary information for access credential issuance and access level assignment based on their job function.
*   Access credentials shall be issued by the Security Department only after completion of training and approval from the respective Department Head.

**3.2.2. Employee Access Changes:**
*   Any changes in an employee's job function, department, or security clearance that necessitate a change in access levels shall be communicated by the Department Head/Supervisor to the Security Department.
*   The Security Department shall update the employee's access profile in the EACS within one (1) business day of receiving the approved request.
*   For temporary access needs (e.g., project-specific access), a temporary access request form shall be submitted, specifying the duration and areas of access. Temporary access shall be automatically revoked upon expiration.

**3.2.3. Employee Exit Procedures:**
*   Upon an employee's termination or resignation, the HR Department shall immediately notify the Security Department.
*   The Security Department shall deactivate all access credentials and remove the employee's access profile from the EACS immediately upon notification of termination or at the end of the last working day for resignations.
*   Physical access credentials (e.g., badges) shall be collected by HR or Security as part of the off-boarding process.





### 3.3. Visitor Access Procedures

**3.3.1. Visitor Registration:**
*   All visitors, including vendors, contractors, and guests, must register at the main security desk upon arrival.
*   Visitors shall present a valid government-issued photo identification for verification. A record of the visitor's name, organization, purpose of visit, date, and time of entry/exit shall be maintained.
*   Visitors shall be required to sign a confidentiality agreement if their visit involves access to sensitive areas or information.

**3.3.2. Visitor Escort and Badging:**
*   All visitors must be escorted by an authorized employee at all times while within the facility, unless otherwise approved by the Security Department for specific, pre-approved contractors.
*   Visitors shall be issued a temporary visitor badge, which must be visibly displayed at all times. The badge shall clearly indicate that the individual is a visitor.
*   The escorting employee is responsible for the visitor's adherence to all facility rules and security procedures.

**3.3.3. Visitor Departure:**
*   Upon departure, visitors shall return their temporary badges to the main security desk.
*   The security personnel shall record the time of departure and ensure all visitor logs are accurately updated.





### 3.4. Material and Equipment Access Procedures

**3.4.1. Incoming Materials:**
*   All incoming materials, including raw materials, components, and supplies, shall be received at a designated receiving area.
*   Receiving personnel shall verify the contents against purchase orders and inspect for any signs of tampering or damage.
*   Access to the receiving area shall be restricted to authorized personnel only.
*   Materials requiring specific environmental controls (e.g., temperature-sensitive pharmaceuticals) shall be immediately transferred to appropriate secure storage areas.

**3.4.2. Outgoing Materials and Equipment:**
*   All outgoing materials, including finished products, waste, and equipment, shall be processed through designated exit points.
*   Authorization for removal of materials or equipment must be obtained from the relevant department head and verified by security personnel.
*   A log of all outgoing materials and equipment, including description, quantity, destination, and authorization, shall be maintained.

**3.4.3. Restricted Equipment Access:**
*   Access to specialized or high-value equipment shall be controlled through additional security measures, such as keycard access, biometric scanners, or physical locks.
*   Only trained and authorized personnel shall be granted access to operate or maintain such equipment.
*   Maintenance and calibration records for restricted equipment shall be securely stored and regularly reviewed.





### 3.5. Security Monitoring and Surveillance

**3.5.1. CCTV Surveillance:**
*   Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) cameras shall be strategically placed throughout the facility, covering all entry/exit points, critical manufacturing areas, storage facilities, and perimeter boundaries.
*   CCTV systems shall operate 24/7, with continuous recording capabilities. Recordings shall be stored for a minimum of 90 days, or longer as required by regulatory bodies or internal policies.
*   Access to live and recorded CCTV footage shall be restricted to authorized Security Department personnel only. All access to footage shall be logged.
*   CCTV cameras shall be regularly inspected and maintained to ensure optimal functionality and image quality.

**3.5.2. Alarm Systems:**
*   Intrusion detection alarm systems shall be installed at all vulnerable points, including doors, windows, and critical access areas.
*   Alarm systems shall be monitored 24/7 by the Security Department or a designated third-party monitoring service.
*   All alarm activations shall be immediately investigated by security personnel, and appropriate response protocols shall be initiated.
*   Regular testing of alarm systems shall be conducted to ensure their effectiveness.

**3.5.3. Security Patrols:**
*   Security personnel shall conduct regular, documented patrols of the facility perimeter and interior at varying intervals to deter unauthorized activities and identify potential security breaches.
*   Patrol routes and schedules shall be designed to cover all critical areas and shall be subject to periodic review and modification.
*   Any suspicious activities or security vulnerabilities observed during patrols shall be immediately reported and documented.





### 3.6. Incident Response and Reporting

**3.6.1. Security Incident Definition:**
*   A security incident is defined as any event that compromises or attempts to compromise the security of the facility, personnel, assets, or information. This includes, but is not limited to, unauthorized access, theft, vandalism, suspicious activities, system breaches, or loss of access credentials.

**3.6.2. Reporting Procedures:**
*   All personnel are responsible for immediately reporting any observed or suspected security incidents to the Security Department.
*   Reports can be made via designated emergency contact numbers, internal security hotlines, or directly to security personnel.
*   For critical incidents, immediate notification to senior management and relevant external authorities (e.g., law enforcement) shall be made as per the Emergency Response Plan.

**3.6.3. Incident Investigation and Response:**
*   Upon receiving a security incident report, the Security Department shall initiate an immediate investigation to assess the nature and scope of the incident.
*   The investigation shall include gathering evidence, interviewing witnesses, reviewing surveillance footage, and analyzing access logs.
*   Appropriate response measures shall be implemented to contain the incident, mitigate further damage, and restore security. This may involve coordinating with law enforcement, IT, and other relevant departments.
*   All security incidents, investigations, and response actions shall be thoroughly documented in a Security Incident Report. The report shall include details of the incident, findings of the investigation, actions taken, and recommendations for preventing future occurrences.





### 3.7. Data Security and System Access

**3.7.1. User Account Management:**
*   All users requiring access to the facility's IT systems and data shall be assigned a unique user ID and password.
*   User accounts shall be created, modified, and deactivated by the IT Department based on authorized requests from Department Heads/Supervisors.
*   Access privileges shall be granted based on the principle of least privilege, ensuring users only have access to the data and systems necessary for their job functions.
*   Regular reviews of user access privileges shall be conducted (at least quarterly) to ensure they remain appropriate and to identify and remove any unnecessary access.

**3.7.2. Password Policy:**
*   All system users shall adhere to a strong password policy, requiring a minimum length of 12 characters, including a combination of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters.
*   Passwords shall be changed every 90 days and users shall not reuse previous passwords.
*   Multi-factor authentication (MFA) shall be implemented for access to critical systems and sensitive data.

**3.7.3. Data Access and Protection:**
*   Access to sensitive data, including manufacturing records, quality control data, and patient information, shall be restricted to authorized personnel only.
*   Data shall be classified based on its sensitivity, and appropriate security controls (e.g., encryption, access controls) shall be applied.
*   All data transfers, both internal and external, shall utilize secure protocols (e.g., SFTP, VPN).
*   Regular data backups shall be performed and stored securely off-site to ensure data availability and recovery in case of system failure or disaster.

**3.7.4. System Monitoring and Audit Trails:**
*   All access to IT systems and data shall be logged and monitored. Audit trails shall capture user activities, including login/logout times, data access, and system modifications.
*   Audit logs shall be reviewed regularly by the IT Department for suspicious activities or unauthorized access attempts.
*   Any anomalies or security breaches identified in the audit logs shall be immediately reported to the Security Department and investigated.





## 4. Documentation Requirements

All activities related to security and access control, as outlined in this SOP, shall be thoroughly documented. Accurate and complete documentation is essential for demonstrating compliance with regulatory requirements, facilitating investigations, and ensuring continuous improvement of security measures. The following documentation shall be maintained:

*   **Access Control System Logs:** Detailed records of all entries and exits, including date, time, individual, and access point. These logs shall be retained for a minimum of five (5) years.
*   **Visitor Logs:** Records of all visitors, including name, organization, purpose of visit, escorting employee, and entry/exit times. Retained for a minimum of three (3) years.
*   **Access Credential Issuance and Deactivation Records:** Documentation of all issued, lost, stolen, damaged, and deactivated access credentials, including dates and reasons. Retained for the duration of the employee's tenure plus two (2) years.
*   **Security Incident Reports:** Comprehensive reports detailing all security incidents, investigations, findings, and corrective actions taken. Retained for a minimum of ten (10) years.
*   **Security System Maintenance and Calibration Records:** Records of all inspections, maintenance, and calibration activities performed on security systems (CCTV, alarms, EACS). Retained for the lifetime of the equipment plus two (2) years.
*   **Security Training Records:** Documentation of all security awareness and specific security procedure training provided to employees, including attendance, dates, and training content. Retained for the duration of the employee's tenure plus two (2) years.
*   **Access Level Review Records:** Documentation of periodic reviews of access levels for all personnel. Retained for a minimum of five (5) years.
*   **Audit Trails for IT Systems:** Logs of all user activities, system access, and data modifications within IT systems. Retained as per data retention policies, typically a minimum of five (5) years.
*   **Change Control Documentation:** All requests, approvals, and implementations of changes to security policies, procedures, or systems. Retained indefinitely.

All documentation shall be stored in a secure and easily retrievable manner, either electronically or in hard copy, protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction. Electronic records shall comply with 21 CFR Part 11 requirements for electronic signatures and records.



## 5. Training Requirements

Effective security and access control are dependent on the knowledge and adherence of all personnel. Therefore, comprehensive training programs shall be implemented and regularly updated. All training shall be documented, and records maintained as per Section 4.0, Documentation Requirements.

**5.1. Initial Security Awareness Training:**
*   All new employees, contractors, and long-term visitors shall undergo mandatory initial security awareness training prior to being granted unsupervised access to the facility or IT systems.
*   This training shall cover:
    *   The importance of security and access control in a pharmaceutical environment.
    *   Overview of this SOP and other relevant security policies.
    *   Procedures for proper use and safeguarding of access credentials.
    *   Reporting procedures for lost/stolen credentials, suspicious activities, and security incidents.
    *   Emergency response procedures related to security breaches.
    *   Basic data security practices and password policies.

**5.2. Annual Refresher Training:**
*   All employees and contractors shall undergo annual refresher security awareness training to reinforce key concepts, communicate updates to policies or procedures, and address emerging security threats.
*   This training may be conducted through online modules, in-person sessions, or a combination thereof.

**5.3. Role-Specific Training:**
*   Personnel with specific security responsibilities (e.g., Security Department staff, IT personnel managing access systems, Department Heads approving access) shall receive specialized training relevant to their roles.
*   This training shall cover the technical aspects of access control systems, incident response protocols, data security best practices, and regulatory compliance specific to their functions.

**5.4. Training Effectiveness Evaluation:**
*   The effectiveness of security training programs shall be periodically evaluated through methods such as quizzes, practical exercises, or observation of adherence to procedures.
*   Feedback from training evaluations shall be used to improve training content and delivery methods.





## 6. Quality Control Measures

To ensure the continuous effectiveness and compliance of security and access control measures, the following quality control measures shall be implemented:

**6.1. Regular Audits and Inspections:**
*   The Security Department, in conjunction with Quality Assurance (QA), shall conduct regular, scheduled internal audits and inspections of all security and access control systems, procedures, and documentation.
*   These audits shall verify adherence to this SOP, identify any deviations or non-conformances, and assess the overall effectiveness of security measures.
*   External audits by regulatory bodies (e.g., FDA, USDA) or third-party security experts shall be facilitated and their findings addressed promptly.

**6.2. Performance Monitoring:**
*   Key performance indicators (KPIs) related to security and access control shall be established and regularly monitored. These may include:
    *   Number of security incidents reported.
    *   Response times to security alarms and incidents.
    *   Compliance rates for access credential usage and return.
    *   Effectiveness of security awareness training (e.g., through phishing test results).
    *   Timeliness of access level reviews and updates.

**6.3. Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA):**
*   Any non-conformances, deviations, or security breaches identified through audits, inspections, or incident reports shall trigger a formal Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) process.
*   The CAPA process shall involve investigating the root cause of the issue, implementing corrective actions to address the immediate problem, and implementing preventive actions to avoid recurrence.
*   All CAPA activities shall be documented and their effectiveness verified.

**6.4. Management Review:**
*   The effectiveness of the security and access control program, including audit findings, incident trends, and KPI performance, shall be reviewed by senior management at least annually.
*   This review shall assess the suitability and effectiveness of the security management system and identify opportunities for continuous improvement.





## 7. Safety Considerations

Security and access control measures must be implemented with due consideration for the safety and well-being of all personnel within the pharmaceutical facility. This section outlines key safety considerations related to security operations.

**7.1. Emergency Egress and Evacuation:**
*   Access control systems shall be designed and maintained to ensure unimpeded emergency egress from all areas of the facility. In the event of a power failure or emergency, all access-controlled doors leading to emergency exits must automatically unlock or be capable of manual override from the inside.
*   Emergency exit routes shall be clearly marked, well-lit, and kept free from obstructions at all times.
*   Regular emergency evacuation drills shall be conducted to ensure all personnel are familiar with evacuation procedures and emergency assembly points.

**7.2. Response to Security Incidents with Safety Implications:**
*   Security personnel responding to incidents (e.g., unauthorized entry, suspicious packages, active threats) shall prioritize the safety of personnel. Procedures for engaging with potentially dangerous situations shall be clearly defined and regularly trained upon.
*   Coordination with local emergency services (police, fire, ambulance) shall be established and maintained for incidents requiring external assistance.

**7.3. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):**
*   Security personnel, and any other personnel involved in security-related tasks that may expose them to hazards (e.g., patrolling manufacturing areas, responding to spills), shall be provided with and trained in the proper use of necessary Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) as per facility safety guidelines.

**7.4. Handling of Suspicious Individuals/Packages:**
*   Procedures for identifying, reporting, and safely handling suspicious individuals or packages shall be clearly communicated to all personnel. This includes maintaining a safe distance and awaiting trained security or law enforcement response.

**7.5. Training on Safety Protocols:**
*   Security training shall incorporate relevant safety protocols, including fire safety, hazardous material awareness, and emergency first aid, to ensure security personnel can respond effectively to a wide range of incidents.





## 8. Regulatory Compliance References

This Standard Operating Procedure is developed in accordance with, and references, the following key regulatory guidelines and standards applicable to pharmaceutical manufacturing, particularly for large animal pharmaceutical operations:

**8.1. U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Regulations:**
*   **21 CFR Part 210 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Processing, Packing, or Holding of Drugs; General:** This part outlines general cGMP regulations for drug products, including requirements for facilities and controls to prevent contamination and ensure product quality. Security measures contribute to maintaining controlled environments.
*   **21 CFR Part 211 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This part details specific cGMP requirements for finished pharmaceuticals. Relevant sections include:
    *   **Subpart C - Buildings and Facilities (21 CFR 211.42):** Requires facilities to be designed to prevent contamination and unauthorized access. Security and access control are critical for maintaining the integrity of manufacturing and storage areas.
    *   **Subpart D - Equipment (21 CFR 211.68):** Addresses controls over automated equipment, which often involves logical access controls and data security.
    *   **Subpart J - Records and Reports (21 CFR 211.180 - 211.198):** Emphasizes the need for accurate and secure record-keeping, which necessitates robust data security and access controls for electronic records.
*   **21 CFR Part 11 - Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures:** This regulation sets forth the criteria under which the FDA considers electronic records, electronic signatures, and handwritten signatures executed to electronic records to be trustworthy, reliable, and generally equivalent to paper records and handwritten signatures executed on paper. This directly impacts logical access control, audit trails, and data integrity for electronic systems used in security and manufacturing.

**8.2. U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) Regulations (Applicable for Veterinary Biologics/Animal Health Products):**
*   **9 CFR Part 101 - Definitions:** Provides definitions relevant to veterinary biologics.
*   **9 CFR Part 104 - Permits for Biological Products:** Outlines requirements for facilities and operations involved in the production of veterinary biologics. While less prescriptive on security than FDA human drug regulations, the general principles of facility control and prevention of contamination are supported by strong security measures.
*   **9 CFR Part 108 - Suspension, Revocation, or Termination of Licenses or Permits:** Non-compliance with facility and operational standards, which includes security breaches, can lead to regulatory action.

**8.3. Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) Guidelines (General Principles):**
*   **Facility Security:** cGMP principles inherently require facilities to be secure to prevent adulteration, contamination, and mix-ups. This includes physical barriers, access control systems, and monitoring.
*   **Personnel Control:** Ensuring that only authorized and trained personnel have access to critical areas and systems is a fundamental cGMP requirement.
*   **Documentation and Record Keeping:** Accurate and secure documentation of all manufacturing and quality control activities, including security logs and audit trails, is central to cGMP compliance.
*   **Data Integrity:** A core cGMP expectation, data integrity (ALCOA+ principles: Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate, Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available) is heavily reliant on robust logical access controls and system security.

This SOP aims to integrate these regulatory requirements into practical, enforceable procedures to ensure the highest standards of security and compliance for pharmaceutical manufacturing operations.



## 9. Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to this Standard Operating Procedure, or to any security and access control systems, equipment, or procedures that could impact the security posture of the pharmaceutical facility, shall be managed through a formal change control process. This ensures that all changes are evaluated, approved, implemented, and documented in a controlled manner, minimizing risks and maintaining regulatory compliance.

**9.1. Initiation of Change:**
*   Any employee or department identifying a need for a change to this SOP or related security systems/procedures shall initiate a Change Request (CR) form.
*   The CR form shall clearly describe the proposed change, the reason for the change, the anticipated impact (e.g., on security, operations, compliance), and the proposed implementation timeline.

**9.2. Review and Assessment:**
*   The Change Control Board (CCB), comprising representatives from Security, IT, Quality Assurance, and relevant operational departments, shall review the Change Request.
*   The CCB shall assess the potential risks associated with the proposed change, including its impact on personnel safety, data integrity, product quality, and regulatory compliance.
*   A risk assessment shall be conducted for significant changes.

**9.3. Approval:**
*   Changes shall be approved by all relevant stakeholders on the CCB, including a final approval from Quality Assurance to ensure compliance.
*   No change shall be implemented without documented approval.

**9.4. Implementation:**
*   Approved changes shall be implemented according to a defined plan, which may include:
    *   Updating relevant documentation (e.g., this SOP, system configurations).
    *   Training affected personnel on the new procedures or system functionalities.
    *   Testing the implemented change to verify its effectiveness and ensure no unintended negative impacts.
*   All implementation activities shall be documented.

**9.5. Post-Implementation Review:**
*   Following implementation, a post-implementation review shall be conducted to confirm that the change achieved its intended objective and did not introduce new issues.
*   The effectiveness of the change shall be monitored for a defined period.

**9.6. Version Control:**
*   This SOP shall be subject to strict version control. Each revision shall be assigned a new version number, and the effective date shall be updated.
*   Previous versions of the SOP shall be archived and readily retrievable for historical reference and audit purposes.
