# Standard Operating Procedure

## SOP-REG-006: Import/Export Documentation for Animal Health Products

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 2025-08-12
**Category:** Regulatory
**Compliance Areas:** FDA, USDA, cGMP

## 1.0 Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the procedures for the accurate and compliant preparation, submission, and maintenance of import and export documentation for animal health products. This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the import and export of animal health products, including but not limited to, regulatory affairs, quality assurance, supply chain, and logistics departments. The scope includes all animal drugs, medicated feeds, animal devices, and animal-derived materials intended for use in animals, ensuring adherence to regulations set forth by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), and current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP).

## 2.0 Responsibilities

*   **Regulatory Affairs Department:** Responsible for staying updated on all relevant import/export regulations, providing guidance on documentation requirements, reviewing and approving all regulatory submissions, and liaising with regulatory authorities (FDA, USDA). 
*   **Quality Assurance Department:** Responsible for ensuring that all products meet quality standards, reviewing and approving documentation related to product quality, and ensuring compliance with cGMP guidelines for import/export operations. 
*   **Supply Chain and Logistics Department:** Responsible for the physical movement of goods, preparing initial shipping and receiving documentation, coordinating with customs brokers and freight forwarders, and ensuring proper handling and storage of products during transit. 
*   **All Personnel Involved:** Responsible for adhering to the procedures outlined in this SOP, completing all required documentation accurately and in a timely manner, and reporting any deviations or issues to their respective supervisors.

## 3.0 Detailed Procedures

### 3.1 Import Procedures

#### 3.1.1 Pre-Importation Assessment

1.  **Determine Product Classification:** Identify whether the animal health product is an animal drug, medicated feed, animal device, or animal-derived material. This classification dictates the specific regulatory requirements.
2.  **Identify Regulatory Requirements:** Consult FDA and USDA regulations applicable to the specific product classification. This includes reviewing FDA guidance documents for animal drugs, feeds, and devices, and USDA APHIS requirements for animal products and byproducts.
3.  **Verify Manufacturer Compliance:** Ensure that the foreign manufacturer of the animal health product complies with relevant cGMP regulations and any specific FDA or USDA requirements for foreign facilities.
4.  **Obtain Necessary Permits/Licenses:** Determine if any import permits from USDA APHIS or other licenses are required for the specific product. Apply for and obtain these permits well in advance of shipment.

#### 3.1.2 Documentation Preparation for Import

1.  **Commercial Invoice:** Prepare a detailed commercial invoice including:
    *   Seller and buyer information
    *   Product description (including common name, scientific name, and intended use)
    *   Quantity and unit of measure
    *   Unit price and total value
    *   Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) code
    *   Country of origin
    *   Terms of sale (Incoterms)
2.  **Packing List:** Create a comprehensive packing list detailing the contents of each package, including:
    *   Number of packages
    *   Weight (gross and net) and dimensions of each package
    *   Product identification (e.g., lot numbers, serial numbers)
3.  **Bill of Lading/Air Waybill:** Obtain from the carrier, serving as a contract of carriage and a receipt for the goods.
4.  **Certificate of Analysis (CoA):** Obtain from the manufacturer, verifying the quality and specifications of the product, ensuring it meets established standards.
5.  **Certificate of Origin:** Obtain, if required by the importing country or for preferential tariff treatment.
6.  **FDA-Specific Documentation:**
    *   **Prior Notice:** For animal food and feed, submit prior notice to FDA before arrival.
    *   **Affirmations of Compliance (A of C):** Provide relevant A of C codes to expedite FDA entry review. These codes affirm that the product meets specific FDA requirements (e.g., labeling, registration, marketing application status).
    *   **Manufacturer Registration and Product Listing:** Verify that the foreign manufacturer is registered with FDA and that the product is listed, if applicable.
    *   **Marketing Application Information:** If the product is an animal drug, ensure that the relevant marketing application (e.g., New Animal Drug Application - NADA) information is available and referenced.
7.  **USDA-Specific Documentation:**
    *   **Veterinary Services (VS) Permit:** If required, ensure the valid VS permit number is included in the documentation.
    *   **Health Certificates:** For certain animal-derived materials, obtain official health certificates from the exporting country's veterinary authorities.

#### 3.1.3 Customs Clearance for Import

1.  **Engage Customs Broker:** Utilize a licensed customs broker to facilitate the customs clearance process. Provide the broker with all necessary documentation.
2.  **Respond to Information Requests:** Promptly respond to any requests for additional information or documentation from customs authorities (U.S. Customs and Border Protection - CBP), FDA, or USDA.
3.  **Address Detentions/Refusals:** In case of detention or refusal, work with the customs broker and regulatory affairs to address the issues, provide corrective actions, or arrange for re-export/destruction as required.

### 3.2 Export Procedures

#### 3.2.1 Pre-Exportation Assessment

1.  **Determine Importing Country Requirements:** Research and understand the import regulations of the destination country, including product registration, labeling, and specific documentation requirements.
2.  **Verify Product Eligibility:** Ensure the animal health product is eligible for export to the destination country based on its regulations and any existing trade agreements.
3.  **Confirm cGMP Compliance:** Verify that the manufacturing process for the exported product adheres to cGMP standards recognized by the importing country.

#### 3.2.2 Documentation Preparation for Export

1.  **Commercial Invoice:** Prepare a detailed commercial invoice as per import procedures, tailored to the importing country's requirements.
2.  **Packing List:** Create a comprehensive packing list.
3.  **Bill of Lading/Air Waybill:** Obtain from the carrier.
4.  **Certificate of Analysis (CoA):** Provide a CoA for the exported product.
5.  **Certificate of Origin:** Provide, if required by the importing country.
6.  **FDA Export Certificates:** Obtain necessary export certificates from FDA (e.g., Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product - CPP, Certificate to Foreign Government - CFG) if required by the importing country.
7.  **USDA Export Certificates:** For certain animal products or byproducts, obtain export health certificates from USDA APHIS.
8.  **Other Country-Specific Documents:** Prepare any additional documents mandated by the importing country, such as free sale certificates, good manufacturing practice certificates, or specific import permits.

#### 3.2.3 Shipping and Post-Exportation

1.  **Coordinate with Freight Forwarder:** Work with a freight forwarder experienced in international pharmaceutical shipments to arrange transportation.
2.  **Track Shipment:** Monitor the shipment's progress and address any transit-related issues.
3.  **Maintain Records:** Retain all export documentation for the required retention period.

## 4.0 Documentation Requirements

All documentation related to import and export activities must be:

*   **Accurate and Complete:** All information must be truthful, precise, and comprehensive.
*   **Legible:** All documents must be clearly readable.
*   **Timely:** Documents must be prepared and submitted within specified deadlines.
*   **Retrievable:** All records must be stored in an organized manner, allowing for easy retrieval upon request by regulatory authorities or for internal audits.

**Required Documents (but not limited to):**

*   Commercial Invoice
*   Packing List
*   Bill of Lading/Air Waybill
*   Certificate of Analysis (CoA)
*   Certificate of Origin
*   FDA Import/Export Certificates (e.g., A of C, CPP, CFG)
*   USDA Veterinary Services (VS) Permits and Health Certificates
*   Customs Entry Forms
*   Correspondence with regulatory agencies, customs brokers, and freight forwarders

## 5.0 Training Requirements

All personnel involved in import/export documentation and procedures must receive comprehensive training, including:

*   Initial training upon hiring or assignment to relevant roles.
*   Annual refresher training on updated regulations, procedures, and best practices.
*   Specific training on the use of electronic submission systems (e.g., FDA's ACE, USDA's eFile).
*   Training records must be maintained by the Human Resources or Training Department.

## 6.0 Quality Control Measures

*   **Document Review:** All import/export documentation must undergo a multi-level review process by trained personnel (e.g., preparer, supervisor, regulatory affairs) before submission.
*   **Checklists:** Utilize standardized checklists for each type of import/export documentation to ensure all required fields are completed and supporting documents are attached.
*   **Internal Audits:** Conduct periodic internal audits of import/export processes and documentation to identify non-compliance or areas for improvement.
*   **Feedback Mechanism:** Establish a system for feedback from customs brokers, freight forwarders, and regulatory agencies to continuously improve documentation accuracy and efficiency.

## 7.0 Safety Considerations

While import/export documentation primarily involves administrative tasks, safety considerations are paramount, especially concerning the handling and transportation of animal health products. 

*   **Product Integrity:** Ensure proper packaging, labeling, and handling procedures are followed during transit to maintain product integrity and prevent contamination or damage.
*   **Hazardous Materials:** For products classified as hazardous materials, ensure all international and national regulations for transportation (e.g., IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations for air cargo, IMDG Code for sea cargo) are strictly followed, including proper documentation (e.g., Dangerous Goods Declaration), labeling, and packaging.
*   **Cold Chain Management:** For temperature-sensitive products, maintain strict cold chain protocols throughout the import/export process, and ensure all temperature monitoring documentation is accurately recorded and maintained.
*   **Biosecurity:** For animal-derived materials, adhere to biosecurity protocols to prevent the spread of animal diseases.

## 8.0 Regulatory Compliance References

This SOP is developed in accordance with, but not limited to, the following regulatory guidelines and acts:

*   **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA):**
    *   Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act), particularly Sections 201(g) (animal drugs), 201(h) (animal devices), and relevant sections for animal food/feed.
    *   21 CFR Part 210: Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Processing, Packing, or Holding of Drugs; General.
    *   21 CFR Part 211: Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals.
    *   21 CFR Part 507: Current Good Manufacturing Practice, Hazard Analysis, and Risk-Based Preventive Controls for Food for Animals.
    *   FDA Guidance for Industry: Importing Animal and Veterinary Products.
    *   FDA Guidance for Industry: Current Good Manufacturing Practice Requirements for Food for Animals.
*   **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA):**
    *   Animal Health Protection Act.
    *   9 CFR Part 92: Importation of Animals and Animal Products.
    *   USDA APHIS Veterinary Services (VS) import and export regulations and permitting requirements.
*   **Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP):**
    *   Adherence to cGMP principles as outlined in FDA regulations (21 CFR Parts 210, 211, 507) to ensure quality, safety, and efficacy of animal health products throughout the supply chain, including import and export activities.

## 9.0 Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to this SOP must follow the company's established change control procedure, which includes:

*   **Initiation:** A change request is initiated by the responsible department, detailing the proposed change and its justification.
*   **Review and Assessment:** The change request is reviewed by relevant stakeholders (e.g., Regulatory Affairs, Quality Assurance, affected departments) to assess its impact on operations, compliance, and product quality.
*   **Approval:** The change must be approved by authorized personnel, typically including the Head of Regulatory Affairs and Head of Quality Assurance.
*   **Implementation:** Once approved, the revised SOP is implemented, and all affected personnel are trained on the changes.
*   **Documentation:** All changes, including rationale, approvals, and implementation dates, are documented and maintained as part of the SOP's version history.
