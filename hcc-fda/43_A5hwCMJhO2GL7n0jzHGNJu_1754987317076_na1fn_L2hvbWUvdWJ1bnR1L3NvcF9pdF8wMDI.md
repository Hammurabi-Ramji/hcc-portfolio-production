## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the requirements for the management of electronic records and electronic signatures to ensure compliance with 21 CFR Part 11 and other applicable regulatory guidelines within large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The purpose of this SOP is to define the procedures for the creation, maintenance, archiving, and retrieval of electronic records, as well as the generation and management of electronic signatures, to ensure their authenticity, integrity, and confidentiality. This SOP applies to all electronic systems, applications, and data used in GxP (Good Practice) regulated activities, including but not limited to manufacturing, quality control, research and development, and regulatory affairs. The scope encompasses all personnel involved in the generation, processing, review, approval, and archiving of electronic records and electronic signatures.




## 2. Responsibilities

**2.1. Management:** Management is responsible for providing the necessary resources, including personnel, training, and technology, to ensure compliance with this SOP and 21 CFR Part 11. They are also responsible for reviewing and approving this SOP and any subsequent revisions.

**2.2. Quality Assurance (QA):** QA is responsible for overseeing the implementation and adherence to this SOP. This includes conducting audits, reviewing electronic records and systems for compliance, and ensuring that all personnel are adequately trained on the requirements of 21 CFR Part 11. QA will also be responsible for approving system validation documentation and change control requests related to electronic systems.

**2.3. Information Technology (IT) Department:** The IT Department is responsible for the design, development, implementation, maintenance, and security of all electronic systems used in GxP operations. This includes ensuring system validation, data integrity, system access controls, audit trail functionality, and data backup and recovery procedures. IT will also provide technical support and training related to electronic systems.

**2.4. System Owners:** System Owners are responsible for the day-to-day operation and maintenance of their respective electronic systems. This includes ensuring that the system is used in accordance with its intended purpose, that data is accurately entered and maintained, and that all users are properly trained. System Owners will also be responsible for initiating change control requests for their systems.

**2.5. Users:** All personnel who create, modify, review, or approve electronic records or use electronic signatures are responsible for adhering to the procedures outlined in this SOP. This includes ensuring the accuracy, completeness, and integrity of electronic records, and protecting the confidentiality of electronic signatures.




## 3. Procedures

### 3.1. System Validation and Qualification

All electronic systems used in GxP regulated activities must undergo a formal validation and qualification process to ensure they are fit for their intended use and comply with regulatory requirements, including 21 CFR Part 11. This process will follow a risk-based approach, consistent with GAMP 5 guidelines, and will include the following phases:

**3.1.1. Planning:** A Validation Plan will be developed for each electronic system, outlining the scope, responsibilities, validation strategy, and acceptance criteria. This plan will consider the system's complexity, criticality, and potential impact on product quality and patient safety.

**3.1.2. User Requirements Specification (URS):** Detailed User Requirements Specifications will be documented, defining the functional and non-functional requirements of the electronic system from the user's perspective. These requirements will be traceable throughout the validation lifecycle.

**3.1.3. Functional Specification (FS) and Design Specification (DS):** The URS will be translated into Functional Specifications, describing how the system will meet the user requirements, and Design Specifications, detailing the technical design of the system. These specifications will serve as the basis for system development and testing.

**3.1.4. Configuration and Development:** The electronic system will be configured or developed according to the approved Functional and Design Specifications. All configuration and development activities will be documented and subject to change control.

**3.1.5. Testing and Verification:** Comprehensive testing will be conducted to verify that the electronic system meets all specified requirements and functions as intended. This will include:
    *   **Installation Qualification (IQ):** Verifying that the system is installed correctly and all components are present and properly configured.
    *   **Operational Qualification (OQ):** Testing the system's functionality to ensure it operates according to its specifications across its operating range.
    *   **Performance Qualification (PQ):** Verifying that the system consistently performs as intended under actual operating conditions, demonstrating its ability to produce accurate and reliable results.

**3.1.6. Traceability Matrix:** A Traceability Matrix will be maintained to link user requirements to functional specifications, design specifications, test cases, and validation results, ensuring complete coverage and verification of all requirements.

**3.1.7. Validation Report:** A comprehensive Validation Report will be prepared upon completion of all validation activities, summarizing the validation process, results, deviations, and a conclusion on the system's fitness for use. The report will be approved by relevant stakeholders, including QA.

**3.1.8. Periodic Review:** Electronic systems will undergo periodic review to ensure their continued validated state. The frequency of these reviews will be determined by the system's criticality and risk assessment.




### 3.2. Electronic Signature Management

Electronic signatures are legally binding and equivalent to handwritten signatures when they meet the requirements of 21 CFR Part 11. This section outlines the procedures for the creation, control, and use of electronic signatures.

**3.2.1. Uniqueness and Linkage:** Each electronic signature must be unique to one individual and cannot be reused or reassigned to anyone else. Electronic signatures must be linked to their respective electronic records in a way that ensures they cannot be excised, copied, or otherwise transferred to falsify a record.

**3.2.2. Identity Verification:** Before an electronic signature is created or assigned, the identity of the individual must be verified. This typically involves a combination of at least two distinct components, such as an ID code and a password. The system must be designed to prevent unauthorized use of electronic signatures.

**3.2.3. Control of Electronic Signatures:**
    *   **Issuance:** Electronic signature components (e.g., ID codes, passwords) will be issued by the IT Department or a designated authority after proper identity verification.
    *   **Protection:** Users are responsible for protecting their electronic signature components from unauthorized disclosure or use. Passwords must be kept confidential and not shared with anyone.
    *   **Revocation:** Electronic signature components will be revoked immediately upon termination of employment, change of responsibilities that no longer require the use of electronic signatures, or suspicion of compromise.

**3.2.4. Electronic Signature Manifestations:** When an electronic signature is applied to a record, the system must capture and display the following information:
    *   The printed name of the signer.
    *   The date and time of the signature execution.
    *   The meaning (e.g., review, approval, authorship, responsibility) associated with the signature.

**3.2.5. Training:** All individuals who use electronic signatures must receive comprehensive training on the proper use of electronic signatures, the requirements of 21 CFR Part 11, and the consequences of misuse.

**3.2.6. Audit Trails for Electronic Signatures:** All actions related to the creation, modification, or use of electronic signatures must be captured in an audit trail, including the identity of the individual, the date and time of the action, and the nature of the action. This audit trail must be secure and unalterable.




### 3.3. Audit Trails

Audit trails are crucial for maintaining the integrity and traceability of electronic records. This section details the requirements for audit trails in electronic systems.

**3.3.1. Purpose of Audit Trails:** Audit trails provide a secure, computer-generated, time-stamped record of all entries, modifications, and deletions of electronic records. They are essential for reconstructing the history of a record, identifying who performed what action, and when. This ensures data integrity and accountability, which are critical for 21 CFR Part 11 compliance.

**3.3.2. Audit Trail Requirements:**
    *   **Automatic Capture:** Audit trails must be automatically generated by the electronic system and capture all GxP-relevant actions, including but not limited to: record creation, modification, deletion, electronic signature application, system access (log-in/log-out), and changes to system configurations.
    *   **Content of Audit Trails:** Each audit trail entry must include:
        *   The identity of the individual performing the action.
        *   The date and time of the action (including seconds).
        *   A description of the action performed.
        *   The old and new values for any modified data (if applicable).
        *   The reason for any change (if applicable, and if the system allows for user input).
    *   **Security and Integrity:** Audit trails must be secure and protected from unauthorized alteration or deletion. This typically involves technical controls such as cryptographic hashing, write-once-read-many (WORM) storage, and access restrictions. Any attempt to alter an audit trail must be recorded.
    *   **Review and Retention:** Audit trails must be regularly reviewed by authorized personnel (e.g., QA, System Owners) to detect any anomalous activities or deviations. The frequency and scope of these reviews will be defined in system-specific procedures. Audit trails must be retained for the same period as the associated electronic records.

**3.3.3. Audit Trail Accessibility:** Audit trails must be readily available and convertible into a human-readable format for review, inspection, and copying by authorized personnel and regulatory authorities. The system should provide tools for efficient searching, filtering, and reporting of audit trail data.

**3.3.4. Training:** Personnel responsible for reviewing audit trails must be trained on the audit trail functionality of the relevant systems and the procedures for identifying and investigating suspicious activities.




### 3.4. Data Integrity and Security

Maintaining data integrity and security is paramount for electronic records in a regulated environment. This section outlines the measures to ensure the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and security of electronic data.

**3.4.1. Data Integrity Principles (ALCOA+):** All electronic data must adhere to the ALCOA+ principles:
    *   **Attributable:** It must be clear who performed an action or generated data.
    *   **Legible:** Data must be readable and understandable.
    *   **Contemporaneous:** Data must be recorded at the time the work is performed.
    *   **Original:** Data must be the first capture of the information.
    *   **Accurate:** Data must be correct and truthful.
    *   **Complete:** All required data must be present.
    *   **Consistent:** Data must be consistent across all records and systems.
    *   **Enduring:** Data must be stored in a durable medium.
    *   **Available:** Data must be accessible when needed.

**3.4.2. Access Controls:** Access to electronic systems and data must be strictly controlled based on job function and responsibility. This includes:
    *   **Unique User IDs:** Each user must have a unique user ID.
    *   **Password Management:** Strong password policies must be enforced, including minimum length, complexity requirements, and regular expiration. Passwords must be encrypted during storage and transmission.
    *   **Role-Based Access:** Access privileges will be assigned based on predefined roles, ensuring users only have access to the data and functions necessary for their job.
    *   **Privileged Access Management:** Administrative and privileged access to systems will be highly restricted and monitored.

**3.4.3. Data Security:** Measures to protect electronic data from unauthorized access, modification, destruction, or disclosure include:
    *   **Physical Security:** Electronic systems and data storage facilities will be protected by appropriate physical security measures (e.g., restricted access, surveillance).
    *   **Network Security:** Firewalls, intrusion detection/prevention systems, and secure network configurations will be implemented to protect against external and internal threats.
    *   **Encryption:** Sensitive data, especially during transmission and storage, will be encrypted using validated cryptographic methods.
    *   **Malware Protection:** Robust antivirus and anti-malware solutions will be deployed and regularly updated across all systems.
    *   **Vulnerability Management:** Regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing will be conducted to identify and address security weaknesses.

**3.4.4. Data Backup and Restoration:** Regular and verified data backups will be performed to prevent data loss. Backup procedures will include:
    *   **Frequency:** Backups will be performed at a defined frequency based on data criticality and change rate.
    *   **Storage:** Backups will be stored securely, off-site, and in an environmentally controlled location.
    *   **Restoration Testing:** Regular testing of backup restoration procedures will be conducted to ensure data can be recovered accurately and efficiently in the event of a system failure or disaster.

**3.4.5. Data Archiving and Retrieval:** Procedures for long-term archiving of electronic records will ensure their continued accessibility and readability throughout their retention period. This includes using appropriate technologies and formats that prevent data degradation and obsolescence.




### 3.5. Record Retention and Archiving

Electronic records must be retained for specific periods as mandated by regulatory requirements and internal policies. This section outlines the procedures for the retention and archiving of electronic records.

**3.5.1. Retention Periods:** The retention period for each type of electronic record will be defined based on applicable regulatory requirements (e.g., FDA, USDA, cGMP) and internal company policies. These retention periods will be documented and communicated to relevant personnel.

**3.5.2. Archiving Procedures:** Electronic records that are no longer actively used but must be retained will be archived. Archiving procedures will ensure:
    *   **Data Integrity:** The integrity of archived records is maintained throughout their retention period, preventing any unauthorized alteration or degradation.
    *   **Accessibility:** Archived records remain accessible and readable throughout their retention period. This may involve migrating data to new formats or systems as technology evolves to prevent obsolescence.
    *   **Search and Retrieval:** Efficient mechanisms are in place for searching and retrieving archived records when needed for audits, inspections, or investigations.
    *   **Storage Media:** Archived records will be stored on reliable and durable media, with appropriate environmental controls to prevent damage or loss.

**3.5.3. Decommissioning of Records:** When electronic records reach the end of their retention period, they will be securely decommissioned or destroyed in a manner that prevents their recovery, in accordance with established procedures and regulatory guidelines. A record of destruction will be maintained.

**3.5.4. Legal Holds:** In the event of litigation, regulatory inquiry, or other legal holds, the destruction of relevant electronic records will be suspended until the hold is released. The IT Department and Legal Department will collaborate to manage legal holds.




### 3.6. Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity

To ensure the continuous availability of electronic records and systems, a comprehensive Disaster Recovery (DR) and Business Continuity Plan (BCP) will be established and maintained. This plan will address potential disruptions to IT infrastructure and ensure the timely recovery of critical systems and data.

**3.6.1. Risk Assessment:** A thorough risk assessment will be conducted to identify potential threats and vulnerabilities to electronic systems and data, including natural disasters, cyber-attacks, power outages, and equipment failures. The assessment will evaluate the potential impact of these risks on business operations.

**3.6.2. Recovery Time Objective (RTO) and Recovery Point Objective (RPO):** Critical electronic systems and data will be assigned specific Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs). RTO defines the maximum tolerable downtime for a system, while RPO defines the maximum tolerable data loss.

**3.6.3. Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP):** A detailed DRP will be developed, outlining the procedures for recovering electronic systems and data in the event of a disaster. The DRP will include:
    *   **Roles and Responsibilities:** Clearly defined roles and responsibilities for the DR team.
    *   **Communication Plan:** Procedures for communicating with internal stakeholders, external vendors, and regulatory authorities during a disaster.
    *   **Recovery Procedures:** Step-by-step instructions for restoring hardware, software, and data from backups.
    *   **Alternative Sites:** Identification and establishment of alternative processing sites or cloud-based solutions for critical systems.

**3.6.4. Business Continuity Plan (BCP):** The BCP will focus on maintaining essential business functions during and after a disaster. It will include:
    *   **Business Impact Analysis (BIA):** Identification of critical business processes and their dependencies on electronic systems.
    *   **Continuity Strategies:** Development of strategies to ensure the continuity of critical business processes, even if electronic systems are partially or fully unavailable.
    *   **Manual Workarounds:** Documentation of manual workarounds for critical processes that can be implemented if electronic systems are down.

**3.6.5. Testing and Training:** The DRP and BCP will be regularly tested through drills and simulations to ensure their effectiveness. Personnel involved in disaster recovery and business continuity will receive regular training on their roles and responsibilities.

**3.6.6. Review and Update:** The DRP and BCP will be reviewed and updated periodically, or after any significant changes to IT infrastructure, business processes, or regulatory requirements.




## 4. Documentation Requirements

Comprehensive documentation is essential for demonstrating compliance with 21 CFR Part 11 and other regulatory requirements. All documentation related to electronic records and systems must be accurate, complete, and readily available.

**4.1. System Documentation:** Each electronic system will have a complete set of documentation, including:
    *   **Validation Plan and Report:** As described in Section 3.1.
    *   **User Requirements Specification (URS):** Detailing user needs and expectations.
    *   **Functional Specification (FS) and Design Specification (DS):** Describing system functionality and technical design.
    *   **Configuration Specifications:** Documenting system settings and parameters.
    *   **Test Plans and Reports:** Detailing testing activities and results.
    *   **System Administration and User Manuals:** Providing instructions for system operation and maintenance.
    *   **System Architecture Diagrams:** Illustrating the system's components and their interconnections.
    *   **Risk Assessments:** Documenting identified risks and mitigation strategies.

**4.2. Electronic Record Documentation:** All electronic records generated or maintained within GxP regulated activities must be documented, including:
    *   **Raw Data:** Original data captured by the system.
    *   **Processed Data:** Data that has undergone calculations, transformations, or analysis.
    *   **Audit Trails:** As described in Section 3.3.
    *   **Metadata:** Information describing the electronic record, such as creation date, author, and version.
    *   **Electronic Signatures:** As described in Section 3.2.

**4.3. Training Records:** All training related to this SOP, electronic systems, and 21 CFR Part 11 compliance must be documented, including attendee lists, training materials, and assessment results.

**4.4. Change Control Documentation:** All changes to electronic systems, software, or validated processes must be documented through the change control system, including the rationale for the change, impact assessment, testing, and approval.

**4.5. Incident and Deviation Records:** Any incidents, deviations, or system failures related to electronic records or systems must be documented, investigated, and resolved, with corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) implemented as necessary.

**4.6. Retention and Archiving of Documentation:** All documentation described in this section will be retained and archived in accordance with the procedures outlined in Section 3.5.




## 5. Training Requirements

Effective training is critical to ensure that all personnel involved in the management of electronic records and electronic signatures understand and comply with the requirements of this SOP and 21 CFR Part 11. Training will be conducted as follows:

**5.1. Initial Training:** All new employees whose roles involve the creation, modification, review, approval, or archiving of electronic records, or the use of electronic signatures, must complete initial training on this SOP, relevant electronic systems, and the principles of 21 CFR Part 11 before performing any GxP-related activities.

**5.2. Refresher Training:** Periodic refresher training will be provided to all relevant personnel to reinforce understanding, communicate updates to this SOP or regulatory requirements, and address any identified gaps in knowledge or compliance. The frequency of refresher training will be determined by the criticality of the role and the complexity of the systems involved, typically on an annual or biennial basis.

**5.3. System-Specific Training:** Personnel will receive specific training on the electronic systems they use, covering system functionality, data entry procedures, electronic signature application, audit trail review, and troubleshooting. This training will be provided by the IT Department or designated System Owners.

**5.4. Training Content:** Training content will include, but not be limited to:
    *   The purpose and scope of this SOP.
    *   Individual responsibilities related to electronic records and signatures.
    *   Detailed procedures for using electronic systems and applying electronic signatures.
    *   The importance of data integrity and security.
    *   The requirements of 21 CFR Part 11, including the legal equivalence of electronic signatures.
    *   Consequences of non-compliance and data integrity breaches.
    *   Procedures for reporting incidents or deviations.

**5.5. Training Documentation:** All training activities will be documented, including:
    *   Training materials (e.g., presentations, handouts, user manuals).
    *   Attendance records, including names, dates, and signatures of attendees.
    *   Assessment results (if applicable) to verify understanding.
    *   Trainer qualifications.

**5.6. Competency Assessment:** Where appropriate, competency assessments will be conducted to ensure that personnel have a thorough understanding of the training material and are capable of performing their duties in compliance with this SOP.




## 6. Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures are implemented to ensure the ongoing integrity, accuracy, and reliability of electronic records and systems. These measures are designed to proactively identify and address potential issues and maintain a state of control.

**6.1. Regular System Audits:** Periodic internal and external audits will be conducted to assess compliance with this SOP, 21 CFR Part 11, and other applicable regulations. Audits will cover:
    *   Review of electronic system validation documentation.
    *   Verification of access controls and security measures.
    *   Examination of audit trails for completeness and integrity.
    *   Assessment of data backup and restoration procedures.
    *   Review of training records and personnel competency.
    *   Evaluation of change control effectiveness.

**6.2. Data Integrity Checks:** Regular data integrity checks will be performed to verify the accuracy, completeness, and consistency of electronic records. This may include:
    *   Automated checks within the system to detect data entry errors or inconsistencies.
    *   Manual review of critical data points against source documents or other reliable records.
    *   Reconciliation of data across different systems where applicable.

**6.3. System Performance Monitoring:** Electronic systems will be continuously monitored for performance, availability, and security. This includes:
    *   Monitoring system logs for errors, security breaches, or unusual activities.
    *   Tracking system uptime and response times.
    *   Regularly reviewing security alerts and implementing necessary patches or updates.

**6.4. Deviation and Incident Management:** Any deviations from this SOP, system malfunctions, data integrity issues, or security incidents will be promptly reported, investigated, and documented. A formal deviation/incident management process will be followed, including:
    *   Root cause analysis to identify the underlying reasons for the deviation.
    *   Implementation of immediate corrective actions to mitigate the impact.
    *   Development and implementation of preventive actions (CAPAs) to prevent recurrence.
    *   Documentation of the entire process, including investigation findings, actions taken, and effectiveness checks.

**6.5. Vendor Management:** For electronic systems or services provided by third-party vendors, a robust vendor management program will be in place. This includes:
    *   Vendor qualification and auditing to ensure their systems and processes meet regulatory requirements.
    *   Service Level Agreements (SLAs) defining responsibilities, performance expectations, and data security provisions.
    *   Regular performance reviews of vendors.

**6.6. Management Review:** The effectiveness of the electronic records management system and compliance with 21 CFR Part 11 will be periodically reviewed by management. This review will consider audit findings, deviation trends, system performance, and regulatory updates to drive continuous improvement.




## 7. Safety Considerations

While electronic records management primarily deals with data and systems, certain safety considerations are relevant, particularly concerning the physical infrastructure and the potential impact of system failures on operational safety.

**7.1. Physical Security of IT Infrastructure:** The physical security of servers, data centers, and other IT infrastructure housing electronic records is crucial. Measures include:
    *   **Access Control:** Restricted access to server rooms and data centers to authorized personnel only.
    *   **Environmental Controls:** Maintaining appropriate temperature, humidity, and fire suppression systems to prevent equipment damage and data loss.
    *   **Power Supply:** Ensuring stable and redundant power supplies (e.g., UPS, generators) to prevent sudden system shutdowns that could corrupt data or compromise system integrity.

**7.2. Data Security and Cyber Safety:** Protecting electronic records from cyber threats is a safety consideration, as data breaches or system compromises can have significant operational and reputational impacts. Measures include:
    *   **Cybersecurity Protocols:** Implementation of robust cybersecurity protocols, including firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and regular security audits.
    *   **Malware Protection:** Deployment and regular updating of antivirus and anti-malware software on all relevant systems.
    *   **Employee Awareness:** Training employees on cybersecurity best practices, including phishing awareness, strong password usage, and reporting suspicious activities.

**7.3. Impact of System Failures on Operations:** The potential for electronic system failures to impact operational safety, particularly in manufacturing environments, must be considered. While this SOP focuses on records, the reliability of systems generating those records is paramount.
    *   **Redundancy:** Implementing redundant systems and network components to minimize downtime in case of hardware or software failures.
    *   **Emergency Procedures:** Establishing clear emergency procedures for system failures that could affect critical manufacturing or quality control processes, ensuring manual backups or alternative methods are available to maintain safety and product quality.

**7.4. Ergonomics and User Interface Design:** While not directly related to data integrity, the design of electronic systems should consider user ergonomics to minimize errors and enhance user safety during data entry and system interaction. User-friendly interfaces can reduce the likelihood of mistakes that could indirectly impact data quality.

## 9. Change Control Procedures

Any changes to validated electronic systems, software, or processes that impact electronic records or electronic signatures must be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are documented, reviewed, tested, and approved before implementation, maintaining the validated state of the system and compliance with 21 CFR Part 11.

**9.1. Initiation of Change:** Any proposed change to an electronic system or related process will be initiated through a formal Change Request (CR) form. The CR will include:
    *   A clear description of the proposed change.
    *   The reason for the change.
    *   The potential impact of the change on the system, data integrity, and regulatory compliance.
    *   The proposed implementation timeline.

**9.2. Impact Assessment:** A cross-functional team, including representatives from IT, QA, and the System Owner, will conduct an impact assessment to evaluate the potential effects of the proposed change. This assessment will determine:
    *   The scope of the change.
    *   The potential impact on data integrity, security, and system functionality.
    *   The need for revalidation or re-qualification activities.
    *   The impact on existing documentation (e.g., URS, FS, DS, SOPs).
    *   The need for additional training.

**9.3. Review and Approval:** The Change Request and impact assessment will be reviewed by relevant stakeholders, including QA, IT management, and the System Owner. Approval will be granted only after all potential impacts have been assessed and appropriate mitigation strategies are in place. For significant changes, senior management approval may be required.

**9.4. Implementation:** Once approved, the change will be implemented according to a defined plan. All implementation activities will be documented, including dates, personnel involved, and any deviations from the plan.

**9.5. Testing and Verification:** After implementation, comprehensive testing will be conducted to verify that the change has been successfully implemented and that the system continues to function as intended without introducing new errors or compromising data integrity. This may involve re-running previously executed validation tests or developing new test cases specific to the change.

**9.6. Documentation Update:** All relevant documentation, including system specifications, user manuals, and this SOP, will be updated to reflect the implemented change. Version control will be strictly maintained for all documents.

**9.7. Training:** If the change impacts user procedures or system functionality, relevant personnel will receive training on the updated system or process before the change is fully deployed.

**9.8. Closure:** Upon successful implementation, verification, and documentation updates, the Change Request will be formally closed. The effectiveness of the change will be monitored as part of ongoing quality control measures.
