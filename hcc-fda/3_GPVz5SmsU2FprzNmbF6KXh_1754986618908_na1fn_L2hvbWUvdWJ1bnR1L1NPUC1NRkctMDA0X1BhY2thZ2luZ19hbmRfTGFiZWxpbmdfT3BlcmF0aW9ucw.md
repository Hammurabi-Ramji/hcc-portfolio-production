# Standard Operating Procedure

**SOP Number:** SOP-MFG-004

**SOP Title:** Packaging and Labeling Operations for Animal Drugs

**Version:** 1.0

**Effective Date:** 2025-12-08

**Category:** Manufacturing

**Compliance Areas:** FDA, USDA, cGMP

## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the detailed instructions and requirements for the packaging and labeling of animal drug products at [Company Name], ensuring compliance with current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP), FDA regulations, and USDA guidelines. The purpose is to ensure that all animal drug products are packaged and labeled accurately, safely, and in a manner that maintains product integrity and traceability, preventing mislabeling, mix-ups, or contamination.

## 2.0 Scope

This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the packaging and labeling operations of animal drug products manufactured at [Company Name]'s facilities. This includes, but is not limited to, production, quality control, quality assurance, and warehousing personnel. It covers all stages from the receipt of packaging materials to the final release of packaged and labeled animal drug products for distribution. This SOP specifically addresses the requirements for large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

### 3.1 Production Department

*   **Production Manager:** Oversees all packaging and labeling operations, ensures adequate resources are available, and verifies adherence to this SOP. Responsible for training and qualification of production personnel.
*   **Packaging and Labeling Operators:** Execute packaging and labeling activities according to this SOP, maintain equipment, and accurately complete all required documentation.
*   **Line Leaders/Supervisors:** Directly supervise packaging and labeling lines, ensure in-process quality checks are performed, and address any deviations or issues promptly.

### 3.2 Quality Control (QC) Department

*   **QC Manager:** Oversees all quality control activities related to packaging and labeling, including sampling, testing, and release of packaging materials and finished products.
*   **QC Analysts:** Perform in-process and final quality control checks on packaged and labeled products, including label verification, package integrity, and content accuracy.

### 3.3 Quality Assurance (QA) Department

*   **QA Manager:** Responsible for the overall quality management system, including review and approval of this SOP, auditing compliance with cGMP and regulatory requirements, and managing deviations and change controls related to packaging and labeling.
*   **QA Specialists:** Review batch records, investigate deviations, approve labeling materials, and ensure all packaging and labeling operations comply with established procedures and regulatory standards.

### 3.4 Warehouse/Materials Management

*   **Warehouse Manager:** Responsible for the proper storage, handling, and issuance of packaging and labeling materials, ensuring only approved materials are released for use.
*   **Material Handlers:** Transport and stage packaging and labeling materials to the production lines and move finished goods to storage areas, maintaining proper identification and segregation.




## 4.0 Detailed Procedures

### 4.1 Preparation of Packaging Line

1.  **Line Clearance:** Before starting any packaging operation, ensure the packaging line is completely cleared of all previous product, labels, and packaging materials. This includes all equipment, conveyors, and work surfaces. Perform a visual inspection and document the line clearance on the Batch Packaging Record (BPR) [1].
2.  **Equipment Setup and Verification:** Set up all necessary packaging and labeling equipment (e.g., fillers, cappers, labelers, cartoners, shrink wrappers). Verify that all equipment is clean, calibrated, and in good working order. Perform routine maintenance checks as per equipment-specific SOPs [2].
3.  **Material Verification:** Verify that all packaging materials (bottles, caps, labels, cartons, inserts, etc.) match the specifications outlined in the Batch Packaging Record (BPR) for the specific product and batch number. Check material integrity, expiration dates (if applicable), and quantity. Any discrepancies must be reported to the Production Supervisor and QA immediately [3].
4.  **Label Verification:** Prior to loading labels onto the labeler, visually inspect a sample of labels for correctness (product name, strength, batch number, expiration date, regulatory statements, barcodes). Ensure the label stock matches the BPR. Use a label reconciliation process to account for all labels issued and used [4].

### 4.2 In-Process Packaging and Labeling

1.  **Product Filling:** If applicable, ensure the correct animal drug product is being filled into the primary containers. Monitor fill volumes/weights, ensuring they are within specified tolerances. Conduct in-process checks at regular intervals as per the BPR [5].
2.  **Container Sealing/Capping:** Ensure primary containers are properly sealed or capped to prevent leakage and maintain product integrity. Verify torque settings for caps and inspect seals for defects. Conduct visual inspections and physical checks at defined frequencies [6].
3.  **Label Application:** Apply labels accurately and securely to the containers. Ensure labels are straight, legible, and free from wrinkles or tears. Verify that variable data (batch number, expiration date) is correctly printed and legible. Perform frequent in-process label checks [7].
4.  **Secondary Packaging:** Place primary containers into secondary packaging (e.g., cartons, boxes). Ensure correct orientation and inclusion of any required inserts or leaflets. Verify carton integrity and proper sealing [8].
5.  **Tertiary Packaging:** Pack secondary packages into tertiary packaging (e.g., shipping cases). Ensure proper count and secure sealing of cases. Apply case labels with accurate product and batch information [9].

### 4.3 In-Process Quality Control Checks

1.  **Visual Inspection:** Conduct continuous visual inspections throughout the packaging and labeling process for defects such as damaged containers, misaligned labels, incorrect printing, or missing components. Any identified defects must be immediately addressed and documented [10].
2.  **Weight/Volume Checks:** Perform periodic weight or volume checks of filled containers to ensure consistency and compliance with specifications. Document all readings [11].
3.  **Label Integrity and Accuracy Checks:** At specified intervals, pull samples from the line to verify label adhesion, legibility, and accuracy of all printed information, including barcodes. Use barcode scanners to confirm scannability and data integrity [12].
4.  **Component Reconciliation:** At the end of each packaging run, reconcile all packaging and labeling materials issued against those used and returned. Any discrepancies must be investigated and documented [13].

### 4.4 Line Clearance and Reconciliation (Post-Packaging)

1.  **Final Line Clearance:** Upon completion of a packaging run, perform a thorough line clearance to remove all remaining product, labels, and packaging materials. This is critical to prevent cross-contamination and mix-ups between different products or batches [14].
2.  **Material Return:** Return all unused and reconciled packaging and labeling materials to the warehouse, ensuring proper documentation of quantities returned [15].
3.  **Equipment Cleaning:** Clean all packaging and labeling equipment according to established cleaning SOPs. Document cleaning activities in equipment logs [16].

## 5.0 Documentation Requirements

All packaging and labeling operations must be thoroughly documented to ensure traceability, accountability, and compliance with cGMP regulations. Required documentation includes, but is not limited to:

*   **Batch Packaging Record (BPR):** A comprehensive record for each batch, detailing all packaging and labeling activities, in-process checks, material reconciliation, and deviations. This record must be completed concurrently with the operation [17].
*   **Equipment Logs:** Records of equipment setup, calibration, maintenance, and cleaning [18].
*   **Line Clearance Forms:** Documentation confirming the thorough clearance of the packaging line before and after each run [19].
*   **In-Process Control Records:** Records of all quality checks performed during the packaging process, including visual inspections, weight/volume checks, and label verification [20].
*   **Material Issuance and Return Forms:** Documentation of all packaging and labeling materials issued to the line and returned to inventory [21].
*   **Deviation Reports:** Any deviations from this SOP or unexpected events must be documented, investigated, and resolved through the company's deviation management system [22].
*   **Training Records:** Documentation of all personnel training on this SOP and related procedures [23].

## 6.0 Training Requirements

All personnel involved in packaging and labeling operations must receive comprehensive training on this SOP and related procedures prior to performing their duties. Training must be documented and include:

*   Initial training upon hiring or transfer to the packaging department [24].
*   Refresher training at a defined frequency (e.g., annually) or when significant changes are made to this SOP or related processes [25].
*   Training on cGMP principles relevant to packaging and labeling [26].
*   Specific training on the operation and maintenance of packaging and labeling equipment [27].
*   Training on proper documentation practices [28].
*   Training on deviation reporting and handling [29].

## 7.0 Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures are integral to ensuring the integrity, identity, purity, and quality of packaged and labeled animal drug products. These include:

*   **Incoming Material Inspection:** All packaging and labeling materials must be inspected upon receipt to ensure they meet specifications before being released for use [30].
*   **In-Process Controls:** As detailed in Section 4.3, regular in-process checks are performed to monitor critical parameters during packaging and labeling [31].
*   **Finished Product Testing:** After packaging and labeling, a representative sample of the finished product is subjected to final quality control testing, including identity, strength, quality, and purity, as per product specifications [32].
*   **Label Reconciliation:** Strict reconciliation of labels to prevent mix-ups and mislabeling [33].
*   **Retention Samples:** Retain samples of each batch of finished product and associated labeling for future reference and investigation if needed [34].

## 8.0 Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all operations. Personnel must adhere to all company safety policies and procedures. Specific safety considerations for packaging and labeling include:

*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Wear appropriate PPE, including safety glasses, gloves, and lab coats, as required by the specific task and product [35].
*   **Equipment Safety:** Follow all safety guidelines for operating packaging and labeling machinery. Ensure guards are in place and emergency stop buttons are functional [36].
*   **Material Handling:** Use proper lifting techniques and material handling equipment to prevent injuries when moving heavy loads of packaging materials or finished products [37].
*   **Chemical Safety:** If handling cleaning agents or other chemicals, follow safety data sheet (SDS) guidelines for safe handling, storage, and disposal [38].
*   **Emergency Procedures:** Be familiar with emergency procedures, including fire safety, spill response, and first aid [39].
