# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

## SOP-MFG-013: Facility Maintenance and Utilities Management

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 2025-12-08
**Author:** Manus AI

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the guidelines and procedures for the effective management of facility maintenance and utilities within large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. The purpose of this SOP is to ensure the continuous, reliable, and compliant operation of all facilities, systems, and equipment critical to the production of safe, efficacious, and quality animal pharmaceutical products. This includes, but is not limited to, buildings, production areas, laboratories, warehouses, and essential utilities such as Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC) systems, purified water systems, compressed air, and electrical power. The scope of this SOP covers all aspects of maintenance, including preventive, corrective, and predictive maintenance activities, as well as the management of utility systems from their source to their point of use within the manufacturing facility. Adherence to this SOP is crucial for maintaining a state of control, preventing contamination, ensuring product integrity, and complying with national and international regulatory requirements, including those set forth by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), and current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP).



## 2. Responsibilities

**2.1. Facility Management Department:**
   - Oversees the overall planning, coordination, and execution of all facility maintenance and utilities management activities.
   - Develops and implements comprehensive preventive maintenance schedules for all critical equipment and utility systems.
   - Ensures that all maintenance activities are performed in accordance with this SOP, cGMP regulations, and other applicable standards.
   - Manages external contractors and service providers involved in facility maintenance and utility services.
   - Maintains accurate records of all maintenance activities, including work orders, repair logs, and calibration records.
   - Conducts regular inspections of facilities and utility systems to identify potential issues and ensure compliance.

**2.2. Production Department:**
   - Collaborates with the Facility Management Department to schedule maintenance activities to minimize disruption to production operations.
   - Reports any equipment malfunctions, utility failures, or facility deficiencies promptly to the Facility Management Department.
   - Ensures that production personnel adhere to all safety procedures and cGMP requirements during maintenance activities.

**2.3. Quality Assurance (QA) Department:**
   - Reviews and approves all SOPs related to facility maintenance and utilities management.
   - Conducts audits and inspections to ensure compliance with cGMP regulations, internal procedures, and this SOP.
   - Approves all deviations, investigations, and corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) related to facility and utility issues.
   - Ensures that all changes to facilities, equipment, or utility systems are managed through the change control process.

**2.4. Engineering Department:**
   - Provides technical expertise and support for the design, installation, and modification of facility systems and utilities.
   - Participates in the qualification and validation of new or modified equipment and utility systems.
   - Troubleshoots complex technical issues related to facility infrastructure and utility performance.

**2.5. All Personnel:**
   - Adheres to all established procedures and safety guidelines related to facility maintenance and utility usage.
   - Reports any observed deficiencies, hazards, or non-conformances to their immediate supervisor and the Facility Management Department.



## 3. Detailed Procedures

### 3.1. Preventive Maintenance Program

The Preventive Maintenance (PM) Program is designed to ensure the optimal functioning and longevity of all critical facility infrastructure, equipment, and utility systems, thereby minimizing downtime, preventing costly breakdowns, and ensuring continuous compliance with regulatory standards. The PM program is based on a scheduled approach, incorporating manufacturer recommendations, industry best practices, and regulatory requirements.

**3.1.1. PM Schedule Development:**
   - The Facility Management Department, in conjunction with the Engineering Department, shall develop and maintain a comprehensive PM schedule for all critical assets. This schedule shall specify:
     - The asset identification number and description.
     - The type of maintenance activity (e.g., inspection, lubrication, cleaning, calibration).
     - The frequency of the maintenance activity (e.g., daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annually).
     - The estimated time required for completion.
     - The required tools, materials, and personnel qualifications.
     - References to specific work instructions or checklists.
   - The PM schedule shall be reviewed and updated at least annually, or as needed, based on equipment performance, maintenance history, and changes in operational requirements or regulatory guidelines.

**3.1.2. PM Work Order Generation and Execution:**
   - PM work orders shall be automatically generated by the Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) or manually initiated by the Facility Management Department according to the established schedule.
   - Each PM work order shall include a unique identification number, the asset to be maintained, the specific tasks to be performed, safety precautions, and acceptance criteria.
   - Qualified maintenance personnel shall execute PM work orders as scheduled. All tasks shall be performed in strict adherence to the detailed work instructions and safety protocols.
   - Any deviations from the work instructions or unexpected findings shall be documented on the work order and reported to the Facility Management Supervisor.

**3.1.3. Documentation of PM Activities:**
   - Upon completion of each PM activity, maintenance personnel shall accurately record all relevant information on the work order, including:
     - Date and time of completion.
     - Name and signature of the personnel performing the work.
     - Details of tasks performed, including any adjustments, repairs, or parts replaced.
     - Any observed abnormalities, potential issues, or recommendations for future action.
     - Confirmation that the asset is returned to its operational state.
   - Completed PM work orders shall be reviewed by the Facility Management Supervisor for accuracy and completeness before being closed in the CMMS.
   - All PM documentation shall be archived and readily retrievable for audit and review purposes for a minimum of 7 years or as required by regulatory bodies.



### 3.2. Corrective Maintenance Procedures

Corrective Maintenance (CM) procedures are implemented in response to equipment malfunctions, breakdowns, or facility failures that occur outside of scheduled preventive maintenance activities. The objective of CM is to restore equipment or systems to their operational state efficiently and effectively, minimizing impact on production and ensuring continued compliance.

**3.2.1. Reporting Malfunctions and Initiating CM:**
   - Any personnel observing an equipment malfunction, utility failure, or facility deficiency shall immediately report the issue to their supervisor and the Facility Management Department. For critical systems impacting product quality or safety, immediate notification to Quality Assurance is also required.
   - The report shall include the asset identification, a clear description of the problem, the time of observation, and any immediate actions taken.
   - Upon receipt of a malfunction report, the Facility Management Department shall assess the urgency and potential impact of the issue.
   - A corrective maintenance work order shall be generated in the CMMS, detailing the reported problem and assigning a priority level (e.g., Critical, High, Medium, Low).

**3.2.2. Troubleshooting and Repair:**
   - Qualified maintenance or engineering personnel shall be dispatched to troubleshoot the reported issue. Initial troubleshooting steps may include visual inspection, diagnostic testing, and review of equipment manuals or schematics.
   - The root cause of the malfunction shall be identified to the extent possible before initiating repairs.
   - Repairs shall be performed in accordance with established safety procedures, manufacturer guidelines, and cGMP principles. This may involve replacing faulty components, adjusting settings, or performing other necessary interventions.
   - For complex repairs or those requiring specialized expertise, external contractors may be engaged. All external work shall be supervised by qualified internal personnel and adhere to the facility's safety and quality standards.

**3.2.3. Post-Repair Verification and Documentation:**
   - Following repairs, the equipment or system shall be thoroughly tested to verify its proper functioning and that the malfunction has been resolved. This may include operational checks, performance tests, and, for critical systems, re-qualification or re-validation as determined by Quality Assurance.
   - All corrective maintenance activities shall be meticulously documented on the work order, including:
     - Date and time of malfunction report and repair completion.
     - Name and signature of personnel performing the repair.
     - Detailed description of the problem and the root cause identified.
     - Specific actions taken to repair the equipment, including parts replaced and tools used.
     - Results of post-repair verification tests.
     - Any recommendations for preventive measures to avoid recurrence.
   - Completed CM work orders shall be reviewed by the Facility Management Supervisor and Quality Assurance (for critical systems) for accuracy, completeness, and effectiveness of the repair.
   - All CM documentation shall be archived and readily retrievable for audit and review purposes for a minimum of 7 years or as required by regulatory bodies.
   - Significant or recurring malfunctions shall trigger an investigation process, potentially leading to a CAPA, to address the underlying causes and prevent future occurrences.



### 3.3. Utilities Management (HVAC, Water, Power, etc.)

Effective management of utility systems is paramount in pharmaceutical manufacturing to ensure a controlled environment, prevent contamination, and maintain product quality. This section outlines the procedures for managing critical utilities, including HVAC, purified water, compressed air, and electrical power.

**3.3.1. HVAC System Management:**
   - The HVAC system is critical for maintaining environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, differential pressure, air changes) within manufacturing areas, cleanrooms, and storage facilities. 
   - Regular maintenance, including filter changes, coil cleaning, and fan inspections, shall be performed according to the PM schedule.
   - Continuous monitoring of critical parameters (e.g., temperature, humidity, pressure differentials) shall be conducted, with alarms set for out-of-specification conditions.
   - Airflow patterns and HEPA filter integrity shall be periodically verified and documented.
   - Any deviations from specified environmental conditions shall be immediately investigated, and appropriate corrective actions implemented.

**3.3.2. Purified Water System Management:**
   - The purified water system (e.g., Purified Water, Water for Injection) is a critical utility for pharmaceutical manufacturing processes. 
   - The system shall be designed, installed, and maintained to prevent microbial contamination and ensure water quality meets pharmacopoeial standards (e.g., USP, EP, JP).
   - Routine monitoring of water quality parameters (e.g., conductivity, total organic carbon (TOC), microbial counts) shall be performed at specified points and frequencies.
   - Sanitization and passivation procedures shall be performed regularly as per the established schedule.
   - Any out-of-specification water test results shall trigger an immediate investigation and appropriate corrective actions.

**3.3.3. Compressed Air System Management:**
   - Compressed air used in manufacturing processes (e.g., for product contact, pneumatic controls) shall be of appropriate quality (e.g., oil-free, dry, filtered) to prevent contamination.
   - Regular maintenance of compressors, dryers, and filters shall be performed according to the PM schedule.
   - Air quality testing (e.g., for particulates, oil, water, viable and non-viable particles) shall be conducted periodically to ensure compliance with specified standards.
   - Pressure and flow rates shall be monitored to ensure consistent supply to critical operations.

**3.3.4. Electrical Power System Management:**
   - The electrical power system provides essential energy for all facility operations, equipment, and utility systems. 
   - Regular inspections of electrical panels, wiring, and emergency power systems (e.g., generators, UPS) shall be performed.
   - Load balancing and power quality monitoring shall be conducted to ensure stable and reliable power supply.
   - Emergency power systems shall be regularly tested to ensure their readiness in case of a power outage.
   - All electrical work shall be performed by qualified personnel and in accordance with applicable electrical codes and safety regulations.

**3.3.5. Utility System Monitoring and Alarms:**
   - All critical utility systems shall be equipped with appropriate monitoring devices and alarm systems to detect deviations from established parameters.
   - Alarms shall be configured to alert responsible personnel immediately upon detection of an out-of-specification condition.
   - Procedures for responding to and investigating utility system alarms shall be clearly defined and followed.
   - All alarm events and subsequent actions shall be documented.



### 3.4. Calibration and Qualification of Equipment

To ensure the accuracy, reliability, and suitability of equipment used in pharmaceutical manufacturing, a robust program for calibration and qualification is essential. This section outlines the procedures for managing these critical activities.

**3.4.1. Calibration Program:**
   - All measuring, weighing, and testing equipment used in manufacturing, quality control, and facility operations that can affect product quality or critical process parameters shall be included in a formal calibration program.
   - Calibration shall be performed at defined intervals using traceable standards and documented procedures.
   - Calibration procedures shall specify the equipment to be calibrated, the calibration method, acceptance criteria, and the frequency of calibration.
   - Calibration records shall include the date of calibration, the identity of the personnel performing the calibration, the results obtained (before and after adjustment), the calibration standard used, and the next due date.
   - Equipment found to be out of calibration shall be immediately removed from service, investigated for potential impact on previously manufactured products, and repaired or replaced. All investigations and corrective actions shall be documented.

**3.4.2. Equipment Qualification:**
   - All new or modified equipment, critical to manufacturing or quality control, shall undergo a formal qualification process before being placed into routine use. This process typically includes:
     - **Design Qualification (DQ):** Documented verification that the proposed design of the equipment is suitable for its intended purpose.
     - **Installation Qualification (IQ):** Documented verification that the equipment has been installed according to the manufacturer's recommendations and approved design specifications.
     - **Operational Qualification (OQ):** Documented verification that the equipment operates consistently within specified limits when performing its intended functions.
     - **Performance Qualification (PQ):** Documented verification that the equipment consistently performs as intended under actual operating conditions.
   - Re-qualification shall be performed when significant changes are made to the equipment, its intended use, or the processes it supports, or as determined by risk assessment.
   - All qualification activities shall be performed according to approved protocols, and the results shall be documented in comprehensive reports. These reports shall be reviewed and approved by relevant departments, including Quality Assurance.



### 3.5. Pest Control and Sanitation

Effective pest control and sanitation programs are essential to prevent contamination of pharmaceutical products and maintain a hygienic manufacturing environment. This section outlines the procedures for managing these critical aspects of facility maintenance.

**3.5.1. Pest Control Program:**
   - A comprehensive pest control program shall be implemented to prevent the entry, harborage, and proliferation of pests (e.g., insects, rodents, birds) within the facility.
   - The program shall include regular inspections, monitoring, and, if necessary, the application of pest control measures by qualified personnel or licensed pest control operators.
   - Pest control measures shall be selected and applied in a manner that does not pose a risk to product quality, personnel safety, or the environment.
   - All pest control activities, including observations, treatments, and corrective actions, shall be documented.
   - Entry points for pests shall be identified and sealed, and good housekeeping practices shall be maintained to eliminate potential food sources and harborage areas.

**3.5.2. Sanitation Program:**
   - A detailed sanitation program shall be established and implemented for all manufacturing areas, equipment, and non-critical surfaces to prevent microbial and particulate contamination.
   - The program shall specify:
     - Cleaning and disinfection procedures for different areas and equipment.
     - The types of cleaning agents and disinfectants to be used, their concentrations, and contact times.
     - The frequency of cleaning and disinfection activities.
     - The responsibilities of personnel performing sanitation tasks.
     - Methods for verifying the effectiveness of cleaning and disinfection (e.g., visual inspection, microbial monitoring).
   - All sanitation activities shall be documented, including the date, time, area/equipment cleaned, cleaning agents used, and the name of the personnel performing the task.
   - Cleaning tools and equipment shall be maintained in a clean and sanitary condition and stored in a manner that prevents contamination.



## 4. Documentation Requirements

Accurate, complete, and timely documentation is critical for demonstrating compliance, ensuring traceability, and supporting continuous improvement in facility maintenance and utilities management. All documentation shall adhere to cGMP principles of ALCOA (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate).

**4.1. Master Documentation:**
   - **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):** This SOP (SOP-MFG-013) and all related SOPs governing specific maintenance tasks, utility operations, calibration, and sanitation.
   - **Maintenance Plans and Schedules:** Comprehensive preventive maintenance schedules, calibration schedules, and utility monitoring plans.
   - **Equipment Manuals and Specifications:** Manufacturer's operating manuals, maintenance guides, and technical specifications for all facility equipment and utility systems.
   - **Facility Drawings and Schematics:** Up-to-date blueprints, P&IDs (Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams) for utility systems, and electrical schematics.

**4.2. Operational Records:**
   - **Work Orders:** All preventive, corrective, and emergency maintenance work orders, including details of tasks performed, personnel involved, time taken, parts used, and observations.
   - **Calibration Records:** Records of all equipment calibration activities, including raw data, certificates, and out-of-calibration investigations.
   - **Utility Monitoring Records:** Logs and charts of critical utility parameters (e.g., HVAC temperature/humidity/pressure, water quality, compressed air quality).
   - **Sanitation Logs:** Records of cleaning and disinfection activities, including areas cleaned, agents used, and personnel.
   - **Pest Control Records:** Documentation of pest inspections, treatments, and observations.
   - **Deviation Reports:** Records of any deviations from established procedures or specifications, including investigations and CAPAs.
   - **Change Control Records:** Documentation of all changes made to facilities, equipment, or utility systems.

**4.3. Record Retention:**
   - All records generated under this SOP shall be retained for a minimum of 7 years or as per regulatory requirements in a secure and easily retrievable manner.
   - Electronic records shall be maintained in validated systems with appropriate backup and security measures.



## 5. Training Requirements

All personnel involved in facility maintenance, utilities management, and those whose activities impact the facility and utility systems shall receive appropriate training to ensure they possess the necessary knowledge and skills to perform their duties effectively and in compliance with this SOP and regulatory requirements.

**5.1. Initial Training:**
   - All new employees shall receive initial training on this SOP, relevant cGMP principles, safety procedures, and job-specific tasks before they are permitted to perform their assigned duties independently.
   - Training shall include both theoretical instruction and practical demonstrations, where applicable.

**5.2. Ongoing Training and Retraining:**
   - Personnel shall receive ongoing training and retraining at regular intervals (e.g., annually) or when there are significant changes to this SOP, equipment, systems, or regulatory requirements.
   - Retraining shall also be conducted if performance deficiencies are identified or if there are changes in job responsibilities.

**5.3. Training Content:**
   - Training content shall cover, but not be limited to:
     - The purpose and scope of this SOP.
     - Specific procedures for preventive and corrective maintenance.
     - Operation and monitoring of critical utility systems.
     - Calibration and qualification procedures.
     - Pest control and sanitation practices.
     - Documentation requirements and good documentation practices.
     - Safety procedures, including Lockout/Tagout (LOTO), confined space entry, and chemical handling.
     - Relevant cGMP regulations (21 CFR Part 211) and USDA regulations.
     - Change control procedures.

**5.4. Training Records:**
   - All training activities shall be documented, including:
     - The date of training.
     - The names and signatures of the trainees and trainer.
     - The training materials used.
     - An assessment of the trainee's understanding and proficiency.
   - Training records shall be maintained by the Human Resources Department and readily available for inspection.



## 6. Quality Control Measures

Quality control measures are integral to ensuring that facility maintenance and utilities management activities consistently support the production of high-quality pharmaceutical products. These measures involve systematic monitoring, testing, and evaluation to verify compliance with established standards and specifications.

**6.1. Environmental Monitoring:**
   - Regular environmental monitoring shall be conducted in classified manufacturing areas (e.g., cleanrooms) to assess air quality, surface cleanliness, and personnel gowning effectiveness.
   - Monitoring parameters shall include viable (microbial) and non-viable (particulate) counts, temperature, humidity, and differential pressures.
   - Results shall be trended and reviewed by the Quality Assurance Department. Any excursions from action or alert limits shall trigger an investigation and appropriate corrective actions.

**6.2. Utility System Monitoring and Testing:**
   - Critical utility systems (e.g., purified water, compressed air, HVAC) shall be continuously or periodically monitored for critical quality attributes.
   - Water quality testing shall include conductivity, TOC, microbial enumeration, and endotoxin levels as per pharmacopoeial requirements.
   - Compressed air quality testing shall include dew point, particulate matter, oil content, and microbial contamination.
   - HVAC system performance shall be verified through temperature, humidity, and differential pressure mapping, as well as airflow visualization studies.
   - All monitoring and testing results shall be documented, trended, and reviewed by Quality Assurance. Out-of-specification results shall lead to investigations and CAPAs.

**6.3. Calibration and Maintenance Effectiveness Checks:**
   - The effectiveness of the calibration program shall be periodically reviewed by Quality Assurance to ensure that all critical instruments are calibrated within their specified intervals and that calibration results are accurate and reliable.
   - The effectiveness of preventive and corrective maintenance activities shall be assessed through key performance indicators (KPIs) such as equipment uptime, breakdown frequency, and adherence to PM schedules. Trends in these KPIs shall be reviewed to identify areas for improvement.

**6.4. Internal Audits and Self-Inspections:**
   - Regular internal audits and self-inspections shall be conducted by trained personnel to assess compliance with this SOP, cGMP regulations, and other relevant quality standards.
   - Audit findings shall be documented, and any non-conformances shall be addressed through the CAPA system.

**6.5. Supplier Qualification:**
   - All critical suppliers of maintenance services, spare parts, and utility components shall be qualified to ensure they meet the company's quality standards and regulatory requirements. This includes suppliers of calibration services, pest control, and specialized repair services.



## 7. Safety Considerations

Safety is a paramount concern in all facility maintenance and utilities management activities. All personnel involved in these operations must adhere to established safety procedures to prevent accidents, injuries, and exposure to hazards. This section outlines key safety considerations.

**7.1. General Safety Rules:**
   - All personnel shall be familiar with and adhere to the company's general safety rules and emergency procedures.
   - Appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) (e.g., safety glasses, gloves, hard hats, respirators) shall be worn as required for specific tasks.
   - All accidents, incidents, and near-misses shall be reported immediately to the supervisor and documented.

**7.2. Lockout/Tagout (LOTO):**
   - LOTO procedures shall be strictly followed for all maintenance or repair activities on equipment or systems where the unexpected energization, start-up, or release of stored energy could cause injury.
   - Only authorized personnel shall perform LOTO, and all energy sources shall be de-energized and locked out before commencing work.

**7.3. Confined Space Entry:**
   - Entry into confined spaces (e.g., tanks, pits, utility tunnels) shall only be permitted after proper authorization, atmospheric testing, and adherence to established confined space entry procedures.
   - A trained attendant shall be present outside the confined space during entry, and rescue equipment shall be readily available.

**7.4. Chemical Handling and Storage:**
   - All chemicals used for maintenance, cleaning, or utility treatment shall be handled, stored, and disposed of in accordance with Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) and company procedures.
   - Personnel shall be trained on the safe handling of chemicals, including spill response procedures.

**7.5. Electrical Safety:**
   - All electrical work shall be performed by qualified and authorized personnel in accordance with applicable electrical codes and safety regulations.
   - Live electrical work shall be avoided whenever possible. When unavoidable, specific permits and precautions shall be implemented.

**7.6. Working at Heights:**
   - When working at heights (e.g., on ladders, scaffolding, elevated platforms), appropriate fall protection measures shall be used, and equipment shall be inspected before use.

**7.7. Emergency Preparedness:**
   - Personnel shall be aware of emergency exits, fire extinguishers, first-aid stations, and emergency contact numbers.
   - Regular drills for fire, chemical spills, and other emergencies shall be conducted.



## 8. Regulatory Compliance References

Compliance with relevant regulatory requirements is a fundamental aspect of facility maintenance and utilities management in pharmaceutical manufacturing. This SOP is developed in accordance with the following key regulatory guidelines and standards:

### 8.1. FDA Regulations (21 CFR Part 211, cGMP)

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) establishes Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) regulations to ensure that drug products meet quality standards. For facilities and equipment, key regulations include:

-   **21 CFR Part 211, Subpart C - Buildings and Facilities:** This subpart outlines requirements for the design, construction, and maintenance of buildings and facilities to prevent contamination and facilitate cleaning, maintenance, and proper operations. It covers aspects such as adequate space, lighting, ventilation, and pest control [1].
-   **21 CFR Part 211, Subpart D - Equipment:** This subpart details requirements for equipment used in the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product. It emphasizes that equipment must be of appropriate design, adequate size, and suitably located to facilitate operations, cleaning, and maintenance. It also addresses calibration, maintenance, and automated equipment [2].
-   **FDA Guidance for Industry: Sterile Drug Products Produced by Aseptic Processing – Current Good Manufacturing Practice:** This guidance provides recommendations for facility design, environmental control, and utility systems (e.g., water, air) in sterile manufacturing operations, which are highly relevant to facility maintenance and utilities management [3].

### 8.2. USDA Regulations (Animal Welfare Act, Veterinary Biologics)

For large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations, the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) also plays a significant regulatory role, particularly concerning animal welfare and veterinary biologics:

-   **Animal Welfare Act (AWA) and Animal Welfare Regulations (9 CFR Chapter I, Subchapter A):** While primarily focused on the humane care and treatment of animals, the AWA and its regulations influence facility design and maintenance to ensure suitable housing, sanitation, and environmental conditions for animals used in research or production [4].
-   **9 CFR Part 108 - Facility and Equipment Requirements for Licensed Establishments:** This part specifies requirements for facilities and equipment used in the production of veterinary biologics, including aspects related to cleanliness, sanitation, and separation of activities to prevent contamination [5].
-   **USDA APHIS Veterinary Biologics Regulations:** These regulations govern the manufacture, testing, and distribution of veterinary biologics, with implications for facility design, maintenance, and utility systems that directly impact product quality and safety [6].

## 9. Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to facilities, equipment, utility systems, or associated processes that could impact product quality, safety, efficacy, or regulatory compliance shall be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are properly evaluated, approved, implemented, and documented.

**9.1. Initiation of Change:**
   - Any department or individual proposing a change shall initiate a Change Request (CR) form, detailing the proposed change, its rationale, and potential impact.
   - The CR shall be submitted to the Change Control Board (CCB) or designated authority for initial review.

**9.2. Evaluation and Risk Assessment:**
   - The CCB, comprising representatives from relevant departments (e.g., Facility Management, Engineering, Production, Quality Assurance), shall evaluate the proposed change.
   - A comprehensive risk assessment shall be conducted to identify potential risks to product quality, patient safety, and regulatory compliance. This assessment will determine the level of testing, validation, or qualification required.

**9.3. Approval of Change:**
   - Based on the evaluation and risk assessment, the CCB shall approve or reject the proposed change. Approval signifies that the change is deemed necessary, justified, and that appropriate measures are in place to mitigate identified risks.
   - For significant changes, approval from Quality Assurance is mandatory.

**9.4. Implementation of Change:**
   - Approved changes shall be implemented according to a detailed implementation plan, which may include:
     - Updating relevant SOPs, drawings, and other documentation.
     - Performing necessary modifications to facilities, equipment, or utility systems.
     - Conducting required testing, qualification, or validation activities.
     - Providing training to affected personnel.
   - All implementation activities shall be documented.

**9.5. Post-Implementation Review:**
   - After implementation, a post-implementation review shall be conducted to confirm that the change was effective, achieved its intended purpose, and did not introduce any unforeseen negative impacts.
   - The review shall also verify that all documentation has been updated and personnel have been adequately trained.

**9.6. Documentation:**
   - All aspects of the change control process, from initiation to closure, shall be thoroughly documented. This includes the CR form, risk assessments, approval records, implementation plans, and verification reports of qualification/validation reports, and training records.
