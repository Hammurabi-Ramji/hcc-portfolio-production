### 1.1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the detailed steps and requirements for the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals. The primary purpose of this document is to ensure consistency, quality, safety, and regulatory compliance throughout the manufacturing process. By adhering to these procedures, we aim to produce high-quality veterinary pharmaceutical products that meet all specified attributes, regulatory standards, and customer expectations. This SOP serves as a critical reference for all personnel involved in the manufacturing operations, providing clear instructions to minimize errors, prevent contamination, and ensure product integrity from raw material receipt to finished product packaging. It is designed to support the continuous improvement of our manufacturing processes and to facilitate effective training and auditing activities.

### 1.2. Scope

This SOP applies to all personnel, equipment, and facilities involved in the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals within our designated manufacturing sites. This includes, but is not limited to, activities such as raw material handling, dispensing, compounding, mixing, blending, filling, packaging, in-process quality control, equipment cleaning, and associated documentation. The scope encompasses all stages of production where the active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and excipients are transformed into a finished dosage form intended for large animal use. This document is applicable to all products manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) regulations, specifically those governed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) for veterinary drugs. It covers both sterile and non-sterile manufacturing processes, as applicable, and addresses specific considerations for large animal formulations, such as volume, dosage accuracy, and administration routes. Excluded from this scope are research and development activities, clinical trials, and distribution processes, which are covered by separate, dedicated SOPs. However, the principles and quality standards outlined herein are foundational to all related operations within the pharmaceutical lifecycle.



## 2. Responsibilities

Effective implementation of this SOP requires clear delineation of responsibilities among all personnel involved in the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals. Each role plays a crucial part in ensuring product quality, safety, and regulatory compliance. Adherence to these responsibilities is mandatory for maintaining Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and preventing deviations.

### 2.1. Manufacturing Department Personnel

**2.1.1. Production Manager:** The Production Manager is ultimately responsible for overseeing all manufacturing operations, ensuring that production schedules are met, and that all processes adhere to this SOP and relevant GMP regulations. This includes resource allocation, personnel training, performance monitoring, and initiating investigations into deviations or non-conformances. The Production Manager also approves batch records and ensures that all manufacturing activities are conducted safely and efficiently.

**2.1.2. Production Supervisors:** Production Supervisors are responsible for the direct oversight of manufacturing activities on the production floor. Their duties include: ensuring that all operators follow the procedures outlined in this SOP; verifying that equipment is properly calibrated and maintained; monitoring in-process controls; managing raw material and intermediate product flow; and ensuring accurate and timely completion of batch records. They are also responsible for initial incident reporting and corrective action implementation.

**2.1.3. Manufacturing Operators:** Manufacturing Operators are responsible for executing the batch manufacturing procedures as described in this SOP. This includes: accurate weighing and dispensing of raw materials; proper operation and cleaning of manufacturing equipment; meticulous adherence to all processing parameters; diligent recording of all manufacturing data in batch records; and immediate reporting of any deviations, equipment malfunctions, or safety concerns to their supervisor. Operators must be fully trained and qualified for the tasks they perform.

### 2.2. Quality Control (QC) Department Personnel

**2.2.1. QC Manager:** The QC Manager is responsible for the overall management of the Quality Control laboratory, ensuring that all testing procedures are performed according to approved methods and that results are accurate and reliable. This includes: approving specifications for raw materials, in-process materials, and finished products; overseeing stability studies; managing the QC budget and resources; and ensuring compliance with all regulatory requirements related to quality testing. The QC Manager also plays a key role in investigating out-of-specification (OOS) results and approving analytical methods.

**2.2.2. QC Analysts/Technicians:** QC Analysts and Technicians are responsible for performing all required analytical and microbiological testing on raw materials, in-process samples, and finished products. Their duties include: conducting tests using validated methods; accurately recording all test data; interpreting results against established specifications; maintaining and calibrating laboratory equipment; and reporting any out-of-specification or out-of-trend results to the QC Manager. They must adhere to all laboratory safety protocols and good laboratory practices (GLP).

### 2.3. Quality Assurance (QA) Department Personnel

**2.3.1. QA Manager:** The QA Manager is responsible for establishing and maintaining the Quality Management System (QMS) to ensure overall compliance with GMP regulations and internal quality standards. This includes: reviewing and approving all critical manufacturing and quality documents, including this SOP, batch records, and validation protocols; overseeing the change control system; managing deviation and CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) systems; conducting internal and external audits; and making final decisions on product release or rejection. The QA Manager acts as the primary liaison with regulatory authorities during inspections.

**2.3.2. QA Specialists:** QA Specialists are responsible for implementing and monitoring various aspects of the QMS. Their duties include: performing in-process and finished product batch record reviews; conducting line clearances; participating in deviation investigations; tracking and trending quality metrics; assisting in the preparation for regulatory inspections; and providing quality oversight for all manufacturing activities to ensure compliance with this SOP and GMP requirements. They are also involved in the training of personnel on quality-related procedures.

### 2.4. Engineering and Maintenance Department Personnel

**2.4.1. Engineering Manager:** The Engineering Manager is responsible for the design, installation, qualification, and maintenance of all manufacturing equipment and facilities. This includes: ensuring that all equipment is fit for purpose and operates within specified parameters; managing preventive maintenance programs; overseeing calibration activities; and ensuring that all utility systems (e.g., HVAC, purified water) meet the required standards for pharmaceutical manufacturing. They also play a critical role in addressing equipment-related deviations and implementing engineering controls.

**2.4.2. Maintenance Technicians:** Maintenance Technicians are responsible for performing routine maintenance, calibration, and repair of all manufacturing and laboratory equipment. Their duties include: executing preventive maintenance tasks as per schedule; responding to equipment breakdowns; documenting all maintenance and repair activities; and ensuring that all work is performed safely and without compromising product quality or facility integrity. They must follow all lockout/tagout procedures and other safety protocols.

### 2.5. Warehouse and Logistics Personnel

**2.5.1. Warehouse Supervisor:** The Warehouse Supervisor is responsible for the proper receipt, storage, and issuance of all raw materials, packaging materials, and finished products. This includes: ensuring materials are stored under appropriate conditions; maintaining accurate inventory records; managing material flow to and from the manufacturing areas; and ensuring that only approved materials are released for use. They are also responsible for adhering to FIFO (First-In, First-Out) or FEFO (First-Expiry, First-Out) principles and managing quarantined materials.

**2.5.2. Material Handlers:** Material Handlers are responsible for the physical movement of materials within the warehouse and to the manufacturing areas. Their duties include: receiving incoming shipments; verifying quantities against purchase orders; labeling and storing materials correctly; picking and staging materials for production batches; and loading finished products for shipment. They must operate forklifts and other material handling equipment safely and efficiently, and report any discrepancies or damage to materials.

### 2.6. Training Department Personnel

**2.6.1. Training Coordinator:** The Training Coordinator is responsible for developing, implementing, and maintaining the training programs for all personnel involved in manufacturing operations. This includes: identifying training needs; developing training materials; scheduling and conducting training sessions; maintaining comprehensive training records; and ensuring that all personnel are qualified and re-qualified as required by GMP regulations and internal policies. They also track training compliance and effectiveness.

All personnel are responsible for understanding and adhering to the procedures outlined in this SOP, as well as any other relevant company policies and regulatory requirements. Any deviations from this SOP must be immediately reported and documented according to the company's deviation management procedure.



## 3. Detailed Procedures

This section outlines the step-by-step procedures for the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals, ensuring adherence to Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and maintaining product quality and safety throughout the process. Each sub-section details a specific stage of the manufacturing operation.

### 3.1. Raw Material Receiving and Inspection

**3.1.1. Purpose:** To ensure that all incoming raw materials are correctly identified, inspected, and handled in a manner that prevents contamination, mix-ups, and degradation, and to verify that they meet the specified quality attributes before being released for manufacturing.

**3.1.2. Scope:** This procedure applies to all raw materials, active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), and excipients received at the manufacturing facility for use in the production of large animal pharmaceuticals.

**3.1.3. Responsibilities:**
*   **Warehouse Personnel:** Responsible for receiving, initial inspection, labeling, and proper storage of incoming materials.
*   **Quality Control (QC) Personnel:** Responsible for sampling, testing, and approving or rejecting raw materials.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Personnel:** Responsible for reviewing documentation and releasing materials for use.

**3.1.4. Procedure:**

**3.1.4.1. Receiving Materials:**
1.  **Verification of Delivery:** Upon arrival, the delivery vehicle and containers must be visually inspected for any signs of damage, tampering, or contamination. Any discrepancies or damage must be immediately reported to the Warehouse Supervisor and documented.
2.  **Unloading:** Materials must be unloaded carefully using appropriate equipment (e.g., forklifts, pallet jacks) to prevent damage to containers or contents. Ensure that the unloading area is clean and free from debris.
3.  **Initial Identification:** Each container or pallet received must be cross-referenced with the accompanying shipping documents (e.g., packing list, bill of lading) to verify the supplier, product name, quantity, and lot number. Any discrepancies must be noted and investigated.
4.  **Quarantine Placement:** All incoming raw materials, regardless of their initial status, must be immediately transferred to a designated “Quarantine” area. This area must be clearly marked and physically segregated from approved or rejected materials to prevent inadvertent use.
5.  **Generation of Receiving Record:** For each incoming lot, a unique Receiving Record must be generated. This record must include: date of receipt, supplier name, product name, internal material code, lot number, quantity received, number of containers, and any observations during initial inspection. This record will serve as the primary document for material traceability.
6.  **Labeling:** Each individual container of raw material must be affixed with a “Quarantine” label. This label must include: product name, internal material code, lot number, quantity, date of receipt, and a unique quarantine number. The label must be durable and resistant to environmental conditions in the storage area.

**3.1.4.2. Sampling and Testing (QC Responsibility):**
1.  **Sampling Request:** Once materials are in quarantine, the Warehouse Personnel will notify QC that materials are ready for sampling. QC will generate a sampling request based on the Receiving Record.
2.  **Controlled Sampling Environment:** Sampling must be performed in a designated, controlled sampling booth or area to prevent contamination of the material and the environment. The sampling area must be cleaned and sanitized before and after each sampling event.
3.  **Aseptic Sampling (for sterile materials):** For sterile raw materials or those requiring aseptic processing, sampling must be performed under aseptic conditions (e.g., in a laminar airflow hood or isolator) using sterile sampling tools and containers. Personnel must wear appropriate sterile gowning.
4.  **Sampling Procedure:** Follow the specific sampling plan outlined in the material’s monograph or specification. This typically involves taking a statistically representative number of samples from different containers within the lot. Ensure that the sampling tools are clean, dry, and appropriate for the material being sampled.
5.  **Sample Labeling:** Each sample container must be clearly labeled with: product name, lot number, sample number, date of sampling, and the initials of the sampler. Samples must be transported to the QC laboratory under conditions that maintain their integrity.
6.  **Laboratory Testing:** QC Analysts will perform all required tests as per the approved raw material specification. This may include, but is not limited to, identity, purity, potency, heavy metals, microbial limits, and other physical or chemical attributes. All testing must be performed using validated analytical methods.
7.  **Documentation of Results:** All test results must be accurately recorded in the laboratory information management system (LIMS) or on designated QC worksheets. Any out-of-specification (OOS) or out-of-trend (OOT) results must be immediately reported and investigated according to the OOS/OOT procedure.

**3.1.4.3. Release or Rejection (QA Responsibility):**
1.  **QC Report Generation:** Upon completion of all required testing, QC will generate a Certificate of Analysis (CoA) or a comprehensive QC Report summarizing all test results and indicating whether the material meets specifications.
2.  **QA Review:** QA Personnel will review the Receiving Record, supplier’s CoA (if applicable), and the internal QC Report to ensure all documentation is complete, accurate, and that the material complies with all established specifications and regulatory requirements.
3.  **Material Status Change:**
    *   **Approved Materials:** If all documentation and test results are satisfactory, QA will change the status of the material from “Quarantine” to “Approved” in the inventory system. The “Quarantine” labels will be replaced with “Approved” labels, indicating the material is released for manufacturing. Approved materials must be moved to a designated “Approved” storage area.
    *   **Rejected Materials:** If the material fails to meet any specification or if there are significant documentation discrepancies, QA will change the status of the material to “Rejected.” “Quarantine” labels will be replaced with “Rejected” labels, and the material must be physically moved to a designated “Rejected” or “Non-Conforming” material area, pending disposition (e.g., return to supplier, destruction). A Non-Conformance Report (NCR) must be initiated.
4.  **Documentation Archiving:** All receiving, sampling, testing, and release/rejection documentation must be securely archived as per document retention policies. This ensures full traceability and audit readiness.



### 3.2. Dispensing and Preparation

**3.2.1. Purpose:** To ensure accurate weighing and dispensing of raw materials according to the batch manufacturing record (BMR) and to prepare them for subsequent processing steps, minimizing contamination and ensuring material integrity.

**3.2.2. Scope:** This procedure applies to all raw materials, active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), and excipients that have been released by Quality Assurance (QA) and are required for the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals.

**3.2.3. Responsibilities:**
*   **Manufacturing Operators:** Responsible for accurate dispensing, proper labeling, and preparation of materials.
*   **Production Supervisors:** Responsible for verifying dispensing accuracy and overseeing the preparation process.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Personnel:** Responsible for line clearance and in-process checks.

**3.2.4. Procedure:**

**3.2.4.1. Preparation for Dispensing:**
1.  **Review Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR):** The Manufacturing Operator must obtain the approved BMR for the specific batch to be manufactured. Review the BMR to understand the exact quantities of each raw material required, the order of addition, and any special handling instructions.
2.  **Clean Dispensing Area:** Ensure the dispensing area, including scales, utensils, and containers, is thoroughly cleaned and sanitized according to the relevant cleaning SOP. A line clearance must be performed by QA or Production Supervisor to confirm the area is ready and free from previous batch residues or foreign materials.
3.  **Equipment Calibration Check:** Verify that all weighing scales and dispensing equipment are within their calibration due dates and have been properly calibrated. Perform a daily calibration check using certified weights before commencing dispensing activities.
4.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Don appropriate PPE as specified in the BMR or Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) for each raw material. This may include respirators, gloves, safety glasses, and protective clothing.
5.  **Material Retrieval:** Retrieve the required approved raw materials from the designated “Approved” storage area. Verify that the material name, lot number, and expiry date match those specified in the BMR. Only materials with an “Approved” status label may be used.

**3.2.4.2. Accurate Dispensing:**
1.  **Tare Weight:** Place the clean, appropriately sized dispensing container (e.g., drum, bag, beaker) on the scale and tare the weight to zero.
2.  **Dispensing:** Carefully dispense the raw material into the container, gradually adding until the exact weight specified in the BMR is achieved. For highly potent or hazardous materials, dispensing should occur within a contained environment (e.g., fume hood, isolator) to prevent exposure and cross-contamination.
3.  **Double-Check Weighing:** After dispensing, the Manufacturing Operator must verify the dispensed weight against the BMR. A second qualified Manufacturing Operator or Production Supervisor must independently verify and sign off on the dispensed weight in the BMR. This double-check is critical for accuracy.
4.  **Labeling Dispensed Materials:** Immediately after dispensing and verification, affix a “Dispensed” label to each container. The label must include: product name, internal material code, lot number, dispensed weight, date of dispensing, batch number, and the signatures of both the dispensing operator and the verifying operator.
5.  **Return Excess Material:** Any remaining raw material in the original container must be re-sealed, re-labeled with its current status, and returned to the “Approved” storage area. Ensure that the inventory system is updated to reflect the dispensed quantity.

**3.2.4.3. Preparation for Processing:**
1.  **Staging:** Transport the accurately dispensed and labeled materials to the designated staging area for the next manufacturing step (e.g., mixing, blending). Ensure that materials are protected from contamination during transport.
2.  **Pre-Mixing/Pre-Dissolving (if required):** If the BMR specifies pre-mixing or pre-dissolving certain components, perform these steps according to the detailed instructions in the BMR. This may involve using specific solvents, temperatures, or mixing speeds. Document all parameters and observations.
3.  **Documentation:** All dispensing and preparation activities, including weights, dates, times, and operator signatures, must be meticulously recorded in the Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR). Any deviations from the BMR must be documented, investigated, and approved by QA.
4.  **Area Cleanliness:** After dispensing and preparation are complete, thoroughly clean the dispensing area and all used equipment according to the cleaning SOP. Prepare the area for the next dispensing activity.



### 3.3. Mixing and Blending

**3.3.1. Purpose:** To ensure uniform distribution of all active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and excipients within the batch, achieving homogeneity and consistent product quality according to established specifications.

**3.3.2. Scope:** This procedure applies to all mixing and blending operations for the production of large animal pharmaceuticals, including both liquid and solid dosage forms.

**3.3.3. Responsibilities:**
*   **Manufacturing Operators:** Responsible for executing mixing/blending operations and monitoring process parameters.
*   **Production Supervisors:** Responsible for overseeing the mixing/blending process and verifying critical parameters.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Personnel:** Responsible for line clearance and in-process quality checks.

**3.3.4. Procedure:**

**3.3.4.1. Preparation for Mixing/Blending:**
1.  **Review Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR):** The Manufacturing Operator must thoroughly review the BMR for specific instructions regarding mixing/blending parameters, including equipment to be used, mixing speeds, temperatures, duration, and order of addition.
2.  **Equipment Preparation:** Ensure that the designated mixing or blending equipment (e.g., high-shear mixer, ribbon blender, planetary mixer, tank with agitator) has been thoroughly cleaned and sanitized according to its specific cleaning SOP. Verify that the equipment is in good working order and that all necessary components (e.g., impellers, baffles, seals) are correctly installed.
3.  **Line Clearance:** A Production Supervisor or QA personnel must perform a line clearance to ensure that the mixing/blending area is free from materials and documentation from previous batches, and that all necessary materials and equipment for the current batch are present and verified.
4.  **Environmental Controls:** Verify that environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity, differential pressure) in the mixing/blending suite are within the specified limits as per the BMR or facility requirements. For sensitive products, ensure appropriate containment measures are in place.
5.  **Material Staging:** Ensure all pre-weighed and dispensed raw materials are staged in an organized manner, clearly labeled, and readily accessible for addition to the mixer/blender in the specified sequence.

**3.3.4.2. Execution of Mixing/Blending:**
1.  **Material Addition:** Add raw materials to the mixing/blending equipment strictly in the order and manner specified in the BMR. For liquid formulations, this may involve dissolving solids in solvents, or dispersing insoluble components. For solid formulations, this involves adding powders in a specific sequence to achieve uniform distribution.
2.  **Mixing/Blending Parameters:** Initiate the mixing/blending process according to the BMR’s specified parameters. This includes:
    *   **Speed:** Set the mixer/blender to the designated RPM or speed setting.
    *   **Duration:** Mix/blend for the exact time specified. Use a timer to ensure accurate duration.
    *   **Temperature (if applicable):** Monitor and control the temperature of the batch if a specific temperature range is required (e.g., for dissolution, reaction, or stability). Document temperature readings at specified intervals.
    *   **Pressure (if applicable):** For vacuum mixing or pressure-controlled processes, monitor and record pressure readings.
3.  **In-Process Monitoring:** Continuously monitor the mixing/blending process for any anomalies, such as unusual sounds, vibrations, changes in material consistency, or foaming. Document all observations in the BMR.
4.  **Sampling for In-Process Control (IPC):** At specified intervals or upon completion of the mixing/blending step, take in-process samples as per the BMR. These samples are typically sent to QC for homogeneity testing, pH, specific gravity, viscosity, or other critical quality attributes. Ensure samples are representative of the entire batch.
5.  **Adjustments (if necessary):** If IPC results indicate that the batch is not meeting specifications (e.g., homogeneity issues, pH out of range), follow the BMR’s instructions for making adjustments. Any adjustments (e.g., additional mixing time, pH adjustment) must be documented in detail in the BMR, including the rationale, materials added, and re-testing results.

**3.3.4.3. Completion of Mixing/Blending:**
1.  **Verification of Homogeneity:** Once the mixing/blending duration is complete and IPC results confirm homogeneity and other critical parameters, the batch is considered ready for the next step.
2.  **Transfer of Material:** Carefully transfer the mixed/blended material to the next processing stage (e.g., filling, further processing) using appropriate transfer methods (e.g., pumping, gravity feed, manual transfer). Ensure that transfer lines and containers are clean and suitable for the product.
3.  **Equipment Shutdown and Cleaning:** Upon completion of material transfer, shut down the mixing/blending equipment according to the manufacturer’s instructions and initiate the cleaning process as per the equipment cleaning SOP. The equipment must be cleaned immediately to prevent material drying and hardening.
4.  **Documentation:** All mixing/blending activities, including start/end times, equipment used, parameters, IPC results, operator signatures, and any deviations or adjustments, must be meticulously recorded in the Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR). The BMR must be signed off by the Manufacturing Operator and verified by the Production Supervisor.



### 3.4. Filling and Packaging

**3.4.1. Purpose:** To accurately fill and package the manufactured large animal pharmaceutical product into its primary and secondary containers, ensuring product integrity, sterility (if applicable), proper labeling, and protection from contamination or damage.

**3.4.2. Scope:** This procedure applies to all filling and packaging operations for liquid, semi-solid, or solid large animal pharmaceutical products, from bulk product transfer to final packaged unit.

**3.4.3. Responsibilities:**
*   **Manufacturing Operators:** Responsible for operating filling and packaging equipment, monitoring fill weights/volumes, and ensuring correct labeling.
*   **Production Supervisors:** Responsible for overseeing the filling and packaging lines, performing line clearances, and verifying in-process checks.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Personnel:** Responsible for in-process quality checks, line clearances, and final product release review.

**3.4.4. Procedure:**

**3.4.4.1. Preparation for Filling and Packaging:**
1.  **Review Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR):** The Manufacturing Operator must review the BMR for specific instructions related to filling volumes/weights, packaging configurations, labeling requirements, and any special handling instructions for the product.
2.  **Line Clearance:** A Production Supervisor or QA personnel must perform a thorough line clearance before starting the filling and packaging operations. This ensures that the line is free from any materials, labels, or components from previous batches, preventing mix-ups. All equipment must be clean and verified as ready for use.
3.  **Equipment Setup and Verification:** Set up the filling and packaging equipment (e.g., filling machines, capping machines, labeling machines, cartoners) according to the BMR and equipment-specific SOPs. Verify that all change parts are correctly installed and that the equipment is in good working order. Perform pre-operational checks as required.
4.  **Component Staging:** Stage all primary packaging components (e.g., bottles, vials, syringes, caps, stoppers), secondary packaging components (e.g., cartons, inserts, leaflets), and labels in an organized manner. Verify that all components match the BMR and have been released by QA.
5.  **Environmental Controls:** Ensure that the filling and packaging areas maintain the required environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity, particulate count, differential pressure) as specified in the BMR or facility requirements. For sterile products, filling must occur in classified cleanrooms under aseptic conditions.

**3.4.4.2. Execution of Filling:**
1.  **Bulk Product Transfer:** Carefully transfer the bulk pharmaceutical product from the mixing/blending vessel to the filling machine’s hopper or reservoir. Ensure that transfer lines are clean and appropriate for the product. For sterile products, maintain aseptic connections.
2.  **Filling Machine Setup:** Calibrate the filling machine to dispense the exact volume or weight specified in the BMR. Perform initial test fills and adjust as necessary. Document all adjustments.
3.  **In-Process Fill Weight/Volume Checks:** Manufacturing Operators must regularly perform in-process checks of fill weights or volumes at specified intervals (e.g., every 15-30 minutes). Record these measurements in the BMR. Any deviations from the acceptable range must trigger immediate investigation and adjustment.
4.  **Visual Inspection during Filling:** Operators must continuously visually inspect containers during filling for defects, foreign particles, or incorrect fill levels. Defective units must be immediately removed from the line and segregated as reject material.
5.  **Capping/Sealing:** Immediately after filling, containers must be capped or sealed using the appropriate equipment. Verify that caps are securely applied and that seals are intact to prevent leakage or contamination.

**3.4.4.3. Execution of Packaging and Labeling:**
1.  **Label Application:** Apply labels to the primary containers using automated labeling equipment or manually, as specified. Ensure that labels are correctly oriented, securely affixed, and free from wrinkles or tears. Verify that the correct label (product name, strength, lot number, expiry date) is being applied.
2.  **Label Reconciliation:** At the beginning and end of each labeling run, perform a label reconciliation. Count the number of labels issued, used, and returned/destroyed. Any discrepancies must be investigated and documented.
3.  **Secondary Packaging:** Place the labeled primary containers into secondary packaging (e.g., cartons, blister packs) along with any required inserts or leaflets. Ensure that the correct components are used and that the packaging configuration matches the BMR.
4.  **Batch Coding:** Apply the batch number, expiry date, and any other required variable data to the secondary packaging using appropriate coding equipment (e.g., inkjet printer, hot stamp). Verify the legibility and accuracy of the printed information.
5.  **Final Packaging and Palletization:** Pack the finished units into shipping cases and palletize them according to standard operating procedures. Ensure that pallets are stable, properly wrapped, and clearly labeled with product name, lot number, quantity, and destination.

**3.4.4.4. Documentation:**
1.  All filling and packaging activities, including equipment settings, in-process check results, component reconciliation, and any deviations, must be meticulously recorded in the Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR). Each step must be signed and dated by the performing operator and verified by the Production Supervisor or QA personnel.
2.  Any rejected or damaged materials/components must be documented and segregated for proper disposition according to the Non-Conforming Material SOP.
3.  Upon completion, the BMR must be reviewed by Production and QA for completeness and accuracy before the batch is moved to the finished product quarantine area.



### 3.5. In-Process Quality Control

**3.5.1. Purpose:** To monitor and control critical process parameters and product attributes during the manufacturing cycle, ensuring that the batch remains within specified limits and meets quality standards before proceeding to subsequent stages.

**3.5.2. Scope:** This procedure applies to all in-process quality control (IPC) tests conducted during the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals, from raw material dispensing through packaging.

**3.5.3. Responsibilities:**
*   **Manufacturing Operators:** Responsible for performing routine IPC checks as specified in the BMR and reporting results.
*   **Production Supervisors:** Responsible for overseeing IPC activities, ensuring adherence to procedures, and initiating investigations for out-of-limit results.
*   **Quality Control (QC) Personnel:** Responsible for performing complex IPC tests, analyzing samples, and providing analytical support.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Personnel:** Responsible for reviewing IPC data, approving deviations, and ensuring overall compliance.

**3.5.4. Procedure:**

**3.5.4.1. General IPC Principles:**
1.  **Defined Test Points:** IPC tests are performed at predetermined stages of the manufacturing process as outlined in the Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR) and product specifications. These points are selected based on critical process parameters and critical quality attributes.
2.  **Sampling Frequency:** The frequency of IPC sampling is specified in the BMR and is based on risk assessment, batch size, and process variability. Adherence to the specified frequency is mandatory.
3.  **Sampling Technique:** Samples must be collected using aseptic techniques (where applicable) and representative methods to ensure the sample accurately reflects the state of the entire batch at that specific point in time. Sampling tools must be clean, appropriate for the material, and dedicated or thoroughly cleaned between uses.
4.  **Documentation:** All IPC results, including raw data, calculations, observations, and the identity of the performing operator, must be immediately and accurately recorded in the BMR or designated IPC log sheets. Any deviations from expected results or procedures must be documented and reported.
5.  **Equipment Calibration:** All equipment used for IPC testing (e.g., pH meters, viscometers, balances, thermometers) must be calibrated and maintained according to their respective SOPs and be within their calibration due dates.

**3.5.4.2. Common IPC Tests and Parameters (Examples):**

*   **During Dispensing:**
    *   **Weight Verification:** Double-check of dispensed raw material weights by a second operator or supervisor. (Refer to Section 3.2.4.2.3)

*   **During Mixing/Blending:**
    *   **Homogeneity:** Visual inspection for uniform appearance (e.g., color, consistency) for liquid and semi-solid products. For solid blends, samples may be taken for content uniformity testing by QC.
    *   **pH:** Measurement of pH for liquid formulations to ensure it is within the specified range, critical for product stability and efficacy. Adjustments must be documented.
    *   **Viscosity:** Measurement of viscosity for liquid or semi-solid products, important for flow properties and filling accuracy.
    *   **Specific Gravity/Density:** Measurement to confirm correct concentration and composition.
    *   **Particle Size Distribution:** For suspensions or emulsions, analysis of particle or droplet size to ensure stability and proper dispersion.
    *   **Temperature:** Monitoring and recording of batch temperature, especially for processes requiring specific temperature ranges.

*   **During Filling:**
    *   **Fill Weight/Volume:** Regular checks of the amount of product dispensed into each primary container to ensure accuracy and consistency. (Refer to Section 3.4.4.2.3)
    *   **Container/Closure Integrity:** Visual inspection of containers and closures for defects, proper sealing, and absence of leaks.
    *   **Visual Inspection:** Continuous visual inspection for foreign particles, cosmetic defects, or damaged containers.

*   **During Packaging:**
    *   **Label Verification:** Confirmation that the correct label is applied, it is legible, correctly oriented, and securely affixed. Verification of batch number and expiry date accuracy.
    *   **Component Verification:** Check that all required packaging components (e.g., inserts, leaflets, caps) are present and correct.
    *   **Code Legibility:** Verification of the legibility and accuracy of batch codes and expiry dates printed on primary and secondary packaging.
    *   **Packaging Integrity:** Inspection of cartons, seals, and other packaging elements for damage or defects.

**3.5.4.3. Handling Out-of-Specification (OOS) or Out-of-Trend (OOT) Results:**
1.  **Immediate Notification:** Any IPC result that falls outside the specified limits (OOS) or shows an undesirable trend (OOT) must be immediately reported to the Production Supervisor and QA personnel.
2.  **Investigation:** A formal investigation must be initiated by QA, in conjunction with Production and QC, to determine the root cause of the OOS/OOT result. This may involve re-testing, reviewing batch records, inspecting equipment, or evaluating raw materials.
3.  **Impact Assessment:** Assess the potential impact of the OOS/OOT result on the quality, safety, and efficacy of the current batch and any potentially affected previous batches.
4.  **Corrective Action:** Based on the investigation findings, appropriate corrective actions must be implemented. This could include adjusting process parameters, re-processing the batch (if permissible and validated), or rejecting the batch. All actions must be documented.
5.  **Documentation:** All OOS/OOT investigations, findings, and corrective actions must be thoroughly documented in a Deviation Report or Non-Conformance Report (NCR) and approved by QA. No batch affected by an OOS/OOT result can proceed to the next stage or be released without a formal disposition from QA.



### 3.6. Equipment Cleaning and Maintenance

**3.6.1. Purpose:** To establish procedures for the effective cleaning, sanitization, and maintenance of all manufacturing equipment and associated areas to prevent cross-contamination, microbial growth, and equipment malfunction, thereby ensuring product quality and operational efficiency.

**3.6.2. Scope:** This procedure applies to all equipment used in the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals, including but not limited to mixers, blenders, filling machines, pumps, hoses, and associated processing areas.

**3.6.3. Responsibilities:**
*   **Manufacturing Operators:** Responsible for performing routine cleaning and basic maintenance tasks as per schedule.
*   **Maintenance Technicians:** Responsible for performing preventive maintenance, calibrations, and repairs.
*   **Production Supervisors:** Responsible for ensuring cleaning and maintenance schedules are adhered to and documented.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA) Personnel:** Responsible for reviewing cleaning validation records and approving cleaning procedures.

**3.6.4. Procedure:**

**3.6.4.1. General Cleaning Principles:**
1.  **Clean-in-Place (CIP) and Clean-out-of-Place (COP):** Determine whether equipment requires CIP (cleaning without disassembly) or COP (cleaning with disassembly) based on equipment design and product contact surfaces. Specific cleaning procedures for each piece of equipment will be detailed in separate, equipment-specific cleaning SOPs.
2.  **Cleaning Agents:** Use only approved cleaning agents and sanitizers at the specified concentrations and contact times. Ensure that cleaning agents are compatible with the equipment materials and do not leave harmful residues.
3.  **Dedicated Equipment:** Where feasible, dedicate equipment to specific products or product families to minimize the risk of cross-contamination. If dedication is not possible, robust cleaning validation is essential.
4.  **Cleaning Validation:** All cleaning procedures must be validated to demonstrate their effectiveness in removing product residues, cleaning agent residues, and microorganisms to acceptable levels. Cleaning validation studies must be approved by QA.
5.  **Documentation:** All cleaning activities, including date, time, equipment cleaned, cleaning agent used, and the identity of the performing operator, must be meticulously documented in equipment logbooks or batch records.

**3.6.4.2. Routine Cleaning Procedures (After Each Batch/Campaign):**
1.  **Pre-Rinse:** Immediately after product discharge, perform a pre-rinse with potable water to remove gross product residues. This prevents drying and hardening of residues, making subsequent cleaning easier.
2.  **Disassembly (for COP):** If required, carefully disassemble equipment parts that come into contact with the product. Ensure all parts are accounted for.
3.  **Washing:** Apply approved cleaning agents using appropriate methods (e.g., spraying, scrubbing, soaking) to all product contact and non-product contact surfaces. Ensure adequate contact time as specified in the cleaning SOP.
4.  **Rinsing:** Thoroughly rinse all surfaces with purified water (e.g., Purified Water USP, Water for Injection USP) until all cleaning agent residues are removed. Conductivity testing of the final rinse water may be performed to confirm cleanliness.
5.  **Sanitization (if required):** For equipment used in aseptic processes or for products susceptible to microbial contamination, apply an approved sanitizer at the specified concentration and contact time. Follow with a final rinse if required by the sanitizer’s instructions.
6.  **Drying:** Ensure all equipment surfaces are thoroughly dried using filtered air or other approved methods to prevent microbial growth. Visually inspect for cleanliness and dryness.
7.  **Reassembly (for COP):** Carefully reassemble the equipment, ensuring all parts are correctly installed and torqued (if applicable).
8.  **“Clean” Status Labeling:** Once cleaned, dried, and inspected, affix a “Clean” status label to the equipment, indicating the date of cleaning and the next scheduled use or cleaning due date.

**3.6.4.3. Preventive Maintenance (PM):**
1.  **PM Schedule:** Implement a comprehensive preventive maintenance program for all manufacturing equipment. The PM schedule must be based on manufacturer recommendations, equipment criticality, and historical performance data.
2.  **Scheduled Tasks:** PM tasks include routine inspections, lubrication, replacement of worn parts, filter changes, and functional checks. All PM activities must be performed by qualified Maintenance Technicians.
3.  **Documentation:** All PM activities, including tasks performed, parts replaced, date, and the identity of the technician, must be documented in the equipment’s maintenance logbook or CMMS (Computerized Maintenance Management System).

**3.6.4.4. Calibration:**
1.  **Calibration Schedule:** All critical measuring and control equipment (e.g., scales, thermometers, pressure gauges, flow meters) must be calibrated at defined intervals using traceable standards. The calibration schedule must be maintained and adhered to.
2.  **Calibration Procedure:** Calibrations must be performed by qualified personnel (internal or external) according to approved calibration SOPs. Out-of-tolerance instruments must be immediately taken out of service and repaired or replaced.
3.  **Documentation:** Calibration records, including calibration date, next due date, results, and the identity of the calibrator, must be maintained for each instrument.

**3.6.4.5. Handling Equipment Malfunctions and Repairs:**
1.  **Reporting:** Any equipment malfunction or breakdown must be immediately reported to the Production Supervisor and Maintenance Department.
2.  **Troubleshooting and Repair:** Qualified Maintenance Technicians will troubleshoot the issue and perform necessary repairs. If external service is required, ensure that the service provider is qualified and that their work adheres to GMP principles.
3.  **Impact Assessment:** Assess the impact of the malfunction on product quality and safety. If a batch was processed using malfunctioning equipment, a deviation investigation must be initiated.
4.  **Re-qualification/Re-validation:** After significant repairs or modifications, equipment may require re-qualification or re-validation before being returned to service. This decision must be made by Engineering and QA.
5.  **Documentation:** All repair activities, including the nature of the malfunction, corrective actions taken, parts replaced, and the date of repair, must be documented in the equipment’s maintenance logbook.



## 4. Documentation Requirements

Accurate, complete, and timely documentation is a cornerstone of Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and is essential for ensuring traceability, accountability, and regulatory compliance in the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals. All records must be maintained in a legible, indelible, and accessible manner.

### 4.1. Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR)

The Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR) is the primary document that details the complete manufacturing history of each batch of product. It serves as a comprehensive record of all materials used, processes performed, in-process controls, and personnel involved. Each BMR must include:

*   **Product Name and Code:** Clear identification of the pharmaceutical product.
*   **Batch Number:** A unique identifier for each manufactured batch.
*   **Manufacturing Date:** Date of commencement and completion of the manufacturing process.
*   **Expiration Date:** The determined shelf-life of the product.
*   **Master Batch Record Reference:** A reference to the approved Master Batch Record (MBR) from which the BMR is derived.
*   **Raw Material Details:** List of all raw materials, including their names, internal codes, lot numbers, dispensed quantities, and verification signatures.
*   **Equipment Used:** Identification of all major equipment used, including their unique IDs and cleaning status.
*   **Process Parameters:** Detailed recording of critical process parameters (e.g., mixing speeds, temperatures, pressures, durations) with actual values and acceptable ranges.
*   **In-Process Control (IPC) Results:** All IPC test results, including measurements, observations, and the identity of the performing and verifying personnel.
*   **Deviations and Investigations:** Any deviations from the MBR or this SOP must be documented, including a description of the deviation, investigation findings, root cause, impact assessment, and corrective/preventive actions (CAPAs) taken. All deviations must be formally approved by Quality Assurance (QA).
*   **Operator Signatures and Dates:** Signatures and dates of all personnel performing each step, along with verification signatures where required.
*   **Yield Calculation:** Actual yield at various stages and final yield, compared against theoretical yield, with any significant discrepancies investigated.
*   **Packaging and Labeling Details:** Records of packaging components used, label reconciliation, and verification of printed information (batch number, expiry date).
*   **QA Review and Release:** Final review and approval by QA personnel before the batch is released for distribution.

### 4.2. Equipment Logbooks

Dedicated logbooks must be maintained for all major manufacturing equipment. These logbooks provide a chronological record of equipment usage, cleaning, maintenance, and calibration. Each entry must be dated and signed. Required information includes:

*   **Equipment ID:** Unique identifier for the equipment.
*   **Date and Time:** Of use, cleaning, maintenance, or calibration.
*   **Product/Batch Number:** For which the equipment was used.
*   **Cleaning Status:** Indication of cleaning performed (e.g., 'Cleaned', 'Dirty').
*   **Maintenance/Calibration Details:** Description of work performed, parts replaced, and next due date.
*   **Operator/Technician Signature:** Identity of the person performing the activity.

### 4.3. Raw Material and Packaging Material Records

Comprehensive records must be maintained for all incoming raw materials and packaging components. These records ensure traceability from receipt to final product. Key documents include:

*   **Receiving Records:** Documenting receipt date, supplier, material name, lot number, quantity, and initial inspection observations.
*   **Certificates of Analysis (CoA):** From the supplier, verifying material specifications.
*   **Internal Quality Control (QC) Reports:** Documenting results of in-house testing and release status.
*   **Material Status Labels:** Records of quarantine, approved, and rejected status changes.

### 4.4. Quality Control (QC) Records

All QC testing activities must be thoroughly documented. This includes:

*   **Laboratory Notebooks/LIMS Entries:** Detailed records of analytical procedures, raw data, calculations, and results for all raw material, in-process, and finished product testing.
*   **Instrument Calibration Records:** Documentation of all calibration activities for laboratory equipment.
*   **Stability Study Data:** Records of ongoing stability testing to support product shelf-life.
*   **Out-of-Specification (OOS) and Out-of-Trend (OOT) Investigations:** Detailed reports of investigations into non-conforming test results.

### 4.5. Training Records

Individual training records must be maintained for all personnel involved in manufacturing operations. These records demonstrate that personnel are qualified to perform their assigned duties. (Refer to Section 5.3 for more details).

### 4.6. Deviation and CAPA Records

All deviations from established procedures, specifications, or expected results must be documented in a Deviation Report. This report must trigger an investigation and, if necessary, the initiation of Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPAs). Records must include:

*   **Deviation Description:** Clear and concise description of the non-conformance.
*   **Investigation Findings:** Root cause analysis and impact assessment.
*   **Corrective Actions:** Immediate actions taken to address the deviation.
*   **Preventive Actions:** Actions implemented to prevent recurrence.
*   **Effectiveness Checks:** Verification that CAPAs have been effective.
*   **Approval Signatures:** Signatures of all relevant personnel (e.g., Production, QC, QA) approving the deviation and CAPA.

### 4.7. Change Control Records

Any planned changes to raw materials, equipment, processes, or documentation must be managed through a formal Change Control system. (Refer to Section 9 for more details).

### 4.8. Document Retention

All documentation must be retained for a period specified by regulatory requirements (e.g., 1 year after the expiration date of the batch or 3 years after distribution of the batch, whichever is longer, as per 21 CFR 211.180). Records must be stored in a secure, environmentally controlled location to prevent degradation and ensure easy retrieval during audits or inspections.

## 6. Quality Control Measures

Quality Control (QC) measures are integral to the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals, ensuring that all raw materials, in-process materials, and finished products consistently meet established quality attributes, specifications, and regulatory requirements. These measures are designed to detect and prevent deviations, ultimately safeguarding product quality and patient safety.

### 6.1. Raw Material Quality Control

1.  **Supplier Qualification:** All suppliers of raw materials and packaging components must be qualified and approved based on their ability to consistently provide materials that meet specified quality standards. This includes audits of supplier facilities and review of their quality systems.
2.  **Incoming Material Testing:** As detailed in Section 3.1, all incoming raw materials are subjected to comprehensive testing (e.g., identity, purity, potency, microbial limits) by the QC laboratory according to approved specifications and validated analytical methods. Only materials that pass all tests are released for use in manufacturing.
3.  **Certificate of Analysis (CoA) Review:** The supplier’s CoA is reviewed against internal specifications. While CoAs provide valuable information, critical raw materials typically require in-house testing to confirm their quality.
4.  **Retention Samples:** A representative retention sample from each lot of raw material is retained for a specified period (e.g., one year after the expiration date of the finished product) to allow for re-testing if necessary during investigations.

### 6.2. In-Process Quality Control (IPC)

IPC measures are performed at critical stages throughout the manufacturing process to monitor and control key parameters and attributes. These are detailed in Section 3.5 and include:

1.  **Weight/Volume Checks:** Verification of dispensed raw material weights and fill volumes during filling operations to ensure accuracy and consistency.
2.  **Homogeneity Testing:** Assessment of the uniformity of mixtures and blends (e.g., visual inspection, content uniformity testing).
3.  **pH Measurement:** For liquid formulations, pH is a critical parameter affecting stability, solubility, and efficacy.
4.  **Viscosity/Specific Gravity:** Measurements to ensure consistency of physical properties.
5.  **Particle Size Distribution:** For suspensions or emulsions, ensuring particles/droplets are within specified ranges.
6.  **Visual Inspection:** Continuous visual inspection for foreign particles, discoloration, or other defects during various stages.
7.  **Environmental Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of manufacturing environments (e.g., cleanrooms) for particulate matter and microbial contamination, especially for sterile products.

### 6.3. Finished Product Quality Control

1.  **Sampling:** Representative samples of the finished product from each batch are collected according to a statistically sound sampling plan.
2.  **Comprehensive Testing:** Finished products undergo a full battery of tests by the QC laboratory to ensure they meet all release specifications. This typically includes:
    *   **Assay/Potency:** To confirm the active ingredient content.
    *   **Purity/Impurity Profile:** To ensure the absence of harmful impurities.
    *   **Dissolution/Disintegration:** For solid dosage forms, to ensure proper drug release.
    *   **Sterility Testing:** For sterile products, to confirm the absence of viable microorganisms.
    *   **Endotoxin Testing:** For parenteral products, to ensure endotoxin levels are within limits.
    *   **Physical Attributes:** (e.g., appearance, color, odor, pH, viscosity, specific gravity).
    *   **Microbial Limits:** For non-sterile products, to ensure microbial counts are within acceptable limits.
    *   **Container/Closure Integrity:** To ensure the packaging maintains product integrity.
3.  **Stability Testing:** Ongoing stability studies are conducted on representative batches of finished product stored under specified conditions to determine and confirm the shelf-life and storage conditions of the product. This involves testing at various time points (e.g., 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 months) for critical quality attributes.
4.  **Retention Samples:** A representative retention sample from each finished product batch is retained for a specified period (e.g., one year after the expiration date of the batch) to allow for re-testing if necessary during complaint investigations or recalls.

### 6.4. Quality Control Documentation and Release

1.  **QC Records:** All QC testing results, raw data, instrument calibration records, and analyst signatures are meticulously documented and reviewed for accuracy and completeness.
2.  **Out-of-Specification (OOS) Management:** Any OOS result triggers a formal investigation (as per Section 3.5.4.3) to determine the root cause and implement corrective actions. No product with an unresolved OOS can be released.
3.  **Batch Release:** The Quality Assurance (QA) department is responsible for the final review of the Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR), all QC testing results, and any associated deviation reports. Only after QA confirms that all specifications have been met and all documentation is complete and compliant, is the batch released for distribution. This release is formally documented and signed by authorized QA personnel.



## 7. Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. This section outlines general safety considerations and specific precautions to be observed during the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals to protect personnel, prevent accidents, and ensure a safe working environment. All personnel must adhere to company safety policies, relevant local and national regulations, and the Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) for all chemicals handled.

### 7.1. General Safety Rules

1.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Always wear appropriate PPE as required by the specific task, the BMR, or the MSDS of the materials being handled. This may include safety glasses, chemical splash goggles, face shields, gloves (e.g., nitrile, latex, chemical-resistant), lab coats, disposable gowns, respirators, and safety footwear. Ensure PPE is in good condition and properly fitted.
2.  **Emergency Procedures:** All personnel must be familiar with emergency procedures, including the location and use of emergency exits, fire extinguishers, safety showers, eyewash stations, and first-aid kits. Know how to report accidents, injuries, or chemical spills.
3.  **Chemical Handling:** Handle all chemicals with care. Read and understand the MSDS for each chemical before use. Be aware of flammability, corrosivity, toxicity, and reactivity hazards. Use chemicals only in designated areas (e.g., fume hoods, biosafety cabinets) with adequate ventilation.
4.  **Spill Management:** Know the procedures for containing and cleaning up chemical or biological spills. Report all spills immediately to a supervisor. Ensure appropriate spill kits are readily available.
5.  **Equipment Safety:** Never operate equipment without proper training and authorization. Ensure all safety guards are in place and functional. Follow lockout/tagout procedures before performing maintenance or cleaning on equipment.
6.  **Housekeeping:** Maintain a clean and organized work area to prevent slips, trips, and falls. Keep aisles clear and dispose of waste properly in designated containers.
7.  **Reporting Hazards:** Immediately report any unsafe conditions, near misses, or potential hazards to your supervisor.

### 7.2. Specific Safety Precautions by Area/Activity

**7.2.1. Raw Material Handling and Dispensing:**

*   **Dust Control:** When handling powdered raw materials, especially APIs, use local exhaust ventilation (LEV) or perform operations within containment booths/isolators to minimize dust generation and inhalation exposure. Wear appropriate respiratory protection.
*   **Heavy Lifting:** Use proper lifting techniques or mechanical aids (e.g., pallet jacks, drum lifters) when handling heavy containers of raw materials to prevent musculoskeletal injuries.
*   **Flammable Solvents:** When dispensing or handling flammable solvents, ensure the area is well-ventilated and free from ignition sources (e.g., open flames, sparks, hot surfaces). Use intrinsically safe equipment where required.

**7.2.2. Mixing and Blending Operations:**

*   **Rotating Equipment:** Be aware of pinch points and rotating parts on mixers and blenders. Never attempt to clean, adjust, or reach into operating machinery. Ensure equipment is de-energized and locked out before any intervention.
*   **Pressure Vessels:** If using pressure vessels, ensure they are properly rated, inspected, and operated within their design limits. Follow safe operating procedures for pressurization and depressurization.
*   **Temperature Control:** Exercise caution when handling hot materials or operating equipment at elevated temperatures. Use insulated gloves and protective clothing as needed.

**7.2.3. Filling and Packaging Operations:**

*   **Sharps:** Exercise extreme caution when handling glass vials, ampoules, or other sharp packaging components to prevent cuts. Dispose of broken glass in designated sharps containers.
*   **Automated Machinery:** Be aware of moving parts on automated filling, capping, and labeling machines. Ensure all safety interlocks are functional and never bypass them.
*   **Repetitive Motion:** For manual packaging tasks, take regular breaks and use ergonomic practices to prevent repetitive strain injuries.

**7.2.4. Cleaning and Maintenance:**

*   **Lockout/Tagout:** Always follow strict lockout/tagout procedures before cleaning, maintaining, or repairing any equipment to prevent unexpected startup.
*   **Chemical Exposure:** When using cleaning agents and sanitizers, wear appropriate chemical-resistant PPE. Ensure adequate ventilation and follow dilution instructions carefully.
*   **Confined Spaces:** If any equipment or area is classified as a confined space, follow confined space entry procedures, including permit-to-work systems, atmospheric monitoring, and standby personnel.

### 7.3. Emergency Response

1.  **First Aid:** For minor injuries, administer first aid and report the incident. For serious injuries, seek immediate medical attention and activate emergency services.
2.  **Fire:** In case of fire, activate the fire alarm, evacuate the area, and use the appropriate fire extinguisher if safe to do so. Do not attempt to fight large or rapidly spreading fires.
3.  **Chemical Spills:** For small, contained spills, use appropriate spill kits and PPE to clean up. For large or hazardous spills, evacuate the area, notify emergency response teams, and follow their instructions.
4.  **Evacuation:** In the event of an emergency requiring evacuation, follow designated evacuation routes to the assembly points. Do not re-enter the building until authorized by emergency personnel.

All personnel are responsible for their own safety and the safety of their colleagues. Adherence to these safety considerations is mandatory.



## 8. Regulatory Compliance References

Compliance with applicable regulatory requirements is non-negotiable in the pharmaceutical industry, particularly for products intended for large animals. This section outlines the primary regulatory bodies and guidelines that govern the batch manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals, ensuring that all processes and products meet the highest standards of quality, safety, and efficacy. Adherence to these regulations is critical for market access, product approval, and maintaining public trust.

### 8.1. U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

The FDA is the primary regulatory authority for veterinary drugs in the United States. The manufacturing of large animal pharmaceuticals falls under the purview of the Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM), which ensures that animal drugs are safe and effective and that animal feeds are safe, effective, and properly manufactured and labeled. Key FDA regulations and guidance documents include:

*   **21 CFR Part 210 – Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Processing, Packing, or Holding of Drugs; General:** This regulation provides general requirements for the methods, facilities, and controls used in the manufacturing, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product to ensure that it meets the requirements of the Act as to safety, and has the identity and strength and meets the quality and purity characteristics that it purports or is represented to possess.
*   **21 CFR Part 211 – Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation sets forth minimum current good manufacturing practice for the preparation of drug products for administration to animals or humans. It covers various aspects including organization and personnel, buildings and facilities, equipment, control of components and drug product containers and closures, production and process controls, packaging and labeling control, holding and distribution, laboratory controls, and records and reports.
*   **Guidance for Industry:** The FDA publishes numerous guidance documents that provide recommendations on how to comply with regulations. Relevant guidances for veterinary pharmaceuticals may include those on manufacturing process validation, stability testing, analytical procedures, and quality systems.

### 8.2. U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)

The USDA plays a significant role in regulating certain biological products for animals, particularly vaccines, diagnostics, and antitoxins. While the FDA primarily regulates animal drugs, the USDA’s Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) is responsible for ensuring that veterinary biologics are pure, safe, potent, and effective. If the large animal pharmaceutical product contains biological components or is classified as a biologic, USDA regulations may apply. Key USDA regulations include:

*   **9 CFR Part 101 – Definitions:** Provides definitions for terms used in the regulations governing veterinary biologics.
*   **9 CFR Part 102 – Licenses for Biological Products:** Outlines the requirements for obtaining licenses to manufacture veterinary biological products.
*   **9 CFR Part 103 – Experimental Products:** Covers regulations for experimental biological products.
*   **9 CFR Part 104 – Permits for Biological Products:** Details requirements for permits for biological products.
*   **9 CFR Part 105 – Suspension, Revocation, or Termination of Licenses and Permits:** Procedures for regulatory actions against licenses and permits.
*   **9 CFR Part 106 – Exemptions from Preparation Pursuant to a License:** Specifies conditions under which certain products may be exempt from licensing requirements.
*   **9 CFR Part 107 – Inspections:** Describes the inspection process for facilities manufacturing veterinary biologics.
*   **9 CFR Part 108 – Sterilization and Disinfection:** Requirements for sterilization and disinfection in manufacturing facilities.
*   **9 CFR Part 112 – Packaging and Labeling:** Regulations concerning the packaging and labeling of veterinary biological products.
*   **9 CFR Part 113 – Standard Requirements for Veterinary Biologics:** Contains specific requirements for various types of veterinary biologics, including purity, safety, potency, and efficacy tests.

### 8.3. Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP)

cGMP refers to the Current Good Manufacturing Practice regulations enforced by the FDA. cGMP regulations assure the identity, strength, quality, and purity of drug products by requiring manufacturers to adequately control manufacturing operations. This includes establishing strong quality management systems, obtaining appropriate quality raw materials, establishing robust operating procedures, detecting and investigating product quality deviations, and maintaining reliable testing laboratories. The 


cGMP regulations for human drugs (21 CFR Parts 210 and 211) are generally applied to veterinary drugs as well, with specific considerations for animal products. Adherence to cGMP principles is fundamental to all aspects of this SOP.

### 8.4. Other Relevant Regulations and Guidelines

*   **State and Local Regulations:** Manufacturers must also comply with any applicable state and local regulations related to pharmaceutical manufacturing, environmental protection, and occupational safety.
*   **International Guidelines:** For products intended for international markets, compliance with guidelines from organizations such as the International Cooperation on Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Registration of Veterinary Medicinal Products (VICH) or the European Medicines Agency (EMA) may be required.

All personnel involved in the manufacturing process must be aware of and adhere to these regulatory requirements to ensure the production of safe, effective, and compliant large animal pharmaceuticals.

## 9. Change Control Procedures

Any planned change that could potentially impact the quality, safety, efficacy, or regulatory compliance of the large animal pharmaceutical product, its manufacturing process, equipment, facilities, or documentation must be managed through a formal Change Control system. The purpose of this system is to ensure that all changes are properly documented, reviewed, approved, implemented, and assessed for their impact before they are put into effect.

### 9.1. Principles of Change Control

1.  **Proactive Management:** Changes must be initiated and approved *before* they are implemented. Unauthorized changes are strictly prohibited.
2.  **Documentation:** All proposed changes, their rationale, impact assessments, and approval decisions must be thoroughly documented.
3.  **Cross-Functional Review:** Changes must be reviewed by all relevant departments (e.g., Production, Quality Assurance, Quality Control, Engineering, Regulatory Affairs) to assess their potential impact on product quality, process performance, and regulatory compliance.
4.  **Risk Assessment:** A risk assessment must be conducted for each proposed change to identify potential risks and determine the level of review and validation required.
5.  **Approval:** All changes must be formally approved by authorized personnel, typically from Quality Assurance, before implementation.
6.  **Implementation and Verification:** Once approved, the change must be implemented as planned, and its effectiveness verified through appropriate testing, validation, or monitoring.
7.  **Training:** Personnel affected by the change must be trained on the revised procedures or processes before the change is implemented.

### 9.2. Types of Changes Subject to Control

Changes requiring formal change control include, but are not limited to:

*   **Raw Materials:** Changes in supplier, grade, specification, or manufacturing process of raw materials.
*   **Packaging Materials:** Changes in primary or secondary packaging components, including labels.
*   **Manufacturing Process:** Changes in batch size, equipment, process parameters (e.g., mixing speed, temperature, duration), order of addition, or manufacturing site.
*   **Equipment:** Introduction of new equipment, significant modifications to existing equipment, or changes in equipment calibration/maintenance procedures.
*   **Facilities/Utilities:** Modifications to manufacturing areas, cleanrooms, HVAC systems, or purified water systems.
*   **Analytical Methods:** Changes to QC testing methods or specifications.
*   **Documentation:** Revisions to Master Batch Records (MBRs), Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), specifications, or test methods.
*   **Personnel:** Changes in key personnel roles or responsibilities that impact product quality.

### 9.3. Change Control Procedure

1.  **Initiation of Change Request:** Any employee identifying a need for a change initiates a Change Request form. The form must clearly describe the proposed change, its rationale, and the perceived benefits.
2.  **Initial Assessment:** The Change Control Coordinator (typically from QA) performs an initial assessment to determine the scope and potential impact of the change and assigns a criticality level (e.g., minor, major, critical).
3.  **Cross-Functional Review and Impact Assessment:** The Change Request is circulated to relevant departmental representatives for their review and assessment of the potential impact on their respective areas. This includes evaluating the need for re-validation, re-qualification, stability studies, or regulatory filings.
4.  **Risk Assessment:** A formal risk assessment is conducted to identify potential risks associated with the change and to determine appropriate mitigation strategies.
5.  **Approval:** Based on the impact and risk assessments, the Change Control Board (comprising representatives from QA, Production, QC, Engineering, and Regulatory Affairs) reviews the proposed change. All members must approve the change before it can proceed. QA provides the final approval.
6.  **Implementation Plan:** A detailed implementation plan is developed, outlining the steps required to implement the change, including timelines, responsibilities, and any necessary validation or qualification activities.
7.  **Training:** All personnel affected by the change are trained on the revised procedures or processes before the change is implemented.
8.  **Implementation:** The change is implemented according to the approved plan.
9.  **Effectiveness Check:** After implementation, an effectiveness check is performed to confirm that the change has achieved its intended outcome and has not introduced any unforeseen negative impacts. This may involve monitoring process performance, product quality data, or conducting post-implementation reviews.
10. **Closure:** Once the effectiveness of the change has been verified, the Change Request is formally closed by QA.

### 9.4. Emergency Changes

In rare circumstances where an immediate change is required to prevent a significant risk to product quality, safety, or regulatory compliance (e.g., equipment breakdown), an emergency change may be initiated. Emergency changes must still be documented, reviewed, and approved as quickly as possible, with a full impact assessment and formal approval completed retrospectively within a defined timeframe.
