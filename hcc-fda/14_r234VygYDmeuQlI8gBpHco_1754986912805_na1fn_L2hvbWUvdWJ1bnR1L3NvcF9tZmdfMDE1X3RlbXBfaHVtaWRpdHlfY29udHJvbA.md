### 1.1 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the guidelines and procedures for the effective control, monitoring, and maintenance of temperature and humidity within all critical areas involved in large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operations. This includes, but is not limited to, raw material storage, in-process material storage, finished product storage, and manufacturing environments. The primary purpose is to ensure product quality, stability, and efficacy by preventing degradation due to adverse environmental conditions, thereby complying with national and international regulatory requirements, including those set forth by the FDA, USDA, and cGMP guidelines.

### 1.2 Scope

This SOP applies to all personnel, equipment, and facilities involved in the handling, storage, and manufacturing of large animal pharmaceutical products where temperature and/or humidity control is critical. This encompasses all stages from the receipt of raw materials to the dispatch of finished products. Specifically, it covers the selection, installation, operation, calibration, maintenance, and documentation of all temperature and humidity monitoring and control systems. This SOP is designed to ensure that environmental conditions are consistently maintained within predefined acceptable ranges to safeguard product integrity and ensure compliance with regulatory standards for pharmaceutical manufacturing.




## 2. Responsibilities

### 2.1 Quality Assurance (QA) Department

*   **Overall Oversight:** Responsible for the overall oversight and approval of this SOP, ensuring its compliance with cGMP regulations and other applicable guidelines.
*   **Auditing:** Conducts regular audits to ensure adherence to the procedures outlined in this SOP.
*   **Deviation Management:** Reviews and approves deviations related to temperature and humidity control, ensuring appropriate corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs) are implemented.
*   **Change Control:** Approves all changes to temperature and humidity control systems and related procedures through the change control system.

### 2.2 Manufacturing Department

*   **Daily Monitoring:** Responsible for daily monitoring of temperature and humidity in manufacturing areas and ensuring that environmental conditions are maintained within specified limits.
*   **Reporting Deviations:** Immediately reports any deviations or alarms related to temperature and humidity to the QA department and facilities management.
*   **Adherence to Procedures:** Ensures that all personnel follow the procedures outlined in this SOP during manufacturing operations.

### 2.3 Facilities Management/Engineering Department

*   **System Maintenance:** Responsible for the installation, calibration, preventive maintenance, and repair of all temperature and humidity control and monitoring systems.
*   **Alarm Response:** Responds promptly to temperature and humidity alarms and initiates corrective actions.
*   **System Qualification:** Conducts and documents the Installation Qualification (IQ), Operational Qualification (OQ), and Performance Qualification (PQ) of all relevant systems.
*   **Documentation:** Maintains accurate records of all maintenance, calibration, and qualification activities.

### 2.4 Personnel

*   **Adherence to SOP:** All personnel working in areas where temperature and humidity are controlled are responsible for understanding and adhering to the requirements of this SOP.
*   **Reporting Issues:** Reports any observed issues or malfunctions with temperature and humidity control systems to their immediate supervisor and the Facilities Management department.
*   **Training:** Participates in all required training sessions related to this SOP and environmental control systems.




## 3. Detailed Procedures

### 3.1 System Selection and Installation

#### 3.1.1 User Requirements Specification (URS)

Before selecting any temperature and humidity control and monitoring system, a comprehensive User Requirements Specification (URS) must be developed. This document will outline the specific needs of the large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing operation, including:

*   **Environmental Parameters:** Define the precise temperature and humidity ranges required for each controlled area (e.g., raw material storage, manufacturing suites, finished product warehouses). These ranges must be based on product stability data and regulatory requirements.
*   **Monitoring Frequency:** Specify the frequency of data collection (e.g., continuous monitoring, hourly, daily).
*   **Alarm Thresholds:** Establish high and low alarm set points for both temperature and humidity, and define the required response times for alarms.
*   **Data Storage and Retrieval:** Detail requirements for data logging, storage duration, data integrity, and ease of retrieval for audit purposes. This should include considerations for electronic records and electronic signatures (21 CFR Part 11 compliance).
*   **System Type:** Determine whether a hosted system (on-premise) or a Software as a Service (SaaS) solution is most appropriate, considering scalability, maintenance, and IT infrastructure.
*   **Reporting Capabilities:** Specify the types of reports required, including deviation reports, trend analysis, and audit trails.
*   **Security and Access Control:** Outline requirements for system security, user access levels, and data protection.
*   **Integration:** Consider integration with existing building management systems (BMS) or enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems.

The URS must be developed collaboratively by relevant departments, including Manufacturing, Quality Assurance, Facilities Management, and IT, and formally approved.

#### 3.1.2 System Selection

The selection of temperature and humidity control and monitoring systems must be based on the approved URS. Key considerations during selection include:

*   **Accuracy and Precision:** Sensors must be accurate to at least ±0.5 °C for temperature and ±5% RH for humidity, or better, as required by product specifications and regulatory guidelines.
*   **Reliability and Robustness:** Systems should be robust enough to withstand the manufacturing environment and provide continuous, reliable data.
*   **Scalability:** The system should be capable of expansion to accommodate future growth or changes in manufacturing operations.
*   **Validation Support:** The vendor should provide comprehensive documentation and support for system validation (IQ, OQ, PQ).
*   **Alarm Capabilities:** The system must include visual and audible alarms, with capabilities for automatic notification to key personnel via telephone dial-up or SMS.
*   **Data Integrity:** The system must ensure data integrity, preventing unauthorized alteration or loss of data.

#### 3.1.3 Installation

Installation of temperature and humidity control and monitoring systems must be performed by qualified personnel according to manufacturer's instructions and an approved installation plan. Key aspects of installation include:

*   **Sensor Placement:** Sensors must be strategically placed in areas identified as 


worst-case locations (e.g., hot and cold spots identified during temperature mapping) and where the greatest variability in temperature and humidity is expected. Sensors should be positioned to be minimally affected by transient events such as door openings. For large animal facilities, this includes consideration of animal activity, ventilation patterns, and potential heat sources.
*   **Wiring and Connectivity:** All wiring and network connections must be installed according to electrical codes and industry best practices, ensuring stable and secure data transmission.
*   **Power Supply:** Ensure reliable power supply with backup options (e.g., UPS) for continuous operation during power outages.
*   **Environmental Considerations:** Protect sensors and equipment from physical damage, dust, and moisture, especially in animal housing areas where cleaning and animal activity can impact equipment.




### 3.2 Routine Monitoring and Control

#### 3.2.1 Continuous Monitoring

All critical temperature and humidity controlled areas must be continuously monitored using validated electronic systems. Data from sensors should be recorded at predefined intervals (e.g., every 5-15 minutes) and stored in a secure, unalterable format. The monitoring system should provide real-time data visualization to allow for immediate assessment of environmental conditions.

#### 3.2.2 Manual Checks

In addition to continuous electronic monitoring, daily manual checks of temperature and humidity readings should be performed by designated personnel. These manual checks serve as a verification of the electronic system and provide an opportunity to visually inspect the monitoring equipment for any signs of malfunction or damage. Any discrepancies between manual and electronic readings must be immediately investigated and documented.

#### 3.2.3 Data Review and Analysis

Recorded temperature and humidity data must be regularly reviewed by authorized personnel (e.g., Manufacturing Supervisors, QA personnel). This review should include:

*   **Trend Analysis:** Identify any trends or patterns that may indicate a potential issue with the control system or environmental conditions. This can help in proactive maintenance and prevention of excursions.
*   **Deviation Identification:** Promptly identify any readings that fall outside the predefined acceptable ranges.
*   **Audit Trail Review:** Verify the integrity of the data and ensure that all actions related to the monitoring system (e.g., alarm acknowledgements, system adjustments) are properly logged with an audit trail.

#### 3.2.4 Environmental Control Adjustments

Adjustments to temperature and humidity control systems (e.g., HVAC systems, dehumidifiers) should only be made by authorized and trained Facilities Management personnel. All adjustments must be documented, including the reason for the adjustment, the parameters changed, and the impact on environmental conditions. Any significant adjustments should be subject to the change control procedure.




### 3.3 Alarm Management and Excursion Handling

#### 3.3.1 Alarm Activation and Notification

The temperature and humidity monitoring system must be configured to trigger alarms when environmental conditions deviate from the predefined acceptable ranges. Alarms should be:

*   **Visual:** Clearly visible indicators (e.g., flashing lights) in the affected area and at a central monitoring station.
*   **Audible:** Distinct audible alerts to draw immediate attention.
*   **Automated Notifications:** Configured to automatically notify designated personnel (e.g., Facilities Management, Manufacturing Supervisor, QA) via SMS, email, or automated dialer system. A clear escalation matrix must be established for alarm notifications.

#### 3.3.2 Alarm Response Procedure

Upon receiving an alarm notification, designated personnel must respond immediately according to the following steps:

1.  **Acknowledge Alarm:** Acknowledge the alarm within [Specify Time, e.g., 5 minutes] to indicate that it has been received and is being addressed. This acknowledgment must be recorded by the monitoring system.
2.  **Assess Situation:** Immediately proceed to the alarmed area to assess the situation. Verify the actual temperature and humidity readings using a calibrated reference device, if available. Identify potential causes for the deviation (e.g., open doors, equipment malfunction, HVAC issues).
3.  **Initiate Corrective Action:** Take immediate corrective actions to bring the environmental conditions back within the acceptable range. Examples of corrective actions include:
    *   Closing doors or windows.
    *   Adjusting HVAC settings (only by authorized personnel).
    *   Initiating emergency repairs for malfunctioning equipment.
    *   Relocating sensitive products to an alternative, controlled environment if the deviation is prolonged or severe.
4.  **Document Excursion:** If the environmental conditions remain outside the acceptable range for more than [Specify Time, e.g., 15 minutes], it is considered a temperature/humidity excursion. All excursions must be immediately documented in a Deviation Report (refer to Section 4.1).
5.  **Notify QA:** Immediately notify the Quality Assurance (QA) department of any confirmed excursions. QA will initiate a formal investigation.

#### 3.3.3 Excursion Investigation

All temperature and humidity excursions must be thoroughly investigated by QA in collaboration with Manufacturing and Facilities Management. The investigation should aim to:

*   **Determine Root Cause:** Identify the underlying reason for the excursion.
*   **Assess Product Impact:** Evaluate the potential impact of the excursion on the quality, safety, and efficacy of affected pharmaceutical products. This may involve stability testing or other quality assessments.
*   **Implement CAPA:** Develop and implement appropriate Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPAs) to prevent recurrence of similar excursions. This could include equipment upgrades, procedural changes, or additional training.
*   **Document Findings:** All investigation findings, product impact assessments, and CAPAs must be thoroughly documented in the Deviation Report.




### 3.4 Maintenance and Calibration

#### 3.4.1 Preventive Maintenance

All temperature and humidity control and monitoring systems must undergo regular preventive maintenance to ensure their optimal performance and longevity. A preventive maintenance schedule must be established and adhered to, including:

*   **Routine Inspections:** Daily/weekly visual inspections of sensors, wiring, and control units for any signs of damage, wear, or malfunction.
*   **Cleaning:** Regular cleaning of sensors and equipment to prevent dust accumulation and ensure accurate readings.
*   **Filter Replacement:** Periodic replacement of air filters in HVAC systems to maintain proper airflow and temperature/humidity control.
*   **System Checks:** Functional checks of alarm systems, data logging, and notification features.

All preventive maintenance activities must be documented, including the date, personnel performing the maintenance, and any observations or actions taken.

#### 3.4.2 Calibration

All temperature and humidity sensors and monitoring devices must be calibrated at a defined frequency (e.g., annually, semi-annually) using traceable standards. Calibration procedures must ensure that:

*   **Traceability:** Calibration standards are traceable to national or international standards.
*   **Accuracy:** Devices are calibrated to meet the required accuracy specifications (e.g., ±0.5 °C for temperature, ±5% RH for humidity).
*   **Documentation:** All calibration activities are thoroughly documented, including:
    *   Date of calibration.
    *   Identification of the device calibrated.
    *   Calibration standards used.
    *   "As found" and "as left" readings.
    *   Signature of the calibrating technician.
    *   Next calibration due date.

Devices that fail calibration must be immediately removed from service, tagged as "Out of Calibration," and repaired or replaced. Any products stored or manufactured under conditions monitored by an out-of-calibration device must be assessed for potential impact on quality.




## 4. Documentation Requirements

Accurate and complete documentation is critical for demonstrating compliance with regulatory requirements and ensuring the integrity of temperature and humidity controlled environments. All documentation must be maintained in a legible, retrievable, and secure manner.

### 4.1 Deviation Reports

Any deviation from the established temperature and humidity limits, or any malfunction of the control and monitoring systems, must be documented in a formal Deviation Report. The report must include:

*   **Deviation Details:** Date, time, location, and duration of the deviation.
*   **Observed Conditions:** Actual temperature and humidity readings during the deviation.
*   **Product Impact Assessment:** Evaluation of the potential impact on affected products, including lot numbers and quantities. This assessment should determine if the product quality, safety, or efficacy has been compromised.
*   **Root Cause Investigation:** Detailed investigation into the cause of the deviation.
*   **Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPAs):** Specific actions taken to correct the immediate issue and prevent recurrence.
*   **Approval:** Signatures of relevant personnel (e.g., Manufacturing Supervisor, QA Manager, Facilities Manager) indicating review and approval.

### 4.2 Monitoring Records

All temperature and humidity monitoring data, whether electronic or manual, must be recorded and maintained. Electronic records must comply with 21 CFR Part 11 requirements for electronic signatures and audit trails. Records should include:

*   **Continuous Data Logs:** Raw data from electronic monitoring systems, including time-stamped readings.
*   **Manual Log Sheets:** Completed manual log sheets for daily checks.
*   **Alarm Logs:** Records of all alarms, including activation time, acknowledgment time, and response actions.
*   **Trend Reports:** Periodic reports summarizing temperature and humidity trends over time.

### 4.3 Maintenance and Calibration Records

Comprehensive records of all maintenance and calibration activities for temperature and humidity control and monitoring equipment must be maintained. These records should include:

*   **Preventive Maintenance Logs:** Documentation of routine inspections, cleaning, and system checks.
*   **Calibration Certificates:** Certificates from external calibration services or internal calibration records, including traceability information, "as found" and "as left" data, and next calibration due dates.
*   **Repair Records:** Documentation of any repairs performed on the equipment, including parts replaced and troubleshooting steps.

### 4.4 Training Records

All personnel involved in activities covered by this SOP must have their training documented. (Refer to Section 5.0 for more details).

### 4.5 Change Control Documentation

All changes to temperature and humidity control systems, monitoring systems, or related procedures must be documented through the change control system. (Refer to Section 9.0 for more details).




## 5. Training Requirements

All personnel whose job functions impact or are impacted by temperature and humidity control systems must receive appropriate training on this SOP and related procedures. Training must be documented and refreshed periodically.

### 5.1 Initial Training

Prior to assuming responsibilities related to temperature and humidity control, all new employees and personnel newly assigned to relevant roles must complete initial training. This training must cover:

*   **Understanding of this SOP:** Comprehensive review of all sections of this SOP, including purpose, scope, responsibilities, and detailed procedures.
*   **Importance of Environmental Control:** Education on the critical role of temperature and humidity control in maintaining product quality, safety, and efficacy, particularly for large animal pharmaceutical products.
*   **System Operation:** Hands-on training on the operation of the specific temperature and humidity monitoring and control systems used in the facility, including how to interpret readings, identify alarms, and perform manual checks.
*   **Alarm Response:** Detailed training on the alarm escalation procedure and immediate corrective actions to be taken during an excursion.
*   **Documentation Practices:** Training on proper documentation practices, including completion of log sheets, deviation reports, and maintenance records.
*   **Regulatory Requirements:** Overview of relevant regulatory requirements (FDA, USDA, cGMP) pertaining to environmental control in pharmaceutical manufacturing.

### 5.2 Refresher Training

Refresher training must be conducted at least annually, or more frequently if there are significant changes to this SOP, the control systems, or regulatory requirements. Refresher training should reinforce key concepts and address any common issues or deviations identified during routine operations or audits.

### 5.3 Training Records

All training activities must be documented and maintained by the Human Resources or Training Department. Training records must include:

*   **Employee Name and ID.**
*   **Date of Training.**
*   **Training Topic/Module.**
*   **Trainer's Name.**
*   **Assessment of Understanding:** (e.g., quiz results, practical demonstration).
*   **Employee Signature:** Confirmation of attendance and understanding.




## 6. Quality Control Measures

Effective quality control measures are essential to ensure the continuous and reliable operation of temperature and humidity control systems and to maintain product quality. These measures include regular system validation, performance monitoring, and adherence to established quality standards.

### 6.1 System Validation (IQ, OQ, PQ)

All temperature and humidity control and monitoring systems, including HVAC systems, sensors, data loggers, and alarm systems, must be formally validated prior to their initial use and after any significant changes. The validation process includes:

*   **Installation Qualification (IQ):** Verifies that the system is installed correctly according to design specifications and manufacturer's recommendations. This includes checking documentation, utilities, and physical installation.
*   **Operational Qualification (OQ):** Confirms that the system operates as intended across its anticipated operating ranges. This involves testing alarm functions, data accuracy, and system response under various conditions.
*   **Performance Qualification (PQ):** Demonstrates that the system consistently performs according to its intended use under actual operating conditions. This includes long-term monitoring to confirm stability and reliability.

All validation activities must be documented, reviewed, and approved by the Quality Assurance department. Revalidation may be required after major repairs, upgrades, or changes to the system.

### 6.2 Performance Monitoring

Ongoing performance monitoring of the temperature and humidity control systems is crucial. This includes:

*   **Regular Data Review:** As described in Section 3.2.3, regular review of temperature and humidity data for trends and deviations.
*   **Alarm System Testing:** Periodic testing of alarm systems to ensure they are functional and notifications are sent to the correct personnel.
*   **Sensor Verification:** Routine verification of sensor accuracy using a calibrated reference device, especially for critical monitoring points.

### 6.3 Quality Audits

The Quality Assurance department will conduct periodic internal audits to assess compliance with this SOP and the effectiveness of the temperature and humidity control program. External audits by regulatory bodies (e.g., FDA, USDA) or third-party auditors may also occur. Findings from all audits must be documented, and any deficiencies must be addressed through the CAPA system.




## 7. Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in all pharmaceutical manufacturing operations, including those related to temperature and humidity control systems. All personnel must be aware of potential hazards and follow established safety protocols.

### 7.1 Electrical Safety

*   **Qualified Personnel:** Only qualified and authorized personnel (e.g., electricians, facilities technicians) should perform installation, maintenance, or repair work on electrical components of temperature and humidity control systems.
*   **Lockout/Tagout:** Implement and adhere to strict lockout/tagout procedures before performing any maintenance or repair on electrical equipment to prevent accidental startup.
*   **Grounding:** Ensure all electrical equipment is properly grounded to prevent electrical shock.
*   **Inspections:** Regularly inspect electrical wiring, conduits, and connections for damage or wear. Report any issues immediately.

### 7.2 Chemical Safety

*   **Refrigerants:** If refrigeration systems are used, personnel must be trained on the safe handling of refrigerants, including proper storage, leak detection, and emergency procedures in case of a leak.
*   **Cleaning Agents:** Use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) when handling cleaning agents for sensors or equipment. Follow manufacturer's safety data sheet (SDS) recommendations.

### 7.3 Confined Spaces

*   **Permit-Required Confined Spaces:** If any part of the temperature and humidity control system is located in a confined space (e.g., HVAC ducts, equipment enclosures), follow all permit-required confined space entry procedures. This includes atmospheric monitoring, ventilation, and having trained attendants.

### 7.4 Emergency Procedures

*   **Fire Safety:** Ensure that fire suppression systems are not compromised by the installation or operation of environmental control equipment. Know the location of fire extinguishers and emergency exits.
*   **Emergency Contacts:** Maintain a readily accessible list of emergency contacts for system malfunctions, including Facilities Management, IT support, and relevant emergency services.
*   **First Aid:** Ensure that first aid kits are available and personnel are trained in basic first aid for electrical shocks or chemical exposures.

### 7.5 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

*   **Appropriate PPE:** Personnel performing maintenance or troubleshooting on temperature and humidity control systems must wear appropriate PPE, such as safety glasses, gloves, and hearing protection, as required by the task and risk assessment.




## 8. Regulatory Compliance References

This Standard Operating Procedure is developed in accordance with, and references, the following key regulatory guidelines and standards applicable to pharmaceutical manufacturing, particularly for large animal products:

### 8.1 U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

*   **21 CFR Part 211 – Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals:** This regulation outlines the minimum current good manufacturing practice for the preparation of drug products for administration to humans or animals. Specific sections relevant to temperature and humidity control include:
    *   **Subpart C – Buildings and Facilities:** Requires adequate control over air pressure, micro-organisms, dust, humidity, and temperature when appropriate for the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product.
    *   **Subpart D – Equipment:** Addresses the design, construction, and placement of equipment to facilitate operations and cleaning, and to prevent contamination.
    *   **Subpart I – Laboratory Controls:** Requires appropriate laboratory controls, including the establishment of specifications, standards, sampling plans, and test procedures.
*   **Guidance for Industry: Q7A Good Manufacturing Practice Guidance for Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients:** While primarily for APIs, many principles apply to finished products, emphasizing environmental controls.
*   **FDA Warning Letters:** Review of FDA Warning Letters often highlights common deficiencies related to inadequate temperature and humidity control and monitoring, underscoring the importance of robust systems and documentation.

### 8.2 U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)

*   **Animal Welfare Act and Regulations (9 CFR Parts 1, 2, 3):** While not directly focused on pharmaceutical manufacturing, these regulations govern the humane handling, care, treatment, and transportation of animals used in research, exhibition, or for other purposes. For large animal pharmaceutical manufacturing, this is relevant where animals are housed or involved in the production process, requiring appropriate environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, ventilation) in animal housing facilities.
*   **FSIS Cooking Guideline for Meat and Poultry Products:** Although specific to food products, it provides insights into humidity considerations in controlled environments, which can be analogous to certain aspects of pharmaceutical storage and processing where moisture control is critical.

### 8.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP)

*   **General cGMP Principles:** Encompasses a broad set of principles and practices that ensure products are consistently produced and controlled according to quality standards. This includes maintaining appropriate environmental conditions to prevent contamination, degradation, and to ensure product identity, strength, quality, and purity.
*   **WHO Good Distribution Practices (GDP) for Pharmaceutical Products:** Provides guidance on the proper distribution of medicinal products, including requirements for storage conditions and temperature control throughout the supply chain. (Referenced in the initial research, e.g., WHO Technical Report Series No. 961, Annex 9).

### 8.4 International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) Guidelines

*   **ICH Q1A(R2) Stability Testing of New Drug Substances and Products:** Provides guidance on stability studies, which define the appropriate storage conditions (including temperature and humidity) for drug substances and products. This directly informs the setting of acceptable ranges for environmental controls.
*   **ICH Q10 Pharmaceutical Quality System:** Describes a model for an effective quality management system for the pharmaceutical industry, emphasizing process performance and product quality monitoring, which includes environmental controls.




## 9. Change Control Procedures

Any proposed changes to the temperature and humidity control systems, monitoring systems, or the procedures outlined in this SOP must be managed through a formal change control system. This ensures that all changes are evaluated for their potential impact on product quality, system performance, and regulatory compliance.

### 9.1 Initiating a Change

Any department or individual identifying a need for a change must initiate a Change Control Request (CCR) form. The CCR must include:

*   **Description of Change:** A clear and concise description of the proposed change.
*   **Reason for Change:** Justification for the proposed change (e.g., system upgrade, regulatory requirement, process improvement).
*   **Proposed Implementation Plan:** A high-level plan for implementing the change.
*   **Impact Assessment:** Initial assessment of the potential impact on product quality, safety, efficacy, and regulatory compliance.

### 9.2 Review and Approval of Change

All CCRs must be reviewed and approved by a cross-functional team, including representatives from Quality Assurance, Manufacturing, Facilities Management, and IT. The review process will assess:

*   **Necessity and Justification:** Is the change necessary and adequately justified?
*   **Risk Assessment:** A thorough risk assessment to identify potential risks associated with the change and develop mitigation strategies.
*   **Validation Requirements:** Determine if re-qualification (IQ, OQ, PQ) or re-validation of the system is required after the change.
*   **Training Requirements:** Identify any new training required for personnel due to the change.
*   **Documentation Updates:** Ensure that all relevant documents (e.g., SOPs, schematics, calibration procedures) will be updated.

No change shall be implemented until it has received formal approval from all designated stakeholders, particularly Quality Assurance.

### 9.3 Implementation of Change

Once approved, the change must be implemented according to the approved plan. All implementation activities must be documented, including:

*   **Date of Implementation.**
*   **Personnel Involved.**
*   **Verification of Implementation:** Confirmation that the change was implemented as planned.
*   **Post-Implementation Review:** A review conducted after implementation to confirm that the change achieved its intended purpose and did not introduce any unforeseen negative impacts.

### 9.4 Documentation of Change

All change control documentation, including the CCR, review comments, approval signatures, implementation records, and post-implementation review, must be maintained as part of the quality management system.
