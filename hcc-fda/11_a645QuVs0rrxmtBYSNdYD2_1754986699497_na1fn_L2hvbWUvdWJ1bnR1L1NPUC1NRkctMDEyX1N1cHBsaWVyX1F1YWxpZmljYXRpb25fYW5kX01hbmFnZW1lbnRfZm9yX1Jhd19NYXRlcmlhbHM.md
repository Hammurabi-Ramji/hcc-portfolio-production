## 1.0 Purpose
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the guidelines and procedures for the qualification, approval, and ongoing management of suppliers of raw materials used in the manufacturing of large animal pharmaceutical products. The purpose is to ensure that all raw materials consistently meet established quality, purity, and safety specifications, thereby safeguarding product quality, efficacy, and patient safety. This SOP aims to minimize risks associated with raw material supply, ensure compliance with regulatory requirements, and maintain a robust quality management system.

## 2.0 Scope
This SOP applies to all raw materials, active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), excipients, and packaging components that directly contact the product, used in the manufacturing of large animal pharmaceutical products at [Company Name/Site Name]. It covers the entire lifecycle of supplier and raw material management, from initial supplier selection and qualification to ongoing performance monitoring, re-qualification, and change control. This includes, but is not limited to, the assessment of supplier quality systems, auditing processes, material specifications, incoming material testing, and the management of the Approved Supplier List (ASL).

## 3.0 Responsibilities

### 3.1 Quality Assurance (QA)
- Oversees the overall implementation and adherence to this SOP.
- Develops and maintains the Approved Supplier List (ASL).
- Conducts or oversees supplier audits and assessments.
- Reviews and approves supplier qualification documentation.
- Manages supplier non-conformances and corrective actions.
- Ensures compliance with relevant regulatory requirements (FDA, USDA, cGMP).
- Provides training on this SOP to relevant personnel.

### 3.2 Procurement/Purchasing
- Identifies potential raw material suppliers.
- Initiates the supplier qualification process.
- Communicates material specifications and quality requirements to suppliers.
- Manages supplier relationships and contracts.
- Ensures that raw materials are procured only from approved suppliers listed on the ASL.

### 3.3 Quality Control (QC)
- Develops and validates analytical methods for raw material testing.
- Performs incoming raw material testing according to established specifications.
- Reviews and approves Certificates of Analysis (CoAs) from suppliers.
- Manages retention samples of raw materials.
- Investigates out-of-specification (OOS) results for raw materials.

### 3.4 Manufacturing/Production
- Ensures proper handling, storage, and use of raw materials in accordance with manufacturing instructions.
- Provides feedback on raw material quality and performance during production.

### 3.5 Warehouse/Receiving
- Receives and quarantines incoming raw materials.
- Ensures proper identification, labeling, and storage conditions for raw materials.
- Collects samples for QC testing as per established procedures.
- Maintains accurate inventory records of raw materials.

### 3.6 Research and Development (R&D)
- Provides initial raw material specifications for new products.
- Collaborates with QA and QC during the qualification of new raw materials and suppliers.

### 3.7 All Personnel
- Adhere to the procedures outlined in this SOP.
- Report any deviations or concerns regarding raw materials or suppliers to their immediate supervisor and QA.



## 4.0 Detailed Procedures

### 4.1 Supplier Selection and Initial Assessment

#### 4.1.1 Identification of Potential Suppliers
Procurement, in conjunction with R&D and QA, identifies potential raw material suppliers based on:
- Industry reputation and track record.
- Recommendations from industry associations or established networks.
- Preliminary assessment of their quality management systems (e.g., ISO certifications, cGMP compliance).
- Financial stability and capacity to meet supply demands.

#### 4.1.2 Initial Supplier Questionnaire
Procurement issues a comprehensive Supplier Questionnaire to potential suppliers. This questionnaire gathers essential information regarding:
- Company profile, history, and organizational structure.
- Quality management system (QMS) documentation (e.g., quality manual, standard operating procedures).
- Manufacturing processes and capabilities relevant to the raw material.
- Raw material specifications, Certificates of Analysis (CoAs), and stability data.
- Regulatory compliance history and any past warning letters or recalls.
- Environmental, health, and safety (EHS) policies.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plans.

### 4.2 Supplier Qualification Process

#### 4.2.1 Risk Assessment of Raw Materials and Suppliers
QA, in collaboration with Procurement and R&D, conducts a risk assessment for each raw material and its potential suppliers. The risk assessment considers:
- **Criticality of the Raw Material:** Impact on product quality, safety, and efficacy (e.g., API vs. excipient vs. packaging material).
- **Supplier's Quality History:** Past performance, audit results, and regulatory compliance.
- **Complexity of Manufacturing Process:** The supplier's ability to consistently produce the material to required specifications.
- **Supply Chain Vulnerability:** Single source vs. multiple sources, geographical location, and political stability.
- **Storage and Handling Requirements:** Specific conditions needed to maintain material integrity.

The risk assessment categorizes suppliers (e.g., High, Medium, Low risk) which dictates the intensity of the qualification process.

#### 4.2.2 On-Site Audits (for High and Medium-Risk Suppliers)
For high and medium-risk suppliers, QA schedules and conducts on-site audits. The audit team, typically led by QA, may include representatives from Procurement, QC, and R&D. The audit focuses on:
- **Quality Management System:** Review of documentation, procedures, and records related to quality control, change control, deviations, and CAPAs.
- **Manufacturing Facilities:** Assessment of facilities, equipment, and environmental controls to ensure suitability for pharmaceutical raw material production.
- **Production Processes:** Verification of adherence to established manufacturing processes, in-process controls, and batch record review.
- **Laboratory Controls:** Evaluation of QC laboratory facilities, equipment calibration, analytical methods, and data integrity.
- **Warehousing and Distribution:** Assessment of storage conditions, material handling, and shipping practices.
- **Personnel Training and Qualification:** Review of training programs and personnel competency.

An audit report is generated, detailing observations, deficiencies, and recommendations. The supplier is required to provide a Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) plan for all identified deficiencies within an agreed timeframe.

#### 4.2.3 Documentation Review and Approval
For all potential suppliers, QA reviews all submitted documentation, including the Supplier Questionnaire, audit reports (if applicable), CoAs, material specifications, and any other relevant quality documents. This review ensures that the supplier's quality systems and material quality meet [Company Name]'s requirements and regulatory standards.

#### 4.2.4 Raw Material Specification Establishment
For each raw material, a detailed Raw Material Specification is established by R&D and QC, and approved by QA. This specification includes:
- **Material Name and Code:** Unique identification.
- **Description:** Physical characteristics.
- **Tests and Acceptance Criteria:** Chemical, physical, and microbiological tests with defined limits.
- **Analytical Methods:** Reference to validated test methods.
- **Storage Conditions:** Recommended storage temperature, humidity, and light protection.
- **Re-test/Expiry Date:** Based on stability data.

#### 4.2.5 Sample Evaluation and Testing
Upon receipt of samples from potential suppliers, QC performs full analytical testing according to the established Raw Material Specification. This testing verifies the supplier's ability to consistently provide material meeting the required quality attributes.

#### 4.2.6 Final Approval and Addition to Approved Supplier List (ASL)
Based on the satisfactory completion of all qualification steps (questionnaire review, risk assessment, audit results, documentation review, and sample testing), QA grants final approval to the supplier. The approved supplier and the raw materials they supply are then added to the official Approved Supplier List (ASL), maintained by QA. Materials may only be procured from suppliers listed on the ASL.

### 4.3 Raw Material Receipt, Sampling, and Testing

#### 4.3.1 Receipt and Quarantine
Upon arrival at the receiving dock, all raw materials are visually inspected for damage, proper labeling, and integrity of packaging. Materials are immediately assigned a unique Lot Number and moved to a designated 


quarantine area. Access to the quarantine area is restricted to authorized personnel only.

#### 4.3.2 Sampling
QC personnel, following aseptic techniques and using appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), collect samples of raw materials from each received lot in the quarantine area. Sampling plans are based on the raw material's criticality, historical quality data, and regulatory requirements (e.g., 100% sampling for APIs, square root of N + 1 for excipients). Samples are properly labeled and transported to the QC laboratory for testing.

#### 4.3.3 Laboratory Testing
QC performs all required tests on the collected raw material samples as per the established Raw Material Specification and validated analytical methods. Tests may include, but are not limited to, identification, assay, purity, impurities, microbial limits, heavy metals, and physical characteristics (e.g., appearance, pH, solubility).

#### 4.3.4 Release or Rejection
Upon completion of all required tests, QC reviews the test results against the Raw Material Specification. If all results meet the acceptance criteria, QC releases the raw material, and it is moved from quarantine to approved storage. If any test result is out-of-specification (OOS) or a deviation occurs, the raw material lot is rejected, clearly labeled as 'Rejected,' and segregated to prevent accidental use. A deviation investigation is initiated as per SOP-DEV-XXX (Deviation Management).

### 4.4 Supplier Performance Monitoring and Re-qualification

#### 4.4.1 Ongoing Performance Monitoring
QA continuously monitors the performance of approved suppliers. This includes:
- **Review of Certificates of Analysis (CoAs):** Regular review of CoAs for consistency and compliance with specifications.
- **Incoming Material Quality Trends:** Analysis of QC test results for trends, deviations, and non-conformances.
- **Supplier Non-Conformances:** Tracking and trending of all non-conformances related to supplier-provided materials, including investigations and CAPAs.
- **Customer Complaints/Product Issues:** Investigation of any product quality issues linked to raw material quality.
- **Delivery Performance:** On-time delivery and completeness of shipments.

#### 4.4.2 Supplier Re-qualification
Approved suppliers are re-qualified at a defined frequency based on their risk assessment (e.g., high-risk suppliers annually, medium-risk every two years, low-risk every three years). Re-qualification may involve:
- **Updated Supplier Questionnaire:** Requesting updated information from the supplier.
- **Documentation Review:** Review of updated quality manuals, certifications, and regulatory compliance documents.
- **Performance Review:** Comprehensive review of the supplier's performance over the re-qualification period.
- **Re-audit:** On-site audit if significant changes have occurred, or if performance issues warrant it.

#### 4.4.3 Management of Supplier Non-Conformance
Any non-conformance related to a qualified supplier or raw material (e.g., OOS results, delivery issues, audit findings) is documented and investigated. QA works with the supplier to implement appropriate Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPAs). Repeated non-conformances may lead to a supplier being downgraded in status or removed from the ASL.

### 4.5 Approved Supplier List (ASL) Management

#### 4.5.1 Maintenance of the ASL
QA is responsible for maintaining the official Approved Supplier List (ASL). The ASL includes:
- Supplier Name and Contact Information.
- Raw Materials Supplied.
- Qualification Status and Date.
- Re-qualification Due Date.
- Risk Category.

#### 4.5.2 Updates to the ASL
Any changes to a supplier's status (e.g., new approval, re-qualification, downgrade, removal) must be formally documented and updated on the ASL. Procurement is notified of all changes to ensure that materials are only ordered from currently approved suppliers.

## 5.0 Documentation Requirements
All activities related to supplier qualification and management must be thoroughly documented and maintained. These documents serve as objective evidence of compliance with regulatory requirements and internal quality standards. Required documentation includes, but is not limited to:
- **Supplier Qualification Files:** Comprehensive files for each supplier containing:
    - Supplier Questionnaires and responses.
    - Audit reports (including pre-audit documentation, audit checklists, findings, and CAPA responses).
    - Supplier approval letters.
    - Raw Material Specifications.
    - Certificates of Analysis (CoAs) for each received lot.
    - Supplier performance review records.
    - Change notifications from suppliers.
- **Approved Supplier List (ASL):** Current and historical versions.
- **Raw Material Receiving Records:** Documentation of receipt, quarantine, and release status.
- **Raw Material Testing Records:** All QC laboratory testing data, including raw data, chromatograms, and final reports.
- **Deviation Reports:** For any non-conformances or OOS results related to raw materials or suppliers.
- **Change Control Records:** For any changes to this SOP, raw material specifications, or supplier status.
- **Training Records:** Documentation of all personnel trained on this SOP.

All records must be legible, contemporaneous, original (or true copies), and accurate. Records shall be retained for a minimum of [e.g., 7 years or as per regulatory requirements] in a secure and easily retrievable manner.

## 6.0 Training Requirements
All personnel whose responsibilities are defined in Section 3.0 of this SOP, or who are directly involved in the supplier qualification, raw material receipt, sampling, testing, or management processes, must be trained on this SOP prior to performing their assigned tasks. Training shall include:
- Review of the SOP content.
- Understanding of their specific roles and responsibilities.
- Practical demonstration or hands-on training where applicable (e.g., sampling techniques).
- Assessment of understanding (e.g., written test, practical demonstration).

Refresher training shall be conducted periodically (e.g., every two years) or when significant revisions are made to this SOP, or when new regulatory requirements come into effect. Training records must be maintained by the Human Resources or Training Department.

## 7.0 Quality Control Measures
In addition to the testing outlined in Section 4.3, the following quality control measures are implemented to ensure the quality of raw materials:
- **Reference Standards:** Use of qualified reference standards for all analytical testing.
- **Equipment Calibration and Maintenance:** Regular calibration, maintenance, and qualification of all laboratory and manufacturing equipment used in raw material handling and testing.
- **Environmental Monitoring:** Monitoring of storage areas for temperature, humidity, and light to ensure optimal conditions for raw material stability.
- **Stability Studies:** Conducting stability studies on raw materials where appropriate to establish re-test or expiry dates.
- **Validation of Analytical Methods:** All analytical methods used for raw material testing must be validated according to ICH guidelines and regulatory expectations.
- **Inter-laboratory Comparisons:** Participation in inter-laboratory comparison programs or proficiency testing for critical raw material tests.

## 8.0 Safety Considerations
Personnel handling raw materials must be aware of and adhere to all applicable safety regulations and company safety policies. Specific safety considerations include:
- **Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS/SDS):** All raw materials must have current MSDS/SDS available, and personnel must be trained on the hazards and safe handling procedures for each material.
- **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Appropriate PPE (e.g., gloves, safety glasses, lab coats, respirators) must be worn as required during raw material receipt, sampling, testing, and handling.
- **Hazard Communication:** All raw material containers must be properly labeled with hazard warnings.
- **Spill Procedures:** Established procedures for handling spills of hazardous raw materials, including access to spill kits.
- **Emergency Procedures:** Personnel must be trained on emergency procedures, including eyewash stations, safety showers, and emergency exits.
- **Storage Segregation:** Hazardous raw materials must be stored separately from non-hazardous materials and in designated, well-ventilated areas.

This SOP is developed in accordance with, and references, the following key regulatory guidelines and principles:

### 9.1 U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

    - **Subpart B - Organization and Personnel:** Emphasizes responsibilities and training.
    - **Subpart E - Control of Components and Drug Product Containers and Closures:** Specifically addresses receipt, identification, storage, handling, sampling, testing, and approval/rejection of components (raw materials).
    - **Subpart I - Laboratory Controls:** Pertains to testing and release of components.

### 9.2 U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)
While FDA primarily regulates veterinary drugs, USDA regulations may be relevant for certain aspects, particularly if the raw materials or final products involve animal-derived components or are subject to USDA inspection for other reasons (e.g., biologics, food safety aspects in animal feed components).

### 9.3 Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP)
This SOP adheres to the overarching principles of cGMP, which ensure that products are consistently produced and controlled according to quality standards. Key cGMP principles applied include:
- **Quality Management System:** Establishment of a robust QMS.
- **Qualified Personnel:** Ensuring personnel are trained and competent.
- **Qualified Equipment:** Use of calibrated and maintained equipment.
- **Qualified Materials:** Sourcing and controlling raw materials from approved suppliers.
- **Validated Processes:** Ensuring processes are consistent and reproducible.
- **Documentation:** Comprehensive and accurate record-keeping.
- **Change Control:** Managing changes in a controlled manner.

## 10.0 Change Control Procedures
Any proposed changes to this SOP, or to the qualification status of a supplier, or to the specifications of a raw material, must be managed through a formal change control system. The change control process ensures that all changes are evaluated for their potential impact on product quality, safety, efficacy, and regulatory compliance before implementation. The procedure for change control is as follows:

1.  **Initiation of Change Request:** Any department or individual identifying a need for change submits a formal Change Request form.
2.  **Evaluation of Impact:** QA, in conjunction with relevant departments (e.g., Procurement, QC, R&D, Manufacturing), evaluates the potential impact of the proposed change. This includes assessing the impact on raw material quality, product quality, stability, regulatory filings, and other relevant SOPs.
3.  **Risk Assessment:** A risk assessment is conducted for the proposed change to identify and mitigate any potential risks.
4.  **Approval of Change:** The Change Request is reviewed and approved by all affected departments and QA management. For significant changes, regulatory approval may be required.
5.  **Implementation of Change:** Once approved, the change is implemented. This may involve updating documents (e.g., this SOP, Raw Material Specifications), re-qualifying suppliers, or re-validating analytical methods.
6.  **Training:** All affected personnel are trained on the implemented change.
7.  **Effectiveness Check:** An effectiveness check is performed after implementation to confirm that the change achieved its intended purpose and did not introduce any unforeseen negative impacts.
